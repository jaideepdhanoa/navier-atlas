# Public facts: Yamaha Motor

**As of 17 September 2026.** This brief uses only the cited public sources. Yamaha Motor Co., Ltd. is distinct from Yamaha Corporation; references here are to Yamaha Motor and its identified subsidiaries, brands and venture arm. [C01](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/ir/library/report/pdf/2025/marine_en_01.pdf) [C02](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/news/2025/0212/mtp.html)

Marine is a Yamaha Motor core business and financially material. The 2025 Marine plan reports FY2024 consolidated revenue of ¥2,576.2bn and operating income of ¥181.6bn, showing Marine at 21% of revenue and 48% of operating income. [C01](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/ir/library/report/pdf/2025/marine_en_01.pdf) Yamaha’s 2025–2027 Marine plan describes an integrated-boat direction, connected products, automated boat control, electric outboards and additional efficiency pathways. It also calls for external collaboration and reports R&D expense at 1.7× and capital investment at 1.8× the previous plan. These are company plans, not proof of any selected partner, launch, or third-party commitment. [C01](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/ir/library/report/pdf/2025/marine_en_01.pdf) [T08](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/ir/library/report/pdf/2025/marine_en_01.pdf)

The results releases report FY2025 Marine revenue of ¥527.6bn and operating income of ¥53.6bn, followed by first-half FY2026 revenue of ¥300.7bn and operating profit of ¥46.0bn. Yamaha cites demand, tariffs, cost and regional factors in that reporting. [C03](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/news/2026/0213/result.html) [C04](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/news/2026/0804/result.html) These figures provide public business context but do not disclose a Marine-specific budget or any third-party allocation.


Yamaha acquired Torqeedo in 2024. The official materials describe Torqeedo electric and hybrid drives, batteries, accessories, patents, series-production facilities and R&D, with integrated systems described in the 0.5–100 kW range. [T01](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/news/2024/0119/torqeedo.html) [T02](https://www.torqeedo.com/en/news-and-press/press-releases/blog-2024-4-3.html) The current Deep Blue Hybrid page describes 25–100 kW continuous electric motor power at 360 V, 360-V batteries, generator-to-DC-bus integration, CAN/Ethernet connections and electric/power/hybrid modes, with ferries and water taxis among typical applications. [G05](https://www.torqeedo.com/us/en-us/products/hybrid-drives) These are published capabilities; they do not establish a third-party-compatible configuration, drop-in fit, certification, supply commitment, or warranty terms.

Yamaha’s published marine footprint includes outboards from 2–450 hp, production at Fukuroi South and Kumamoto in Japan, and additional outboard production bases in Thailand and Brazil. Its boat-production pages identify Yamaha Marine Hokkaido Manufacturing for fishing/utility boats and Yamaha Amakusa Manufacturing for medium/small and utility boats, alongside group brands Skeeter and G3. [T06](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/about/business/marine-engines/) [T09](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/ir/library/report/pdf/2024/spv_en.pdf) [T10](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/about/business/boats/) [R01](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/about/business/boats/) The public sources do not establish spare capacity, third-party vessel manufacturing willingness, or suitability for a particular vessel.

Helm Master EX publicly lists Yamaha DEC/DES integration, joystick, Course/Heading Hold, Track Point, SetPoint, bow-thruster integration and wireless control for compatible Yamaha outboards. [T05](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/business/outboards/products/accessories/hm-ex/) [G10](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/business/outboards/products/accessories/hm-ex/) A 2024 investor presentation recorded a GPS-guided autodocking target, while the current reviewed product pages document assisted control features rather than general autonomous navigation or an open third-party interface. [G09](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/ir/library/report/pdf/2024/spv_en.pdf) [G10](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/business/outboards/products/accessories/hm-ex/) [G11](https://yamahaoutboards.com/owner-center/videos/helm-master-ex/autopilot-helm-master-ex-2024) No third-party flight-control compatibility is established.

Yamaha’s connected activity includes Siren Marine monitoring of boat systems and the Skipperi relationship. Public materials describe Yamaha investment, later Yamaha Motor Connected Innovation in Finland and acquisition of Skipperi fleet-software/IP assets. A 2026 Yamaha announcement describes bookings, payments, training, contracts, access, fuel billing, geofencing and fleet data functions. [T12](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/news/2021/1216/corporate.html) [T13](https://yamahaoutboards.com/newsroom/company-news/yamaha-to-purchase-siren-marine) [G01](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/news/2023/0207/marine.html) [G02](https://www.yamaha-motor.eu/is/en/news/2025/yamaha-motor-boosts-its-marine-digitalisation-and-connectivity-w/) [G04](https://yamahaoutboards.com/newsroom/company-news/yamaha-marine-connected-boat-division-announces-expanded-business-and-product-offerings-with-skipper) This demonstrates fleet and connected-product activity—not passenger-route concessions, autonomous-vessel capability, charging infrastructure, or a third-party integration.

Yamaha’s official outboard directory lists marine distributors across Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam, Taiwan, China, Hong Kong and South Korea. [R02](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/business/outboards/distributors/) Separate country group-company listings do not prove marine operations. [R03](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/about/group/asia_oceania/) Distributor presence is not proof of boatbuilding, operator status, passenger permits, route rights, charging readiness or spare capacity. Singapore’s government policy is a demand-side regulatory signal for new harbour craft from 2030, not evidence of Yamaha participation. [R13](https://www.mot.gov.sg/what-we-do/green-transport/maritime-environment-responsibility/)

Yamaha Motor Ventures is a parent-backed CVC channel. Yamaha’s official release describes a US$100m, 10-year Exploratory Fund II, while another describes a sustainability fund; current official team pages identify venture leadership. [C08](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/news/2023/0313/corporate.html) [C09](https://global.yamaha-motor.com/news/2022/0621/corporate.html) [C10](https://www.yamahamotor.vc/team) [C11](https://www.yamahamotor.vc/team/keita-nakanishi) [C12](https://www.yamahamotor.vc/anish-patel) [C13](https://www.yamahamotor.vc/team/daniel-barth-) Fund size is not a parent operating budget, individual ticket indication, Marine approval, or willingness to invest in a particular company.
