# Woods Hole, Martha’s Vineyard and Nantucket Steamship Authority (SSA) authority-deck research

**Research date:** 2026-07-29  
**Method:** Primary/authoritative public sources first. A `null — not found` entry is a research result, not a zero or an inference. Operational claims are kept distinct from plans, studies, and an operator’s own statements. Figures from the SSA’s annual report are Authority-reported; the report includes independent audited financial statements, but traffic figures should not be described as independently audited unless the relevant audit expressly says so.

## 1. SSA NETWORK TODAY

- **Statutory mainland/island network and current terminals:** SSA identifies terminals in Woods Hole and Hyannis, Cape Cod; Vineyard Haven and Oak Bluffs, Martha’s Vineyard; and Nantucket. Its enabling purpose is adequate transportation between the mainland ports of Woods Hole/Hyannis and Martha’s Vineyard/Nantucket. [S1] [S2]
- **Routes operated:** SSA’s published schedule system covers the Woods Hole–Martha’s Vineyard and Hyannis–Nantucket services. On Martha’s Vineyard, the Authority identifies terminals at Vineyard Haven and Oak Bluffs; service pattern by terminal is schedule/season dependent. It is therefore accurate to describe the network as Woods Hole↔Martha’s Vineyard (Vineyard Haven/Oak Bluffs) and Hyannis↔Nantucket, rather than to imply that every landing operates on every sailing. [S1] [S3]
- **Fleet:** SSA’s current history page says it operates **ten vessels**, including one high-speed passenger-only ferry. Its vessel roster is M/V Aquinnah, Barnstable, Eagle, Governor, Island Home, Iyanough, Martha’s Vineyard, Monomoy, Nantucket, Sankaty, and Woods Hole—**eleven named vessels** on the current page. This is an internal source discrepancy; do not state a fleet count without resolving it with SSA. The list indicates that the “ten” figure is likely out of date following M/V Monomoy’s addition, but that is an inference and is not used here. [S1] [S4]
- **Recent fleet modernization:** The Authority purchased three former offshore supply vessels in 2022—Barnstable, Aquinnah and Monomoy—and converted them for ferry use. The 2025 annual report says Barnstable entered service in 2024, Aquinnah was commissioned May 16, 2025, and Monomoy was due to depart Alabama for the Authority early in 2026. The fleet page confirms each sister vessel’s conversion and states that their purchase/conversion was aided by **$28.1m** in federal funding through an agreement with the Cape Cod Regional Transit Authority. [S4] [S5]
- **Annual traffic, most recent source:** In calendar 2025, SSA reports **24,074 trips; 2,958,355 passengers; 575,527 automobiles and trucks 20 feet or less; and 98,915 trucks longer than 20 feet**—**674,442 total vehicles** when the two reported vehicle classes are added. These are SSA-reported annual-report figures; the 2025 report includes independently audited financial statements, but the report does not label these traffic counts as audited. [S5]
- **Freight/public-service character:** SSA describes itself as the only ferry service to both islands carrying passengers, vehicles, and commercial freight trucks. It says island residents depend on ferry service for commerce and mainland transportation. This is stronger and more precise than claiming no other passenger ferry exists: private passenger operators are licensed and operate on related corridors. [S1]
- **Lifeline mandate:** Chapter 701’s stated purpose is “adequate transportation of persons and necessaries of life for the Islands of Nantucket and Martha’s Vineyard.” SSA calls this its statutory “Lifeline to the Islands” mission. The mandate is for adequate transportation; `null — not found` for statutory language making SSA the **sole** carrier of every form of island passenger service. [S1] [S2]

## 2. GOVERNANCE & MANDATE

- **Enabling law:** The Authority was created by **Chapter 701 of the Acts of 1960**, effective in 1961. The 2025 annual report was submitted pursuant to §13 of that Act as amended by Chapter 276 of the Acts of 1962. [S1] [S5]
- **Board structure:** SSA is governed by five members: Nantucket (appointed by Nantucket County Commissioners), Martha’s Vineyard/Dukes County (Dukes County Commissioners), Falmouth (Falmouth Select Board), Barnstable (Barnstable Town Council), and New Bedford (Mayor, with City Council approval). Island representatives have **35% each** of the combined vote and mainland representatives **10% each**; terms are three years. [S1]
- **Port Council:** In addition to the Board, SSA has a seven-member advisory Port Council representing Barnstable, Fairhaven, Falmouth, Nantucket, New Bedford, Oak Bluffs, and Tisbury. [S1]
- **Fare-setting and self-funding:** The Authority’s 2025 annual report reports **$149.1m** operating revenue and **$146.0m** operating expenses, a **$9.53m** net operating gain after fixed-charge/license-fee/operating-grant effects, and describes 2025 as the **63rd consecutive year without a deficit assessment on taxpayers of Authority port communities**. SSA’s history page says its budget is supported by operating revenue and that it has not assessed participating-community taxpayers for monetary support since 1963. These statements support a self-funded/operating-revenue framing, but do **not** mean that SSA has never received government funding: the annual report identifies federal funding for the OSV program and operating grants. Do not state “no tax subsidy” as an unqualified fact. [S1] [S5]
- **Regulatory role / private operators:** SSA says its Act authorizes it to license and regulate mainland–island private freight carriage and USCG-rated-over-40-passenger vessels, subject to the stated grandfathering exception for services operating before May 1973. Its page lists licensed private passenger services, including New Bedford–Oak Bluffs (seasonal), New Bedford–Vineyard Haven (year-round), Hyannis–Oak Bluffs (seasonal), and Hyannis–Nantucket (seasonal and year-round). [S1]

## 3. DECARBONIZATION / MODERNIZATION

- **Alternative-propulsion evidence:** SSA commissioned Elliott Bay Design Group’s alternative-propulsion feasibility study, presented to the Board May 26, 2022. It compared mechanical diesel, three diesel-electric/battery configurations, and an all-electric Woods Hole–Martha’s Vineyard concept with rapid charging at both ends. SSA’s published summary says the all-electric option was closest to zero wake-side emissions and most expensive; hybrid cases reduced CO2 **7%–8%** relative to diesel mechanical under the study’s load conditions. All-electric Hyannis–Nantucket was not considered because battery quantity would likely be prohibitive. [S6]
- **Near-term position is not a fleet commitment:** SSA says its route profile supports the premise that a hybrid vessel is the most likely near-term solution and that it submitted a MARAD grant application to study design/engineering/conversion of five vessel classes, including the three former OSVs. This is a study/grant-application position—not a procurement award, conversion authorization, shore-power commitment, or deadline to operate an electric ferry. [S6]
- **Fuel pilot:** In May 2025, the Board authorized an R99 renewable-diesel pilot on M/V *Island Home*, expected to run June 1–September 1 using **104,000 gallons**. SSA reported a wheel-to-wheel analysis projecting a minimum **50%** and up to **78%** lifecycle-GHG reduction depending on feedstock. This is an Authority-described fuel-pilot estimate, not measured operational emissions results. [S5]
- **Shore-side energy:** SSA’s Thomas B. Landers Road project is a **5,900 kW solar / 6,100 kWh battery-storage** development; the 18.5-acre lot has roughly **1,900-vehicle** capacity. For the Woods Hole terminal reconstruction, SSA says it is pursuing photovoltaic arrays and geothermal heating/cooling, and its Board affirmed net-zero-energy support for the landside phase in 2021. These are terminal/parking energy measures, not vessel charging infrastructure. [S6]
- **Woods Hole terminal status:** The Authority’s 2025 annual report calls Phase 6 (terminal building, geothermal wells, site works, freight-shed demolition, utility building, employee-parking photovoltaic canopy) **in progress**; Phases 7–8 timing was undetermined. A June 12, 2026 SSA update said the terminal building certificate of occupancy was expected that Friday and listed commissioning/punch-list work; it also specifies no on-site passenger parking and off-site shuttle transport. [S5] [S7]
- **Capital program:** The 2025 report says SSA disbursed **$16.78m** from its Capital Improvement Fund toward Woods Hole reconstruction and **$36.61m** from the Replacement Fund across capital projects, including **$27.46m** toward OSV acquisition/conversion/reactivation and **$1.60m** for two electric-hybrid transit buses. [S5]
- **Key commitment null:** `null — not found` for a published SSA Board-approved procurement to acquire an all-electric or hybrid-electric ferry, a vessel-electrification completion date, a terminal shore-charging installation, or a quantified fleetwide vessel-emissions target in the sources reviewed. The propulsion study must not be presented as an electric-ferry commitment. [S6]

## 4. CONGESTION / PUBLIC-VALUE ARTIFACT

- **Woods Hole pressure, official local framing:** Falmouth’s transportation-planning material says SSA operates the Town’s largest public parking facilities and has added congestion in Woods Hole Village. The Authority itself directs walk-on passengers to off-site lots and shuttle buses because there is no on-site parking at the Woods Hole terminal during reconstruction. [S8] [S7]
- **Traffic-management evidence, not a congestion model:** SSA’s 2025 report lists construction-period measures including pedestrian/vehicle separation, crosswalks, pickup/drop-off designation, bus controls, back-booth use, wayfinding, police details, no-parking messaging, and a planned 2026 off-site standby-staging trial. The report says two candidate staging lots could accommodate a large number of vehicles and trucks, but estimates **eight employees** and **$800,000** of hires would be required for operation. These are operational measures; they do not quantify road-delay reduction. [S5]
- **Demand and peaking anchor:** During 2025 summer reservation opening, SSA processed **21,466 transactions** and nearly **$6.9m** in revenue for the Woods Hole–Martha’s Vineyard opening day; peak processing was **722 transactions/minute**. This is a reservation-opening measure, not a count of completed ferry trips, vehicles, or visitors. [S5]
- **Freight matters:** SSA’s 2025 traffic total includes **98,915 trucks over 20 feet**. Passenger-only fast ferries should therefore not be positioned as substituting for SSA’s lifeline freight/vehicle function. [S5]
- **Road-specific nulls:** `null — not found` for a current authoritative, route-segment-specific measure of Falmouth/Cape road congestion attributable to ferry traffic, and for a primary-source model quantifying vehicle, emissions, or delay relief from a particular SSA route, service change, or electrification project. Do not infer such benefits from village-pressure statements. [S8]

## 5. NEW BEDFORD CONTEXT

- **Governance connection:** New Bedford has one of the five SSA Board seats, appointed by the mayor with City Council approval. It also has a Port Council seat. [S1]
- **Private passenger connection:** SSA’s current history page lists the licensed New Bedford–Oak Bluffs passenger service as seasonal and New Bedford–Vineyard Haven as year-round. Seastreak’s own current New Bedford page advertises direct passenger service to both Oak Bluffs and Vineyard Haven; it states its vessels are passenger-only and cannot carry engine-powered vehicles. [S1] [S9]
- **SSA operational geography:** SSA’s own terminals are not in New Bedford; its listed ferry terminals are Woods Hole, Hyannis, Vineyard Haven, Oak Bluffs, and Nantucket. It does maintain a vessel-maintenance facility in nearby Fairhaven. Do not characterize New Bedford as an SSA departure terminal. [S1]
- **Port shore-power artifact:** New Bedford Port Authority advertised bids for its **North Terminal Shore Power** project on July 17, 2024 (IFB NBPA-FY24-006). The bid notice is evidence of a port shore-power project solicitation, not evidence that it was awarded, built, energized, available to ferry operators, compatible with a specified SSA vessel, or connected to a New Bedford–island ferry proposal. [S10]
- **Offshore-wind port context:** The Port Authority says the Commonwealth completed the **29-acre** Marine Commerce Terminal in 2015 for offshore-wind turbine construction/assembly/deployment; it also describes Vineyard Wind’s 800 MW winning bid and an 18-month terminal lease announced in 2018. This establishes industrial-port/offshore-wind context only; it is not a shore-power claim. [S11]
- **Freight-service null:** `null — not found` for a primary-source SSA/MassDOT/port study, pilot, or adopted plan for a **freight ferry from New Bedford** to Martha’s Vineyard or Nantucket in the sources reviewed. Also `null — not found` for evidence that SSA’s lifeline freight operation is planned to relocate from Woods Hole/Hyannis to New Bedford. [S1] [S9]

## 6. FAST-FERRY & PRIVATE OPERATORS

- **Hy-Line, Hyannis–Nantucket:** Hy-Line’s own page describes year-round, passenger-only high-speed service from Hyannis to Nantucket, with a one-hour crossing and up to nine summer departures. Its published standard adult one-way fare is **$52**, inclusive of a stated $2 one-way fuel surcharge; fares are subject to change. [S12]
- **Hy-Line, Hyannis–Oak Bluffs:** SSA lists a licensed seasonal Hyannis–Oak Bluffs service. `null — not found` in the primary operator pages reviewed for a current, stable published Hy-Line adult one-way fare that can be cited here without relying on a third-party fare aggregator. [S1]
- **Seastreak, New Bedford–Martha’s Vineyard:** Seastreak advertises direct passenger service from New Bedford to Oak Bluffs and Vineyard Haven. Its page shows New Bedford–Oak Bluffs sailing times of roughly **50–55 minutes**, depending on schedule period, and a standard adult one-way fare of **$49**, plus a disclosed 3% booking fee and temporary approximately 5% fuel surcharge at purchase; its page separately flags a seasonal $5 surge fee on specified departures. State the base fare and add-ons separately. [S9]
- **Published private ridership:** `null — not found` for a recent annual ridership total published by Hy-Line or Seastreak for the cited Nantucket/Martha’s Vineyard corridors. Do not substitute SSA traffic figures for private-operator ridership. [S9] [S12]
- **Boundary for a proposal:** These private services provide passenger competition/choice, while SSA’s statutory public-service role includes vehicles and commercial freight. The facts do not support an assertion that private fast ferries replace SSA’s lifeline carriage of necessaries of life. [S1] [S2]
