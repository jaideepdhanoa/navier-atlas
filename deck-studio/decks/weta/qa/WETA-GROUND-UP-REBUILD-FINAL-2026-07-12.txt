 THE BAY NEEDS MORE FERRY
 SERVICE
 Not every trip needs the same ferry
 THE CONTEXT

 WETA is electrifying its fleet and
 advancing service in Mission Bay,
 Treasure Island, Berkeley and Redwood
 City. Large ferries are essential where
 demand is high.

 THE PROBLEM

 Emerging markets can be expensive to
 test when each new trip begins with large
 vessels, major terminal work and
 full-scale operating commitments. Some
 corridors need capacity now. Others
 need a smaller first step.

 A PLATFORM, NOT A ONE-BOAT
 ANSWER
 The N30 is the starting point: a current vessel for validating
 the electric hydrofoil stack, Bay operating data and
 passenger experience. The service layer can grow only when
 evidence supports it.



  NEAR TERM
  Multiple smaller vessels can add frequency and
  flexibility in measured increments where demand and
  public-service requirements support them.




  OVER TIME
  Larger-capacity foiling platforms and autonomy can
  deepen the economics after certification, service design
  and operating proof. Neither is required to begin.




Sources: Navier N30 platform · Navier company and autonomy materials
 ONE NETWORK
 Three practical uses for right-sized vessels


 STRENGTHEN EXISTING SERVICE
 Add targeted frequency, shoulder-period trips or a smaller capacity increment where WETA identifies a real rider need.


 LAUNCH PLANNED EXPANSION
 Give an emerging market a practical electric starting point while WETA learns demand and operating needs.


 OPEN OVERLOOKED LINKS
 Test a waterfront-to-waterfront trip where road travel is indirect and large-ferry demand is not yet proven.


 START SMALL
 Begin with one bounded public problem and a clear operating hypothesis.


 GROW WITH DATA
 Add frequency, vessels or capacity only after real Bay performance is known.


 WETA DECIDES
 WETA retains network, safety, procurement and public-service authority.


Source: WETA 2050 Service Vision and Expansion Policy
START WITH MARKETS WETA ALREADY KNOWS

MISSION BAY + TREASURE ISLAND · WETA’s REEF program identifies
new electric service to Downtown San Francisco. Screen Navier as a
feeder or added-frequency option.

REDWOOD CITY · WETA is advancing design and environmental
review for San Francisco and Oakland markets. Evaluate a smaller
service increment alongside the planned network.

ALAMEDA + OAKLAND · Navier is based in Alameda, inside WETA’s
operating geography. Local demonstration and service learning
can begin close to engineering and support.

These are screening areas, not route, terminal or procurement
commitments.

WETA-shaped Bay test.

OUTPUT · A shared evidence pack—not a route or
procurement commitment.

 WHEN THE DATA SUPPORTS IT
 Build a new electric service layer

 1 · Add frequency or vessels on a successful pilot.
 2 · Extend to another WETA-selected market.
 3 · Introduce larger-capacity foiling vessels when ready.
 4 · Add autonomy only after safety, certification and operating proof.

 Each step follows WETA approval, procurement, terminal, labor and community processes.

 SIX GATES. NO SHORTCUTS.

 SAFETY + CERTIFICATION · USCG pathway, operating envelope, redundancy and emergency procedures.

 ACCESSIBLE PUBLIC SERVICE · Boarding, seating, mobility devices, bikes, luggage and fare integration.

 TERMINAL + CHARGING · Berth access, shore power, turnaround, utilities and resilience.

 BAY OPERATING FIT · Weather, sea state, range reserve, wake, noise and navigation.

 CREW + MAINTENANCE · Training, staffing, spares, diagnostics, dispatch and local support.

 ECONOMICS + PUBLIC VALUE · Lifecycle cost, reliability, useful access, equity and rider outcomes.
