**Method:** Primary/authoritative public sources first. This extends (and does not repeat) the Boston Harbor + Cape census. ## 1. MBTA FERRY NETWORK TODAY

- **Current published network (snapshot, 2026-07-29):** MBTA lists year-round Charlestown (F4), Hingham (F1), Hingham/Hull (F2H), and Harbor Loop (F10); it lists East Boston, Lynn, Winthrop, and Quincy as seasonal. This is the authoritative published service classification, rather than a claim about a particular day’s operating pattern. [S1]
- **Logan/South Shore connectivity:** Massport describes year-round high-speed daily Hingham/Hull service to Long Wharf and Logan’s Water Transportation Dock, and calls Quincy and Winthrop seasonal Logan services. At the dock, Route 66 provides the free airport-terminal connection. [S2]
- **Harbor Loop expansion:** MBTA’s current schedule page identifies Harbor Loop as year-round; Massport describes its stops as Boston Logan, Lovejoy Wharf/North Station, Central Wharf, and Commonwealth Pier/Seaport. MBTA announced the new weekday service would begin June 29, 2026; it was celebrated as new service on July 14, 2026. [S1] [S2] [S3]
- **Lynn / East Boston pilots and subsequent service:** The current MBTA network page classifies both Lynn and East Boston as seasonal. The MBTA’s 2025 year-end report says contactless payments began on East Boston and Charlestown in summer 2025; it also says a newly acquired vessel would supplement Lynn and other routes. `null — not found` for a primary-source statement in the material reviewed that establishes the launch date and formal pilot end-date for each of the Lynn and East Boston services. [S1] [S4]
- **Winthrop and Quincy enhancement:** MBTA reported on January 5, 2026 that the 2025 season doubled weekday trips, created direct weekday Winthrop–Boston and Quincy–Boston service, and improved weekday trip times by about 30 minutes. [S4]
- **Operator under contract:** Boston Harbor City Cruises (BHCC), part of Hornblower Group, announced July 8, 2025 that MBTA selected it to continue as ferry operator. This is an operator announcement, not an MBTA procurement award page; it quotes MBTA Ferry Operations Director David Perry describing the partnership. `null — not found` for the MBTA contract award, term, and value in an accessible MBTA procurement document. [S5]
- **Annual system ridership headline:** BHCC reports it carried **more than 1.4 million passengers** on MBTA ferry services in calendar 2024, completing nearly 58,000 trips. This is the most recent annual system-wide figure located, but it is an operator-reported figure, **not** a verified NTD agency-mode total. [S5]
- **NTD check:** FTA’s NTD has released 2024 annual data and publishes a 2024 Agency Mode Service workbook. `null — not found` for a reproducible Ferry-mode MBTA unlinked-passenger-trips cell from the accessible web extract; do not equate the BHCC “passengers” figure above with NTD UPT without transcribing the NTD workbook. ## 2. MBTA DECARBONIZATION / MODERNIZATION DIRECTION

- **Commonwealth legal direction:** Massachusetts’ 2021 Climate Act requires a 2050 statewide emissions limit that achieves “at least net zero statewide greenhouse gas emissions”; the statute also establishes transport-sector sublimits and says the 2030 and 2040 statewide limits must be at least 50% and 75% below 1990, respectively. This is a statewide legal framework, not an MBTA ferry-specific mandate. [S7]
- **MBTA policy:** MBTA says it operates a low-carbon transit system and seeks to reduce its carbon footprint. Its published Climate Plans page says the Environmental Management & Sustainability Policy guides reductions in GHG and other air-quality impacts, and its Climate Assessment is a framework for decarbonization, resilience, and power planning. [S8] [S9]
- **Published fleet work is mode-specific:** MBTA’s “Greening the Fleet” page documents battery-electric and hydrogen bus pilots and considers a future transition of Commuter Rail from diesel to electrification. It does not set an electric-ferry goal, fleet-conversion date, charging target, or ferry-vessel procurement. [S10]
- **Ferry modernization, not an electrification commitment:** MBTA says its $9.6bn five-year capital plan includes upgrades to services for ferries as well as other modes. Its active MassDOT-led Water Transportation Study is planning the next iteration of an accessible network and can recommend routes, terminals, and facilities. These are planning/modernization facts; neither source commits MBTA to electric ferries. [S11] [S12]
- **Bottom line for deck language:** **No published MBTA commitment to procure or operate electric ferries was found in the MBTA sustainability, fleet-decarbonization, capital-plan, and water-study materials reviewed.** Do not represent the state Climate Act, the bus work, or the water study as such a commitment. `null — not found` for an MBTA ferry propulsion/electrification study, procurement, or quantified charging requirement. [S7] [S10] [S11]

## 3. MASSPORT MANDATE

- **Original verified Net Zero mandate:** On March 17, 2022 Massport announced a goal to be Net Zero by **2031**, tied to its 75th anniversary. Exact scope: the Roadmap “focuses on 100% of the greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions directly controlled by Massport-owned facilities, equipment, and purchased electricity,” while retaining influence in non-controlled areas. The release says the Authority expected to be Net Zero without offsets by 2040. [S13]
- **Important update / date reconciliation:** Massport’s current Roadmap page says it now aims to achieve Net Zero GHG emissions by **2028**, targeting all Massport-controlled sources; the page lists an item dated June 26, 2026 titled “Massport Accelerates Net Zero Plan by 3 Years.” The 2031 statement should therefore be treated as the 2022 commitment, not as Massport’s current target. [S14]
- **Current target scope:** Massport distinguishes controlled sources (including purchased electricity, central heating, Massport fleet/equipment, airport/maritime facilities) from influenced sources (including aircraft, passenger/employee transportation, cruise vessels, harbor craft, and tenant-controlled facilities). That distinction matters: a partner vessel is not automatically within the controlled-emissions target. [S14]
- **Logan water interface:** Massport says scheduled and on-demand water-transport partners serve Logan’s Water Transportation Dock; passengers transfer between the dock and airport terminals on the free Route 66 on-airport shuttle. Massport calls water transport a convenient way to reach Logan from points around Boston Harbor and the South Shore. This confirms an existing passenger-water interface, **not** berth rights, a concession, or shore-power availability for a new operator. [S2]
- **Latest annual airport-demand anchor:** Massport reports **43.2 million Logan passengers in calendar 2025**, with 10 million international passengers. [S15]
- **Water-transportation strategy statement:** Massport’s published Logan page says water transportation is offered from points around Boston Harbor and the South Shore, lists MBTA and on-demand partners, and directs passengers to the dock/shuttle interchange. `null — not found` for a Massport-published strategy or target that commits a specified number of additional water routes, passengers, berths, or water-vessel electrification. [S2]

## 4. CONGESTION / PUBLIC-VALUE ARTIFACT

- **Strongest Logan access hook:** Massport characterizes its 2025 sustainable-ground-transportation work as “More HOV Choices, Less Congestion, Fewer Emissions.” It reports a record **1.8 million** travelers used private scheduled buses to reach Logan in 2025, and says optimizing Back Bay Logan Express cut Logan travel time **30%**. These are Massport-reported ground-access outcomes—not a ferry diversion model. [S15]
- **Relevant airport scale:** Logan’s 43.2 million passengers in 2025 provides the defensible demand scale against which a water-access pilot can be framed. No conversion from airport passengers to water-ferry riders is made here. [S15]

## 5. STATE/REGIONAL WATER TRANSPORT PLANNING

- **Current Commonwealth study:** MassDOT and MBTA launched the Water Transportation Study on September 18, 2025. The stated 12-month study will identify improvements to existing MBTA routes/infrastructure, explore new routes and terminal locations, consider multimodal access, and identify customer-experience upgrades; it is described as called for in the 2022 Massachusetts Transportation Bond Bill. [S12]
- **Study geography and purpose:** MBTA says the study timeline is summer 2025–summer 2026 and that it will plan/design a fully accessible network meeting agency and ADA standards. Potential recommendations include routes, terminals, and facilities in Boston, Gloucester, Lynn, Quincy, Salem, and Winthrop. This is a live planning process—not an adopted service plan. [S11]
- **Public-value language suitable for careful quotation:** MassDOT Transportation Secretary Monica Tibbits-Nutt said at launch: “Ferries are more than an alternative to driving. They are a vital connection that strengthens ties between communities, reduces congestion, and supports sustainable travel.” This is a Secretary quote about the study, not a forecast or an authorization of any specific route/vessel. [S12]
- **Boston Harbor Now work:** Boston Harbor Now maintains a Water Transportation Study page with detailed route reports, including dock/vessel recommendations and projected ridership under specified fares/frequencies. It is a nonprofit planning source, not a Commonwealth-adopted plan; any projections must remain labeled as its scenario outputs, not as observed demand. [S17]

## 6. SALEM FERRY + PROVINCETOWN

- **Salem annual ridership gap closed:** Salem Harbor Port Authority’s approved May 1, 2025 minutes record BHCC’s annual report that Salem Ferry ridership was **81,525 people**, a **19% increase over 2023**, the service’s highest passenger total, with more than 28,000 passengers in October. The minutes do not explicitly label the season year, but because the presentation was the annual report/marketing plan for the upcoming 2025 opening, it is appropriately described as the **reported prior-season figure (most likely 2024; source does not expressly state the year)**. Do not relabel it as audited 2024 ridership. [S18]
- **Salem demand/operations context:** The same official minutes say the Hingham–Salem pilot carried more than 3,800 additional passengers and would be used again. This is not a substitute for Salem–Boston annual ridership and should not be added to the 81,525 figure without confirming counting methodology. [S18]
- **Provincetown fast-ferry annual ridership:** `null — not found` for a primary/operator/municipal source that publishes a recent annual passenger total for either the Bay State Cruise Company or Boston Harbor City Cruises Boston–Provincetown fast ferry. The Town’s official ferry page confirms daily scheduled 90-minute Bay State service between MacMillan Wharf and Boston’s World Trade Center, but provides no annual passenger count. [S19]
