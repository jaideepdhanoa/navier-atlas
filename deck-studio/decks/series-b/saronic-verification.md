## Evidence and interpretation

### Contracts / backlog
WorkBoat reports that the U.S. Navy awarded Saronic a **$392M production contract** for Corsair, with **nearly $200M obligated at the time of award** (8 Dec 2025). The report says the Navy moved from prototype to production in under 12 months. This is the strongest directly citable source for the $392M figure, but the wording matters: it is a single production contract / ceiling or total award, not independently verified “total contracts to date.”

### Fundraising
The disclosed funding arithmetic is:

- Series A: **$55M** (October 2023; led by Caffeinated Capital).
- Series B: **$175M** (July 2024; valuation stated as $1B; led by Andreessen Horowitz/a16z).
- Series C: **$600M** (18 February 2025; led by Elad Gil; valuation **$4B**).
- Series D: **$1.75B** (31 March 2026; led by Kleiner Perkins; valuation **$9.25B**).

These disclosed rounds sum to **$2.58B**. Because Saronic’s seed funding amount was not disclosed, the defensible phrasing is **“at least $2.58B in disclosed capital raised”**, rather than an exact lifetime total. CNBC independently confirms the Series D size and says it more than doubled valuation from the $4B level after the Series C.

## Headline vessel delivery / pipeline points

- Saronic’s February 2025 Series C announcement says the company developed and **delivered Corsair**, its largest ASV model at that point, during 2024.
- The March 2026 Series D announcement says Saronic unveiled its first **180-foot Marauder**, with the **first hull completed in under six months** and under eight months after acquiring the Louisiana facility.
- CNBC (31 Mar 2026) reports Saronic aims to build **more than 20 ships per year by 2027**; it describes a portfolio of six ASV iterations, from six-foot Spyglass to the 40-metric-ton Marauder. WorkBoat also reports the Louisiana expansion is intended to add 300,000+ sq. ft. and 1,500 jobs.

These are production/pipeline indicators, not a disclosed cumulative count of delivered vessels.
