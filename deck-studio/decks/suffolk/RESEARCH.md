# Suffolk County + NYMTC Authority Deck — primary-source research dossier
*Research cut: 29 July 2026. Scope discipline: a numeric, date-specific, or policy claim appears only when a primary source is cited below. “OMITTED” means it was not established from a usable primary source in this research pass; it is not an invitation to infer a value.*

## 1. WHO GOVERNS WHAT

### Suffolk County
- **County Executive — Ed Romaine (2024–present).** Suffolk County’s official County Executive page identifies Ed Romaine as County Executive; the County’s 2024 inauguration release says he was sworn in on 1 January 2024. This is the current named executive to use for a 2025–26 audience, subject to final pre-send verification. **Source (2024 / current page):** *Suffolk County Executive Ed Romaine* / *Ed Romaine Sworn In as Suffolk County Executive*, Suffolk County Government. https://www.suffolkcountyny.gov/Elected-Officials/County-Executive ; https://www.suffolkcountyny.gov/portals/0/press/2024/01-01-2024-Romaine-Sworn-In.pdf
- **County Legislature — 18 elected legislators / local law and budget authority.** The official Legislature describes an 18-member body elected from legislative districts; it adopts local laws and the County budget. Deck implication: county sponsorship, capital/operating direction, and public accountability have an Executive–Legislature dimension, not a single “transit authority” customer. **Source (current page):** *About the Suffolk County Legislature*, Suffolk County Legislature. https://www.scnylegislature.us/189/About-the-Legislature
- **Suffolk County Transit (SCT) — County bus operator.** SCT’s official site describes fixed-route bus service and paratransit/ADA transportation. It is the existing county mobility system to complement rather than displace; do not characterize it as a ferry operator. **Source (current page):** *Suffolk County Transit*, Suffolk County Department of Public Works. https://www.suffolkcountyny.gov/Departments/Public-Works/Transportation-and-Traffic/Suffolk-County-Transit
- **SCT service/ridership, subsidy, fleet, and service-area statistics: OMITTED.** No number is included until pulled from an SCT/County annual report, adopted budget, or NTD submission.

### NYMTC / NYSDOT
- **NYMTC is the federally designated MPO for New York City, Long Island, and the Lower Hudson Valley.** Its official description defines the geography as NYC’s five boroughs plus Suffolk, Nassau, Westchester, Putnam, and Rockland counties. **Source (current page):** *Moving Forward 2055—Introduction*, NYMTC. https://www.nymtc.org/movingforward2055/introduction/index.html
- **Federal role.** NYMTC “coordinates the federally required transportation planning process and access to federal funding” for the region’s transportation projects. Its RTP “guides Federal funding for transportation investment.” This is programming/planning leverage, **not** a representation that NYMTC independently operates a water service. **Source (2025 adoption / 2026–55 period):** *Moving Forward 2055—Introduction*, NYMTC. https://www.nymtc.org/movingforward2055/introduction/index.html
- **NYSDOT relationship.** NYSDOT is explicitly named by NYMTC as a regional planning participant alongside the MTA. NYMTC’s Nassau/Suffolk Transportation Coordinating Committee is the local coordinating forum. **Source (current page):** *Moving Forward 2055—Introduction* and *Who We Are*, NYMTC. https://www.nymtc.org/movingforward2055/introduction/index.html ; https://www.nymtc.org/ABOUT-US/who-we-are
- **Practical governance reading.** County government/SCT can frame a locally useful service and landside integration; NYMTC and NYSDOT matter for federally compliant planning, regional prioritization and funding pathways; waterfront municipality, terminal owner, Coast Guard/USACE and operator permissions remain separate decisions. The latter approvals/timetable: **OMITTED** pending site-specific primary documentation.

## 2. MANDATE / POLICY HOOKS

### NYMTC Regional Transportation Plan
- **Current plan:** *Moving Forward 2055: Connecting Communities, Creating Opportunities*, adopted **5 September 2025**, covering **2026–2055**. NYMTC calls it a federally required plan updated every four years. **Source (2025):** *Moving Forward 2055—Introduction*; *Final Plan Executive Summary*, NYMTC. https://www.nymtc.org/movingforward2055/introduction/index.html ; https://www.nymtc.org/movingforward2055/pdfs/Final_Plan%20ExecutiveSummary_English.pdf
- **Direct funding/strategy language (quote):** “a blueprint that helps support sustainable growth and guides Federal funding for transportation investment in the region.” **Source (2025):** *Moving Forward 2055—Introduction*, NYMTC. https://www.nymtc.org/movingforward2055/introduction/index.html
- **Direct zero-emission ferry hook (quote):** the Plan’s Environment program recommends: “Procure either hybrid or all-electric vehicles and ferries that run with cleaner engines in transit life-cycle replacement programs.” **Source (2025):** *Moving Forward 2055—Our Recommendations*, NYMTC. https://www.nymtc.org/movingforward2055/chapter-4/index.html
- **Resilience hook.** The plan recommends identifying vulnerable transportation assets (including Communities of Concern), a system redundancy assessment, energy-vulnerability assessment, and programming redundancy improvements as appropriate. **Source (2025):** *Moving Forward 2055—Our Recommendations*, NYMTC. https://www.nymtc.org/movingforward2055/chapter-4/index.html
- **Waterborne relevance.** The RTP includes ferry landings in its multimodal system description, and NYMTC’s Mobility Studies page identifies a *Ferry Parking and Landside Access Study* intended to assess sites for facilities supporting waterborne transportation. Specific Suffolk recommendation/project and study result: **OMITTED**—do not claim that NYMTC has endorsed a particular Suffolk–Manhattan service. **Sources (2025/current):** *Final Plan Executive Summary*; *Mobility Studies*, NYMTC. https://www.nymtc.org/movingforward2055/pdfs/Final_Plan%20ExecutiveSummary_English.pdf ; https://www.nymtc.org/Regional-Planning-Activities/Mobility-and-Safety-Planning/Mobility-Studies

### State climate law
- **CLCPA emissions target — at least 85% reduction from 1990 levels by 2050.** New York Environmental Conservation Law §75-0107(1) sets that statewide limit; use “statewide statutory target,” not “a target that directly binds each NYMTC project.” **Source (law; current):** *NYS Environmental Conservation Law §75-0107*, New York State Senate. https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ENV/75-0107
- **Clean-electricity target — 70% renewable electricity by 2030.** New York Public Service Law §66-p(2) directs the Commission to establish a program requiring 70% renewable electricity by 2030; cite it as the state electricity-policy context for shore-power planning. **Source (law; current):** *NYS Public Service Law §66-p*, New York State Senate. https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PBS/66-P
- **CLCPA equity guardrail.** ECL §75-0117 directs investment/benefit tracking for disadvantaged communities with a goal that they receive at least 35%, and no less than 40%, of overall benefits of clean-energy and energy-efficiency spending. **Source (law; current):** *NYS Environmental Conservation Law §75-0117*, New York State Senate. https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ENV/75-0117

## 5. ELECTRIFICATION / RESILIENCE HOOKS

- **Suffolk clean-mobility precedent (state-funded).** NYSERDA reports that Circuit piloted a first/last-mile electric micro-shuttle in **Brentwood and Central Islip**; the fleet comprised **21 100% battery-electric vehicles**, and the project installed **18 EV charging stations** usable by the fleet and public. Award term: **November 2022–30 March 2026**. This is a credible local precedent for public-serving zero-emission mobility and charging—not evidence of marine-shore-power funding. **Source (2022–26):** *Long Island & Queens—Promoting Sustainable Transportation*, NYSERDA. https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/All-Programs/New-York-Clean-Transportation-Prizes-Initiative/Clean-Transportation-in-Action/Long-Island-Promoting-Sustainable-Transportation
- **NYSERDA/LIPA electric-shuttle announcement.** NYSERDA announced the launch of **two 100% electric shuttle services** on Long Island and the Rockaways in **September 2024** under the Clean Transportation Prizes. **Source (2024):** *NYSERDA and LIPA Announce Launch of Electric Shuttle Service*, NYSERDA. https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/About/Newsroom/2024-Announcements/2024-09-23-NYSERDA-And-LIPA-Announce-Launch-Of-Electric-Shuttle-Service
- **Shore-power-specific PSEG Long Island/LIPA incentive, tariff, interconnection capacity, or grant: OMITTED.** Do not claim that an EV program funds marine charging; site-specific electrical feasibility must be checked with the utility/terminal owner.
- **NYMTC planning alignment.** The RTP recommends grid-infrastructure upgrade targeting to accommodate future heavy-duty chargers and procurement of hybrid/all-electric ferries with cleaner engines in transit life-cycle replacement. **Source (2025):** *Moving Forward 2055—Our Recommendations*, NYMTC. https://www.nymtc.org/movingforward2055/chapter-4/index.html

## 6. RECEPTION RISKS — DECK FRAMING REQUIREMENTS

- **Premium Manhattan–Hamptons sensitivity.** Blade’s disclosed Short Distance business is commercial and its cited metrics are company-wide; a proposed premium Manhattan link must not be presented to County/MPO officials as an equity outcome merely because it is electric. **Evidence base (FY 2024):** *Blade 2024 Form 10-K*, SEC. https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1847986&owner=exclude
- **NYMTC’s equity test is explicit.** NYMTC says its policy is to prevent disproportionate adverse effects on minority and low-income populations and its RTP calls for assessment of service levels relative to Communities of Concern. **Source (2025):** *Moving Forward 2055—Introduction* / *Our Recommendations*, NYMTC. https://www.nymtc.org/movingforward2055/introduction/index.html ; https://www.nymtc.org/movingforward2055/chapter-4/index.html
- **State justice guardrail.** CLCPA’s disadvantaged-community benefit goal is at least **35%**, with no less than **40%**, of overall benefits from clean-energy/energy-efficiency spending. **Source (current law):** ECL §75-0117, New York State Senate. https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ENV/75-0117
- **Honest proposition for the deck:** lead with a County-resident/public-purpose network—reliable waterfront/island access, first/last-mile integration with SCT/LIRR, resilience redundancy, workforce access, accessible boarding, public fares set with the authority, and measurable community benefits—then position any Manhattan/premium/tourism service as a secondary, separately governed opportunity. This is a framing recommendation based on the cited policy requirements, not a claim that any authority has approved a route.
- **Other political/implementation risks—terminal availability, maritime permitting, wake/noise, habitat, weather reliability, fare affordability, ADA access, and operating subsidy: OMITTED** for lack of site-specific primary evidence. They require an early agency/terminal/community discovery process rather than an invented mitigation claim.
