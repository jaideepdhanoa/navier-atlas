# Numbers, economics and applicability (V2.1)

Numbers are useful when they establish a relevant proposition. Do not add decorative statistics to pass a density check. Every visible numerical assertion in V2.1 is bound to a claim quantity and its display; product IDs, dates and slide numbers are handled distinctly by the validator.

## Record the basis

A used quantitative claim records metric, value/range, unit and statistic (`typical`, `record`, `single-test`, `target`, `scenario` or `reported`), plus subject/reporter/owner, operating context, dependencies, date and applicability. Currency quantities also need category, currency, geography, date and relevant rates. Revenue is not a fuel tariff; fuel saving is not total operating cost or delivered-service economics.

Applicability is explicit: `same-context`, `supported-transfer`, `benchmark` or `unresolved`. A measured ratio is not portable merely because it is a percentage. Check hull/configuration, speed, payload, mission, sea/operating conditions, geography and date. Supported transfers need evidence/recalculation; a benchmark must remain visibly a benchmark and cannot be sold as partner economics. A model or target is allowed, but must say modeled/target/design and never read as demonstrated.

## Synthetic worked example — fictional only

The following invented figures are a teaching fixture, not a Navier result, market quote or partner case. Currency is **Fictional Credits (FC)**. Mission: a 20-nautical-mile coastal cargo run, 12 tonnes payload, 24 knots, calm-water design condition.

- A fictional test craft in configuration **Blue-12** has modeled propulsion-energy cost of **0.80 FC per tonne-nmi**; a different **Green-9** hull reports **0.50 FC per tonne-nmi** in a different 15-nmi mission at 18 knots. The 37.5% ratio is **not transferable** to Blue-12's mission: hull, speed, route and conditions do not match. It may be shown only as a clearly attributed benchmark or recalculated scenario.
- For Blue-12, a local fictional electricity tariff of **FC 0.20/kWh** and a duty cycle of **400 kWh per run** imply **FC 80 energy cost per run**. That is a scenario calculation, not a measured result.
- A fictional diesel baseline uses **55 litres per run** at **FC 1.60/litre**, or **FC 88 fuel cost per run**. The FC 8 difference is fuel/energy cost only—not total operating cost.
- Total cost also includes fictional crew **FC 120**, maintenance **FC 45**, berth **FC 20** and capital allocation **FC 90**. Blue-12 total operating/service cost is therefore **FC 355** (80 + 120 + 45 + 20 + 90), while the diesel baseline is **FC 373** (88 + 120 + 50 + 20 + 95). A service-price or delivered-cargo claim would additionally need payload, utilization, downtime and pricing assumptions.

Correct authoring: label the FC 80 and FC 355 figures as a modeled scenario with the stated tariff, duty cycle and mission; label the Green-9 number as a foreign-context/other-configuration benchmark if used; do not headline “37.5% saving” for Blue-12 without a supported matched-condition transfer. These numbers are invented and must not be copied into a real deck.

## Review questions

1. What exact proposition does the number establish, and does it change this partner's decision?
2. Are subject, metric/unit, statistic, configuration, mission, conditions, geography and date aligned?
3. Is the claim energy use, energy cost, total operating cost or delivered-service economics?
4. Is the display bound to the authored quantity, with the required unit and claim-linked qualification visible?
5. Does any prior recipient-held document contain a conflicting number? Reconcile by context and source; correct and disclose rather than silently choose the attractive figure.

Diagnostics normalize display formatting (for example `55,000` versus `55k`) separately from substantive conflicts. They compare quantities only when subject, metric/unit/statistic and operating context align. `k`/`K` and uppercase `M` are magnitude abbreviations; lowercase `m` means metres unless currency-prefixed. `kW`, `MW` and `GW` remain distinct units, not silent conversions. Bind each numerical occurrence: one bound figure cannot license an unrelated claim that happens to reuse the same numeral.
