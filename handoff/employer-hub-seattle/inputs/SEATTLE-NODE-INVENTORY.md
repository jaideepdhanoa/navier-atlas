## 1. REGULATORY VERDICT — read this first

### 1.1 Lake Washington speed limits: THE LAKE NETWORK LIVES

**There is no lake-wide speed limit on Lake Washington. Every jurisdiction checked regulates speed only within ~100 yards of shore, piers and restricted areas. Mid-lake is unrestricted (subject to negligent-operation rules).** The screening scan's "7-knot floor" fear (sourced from Reddit) was wrong — that rule is real but applies to the Ship Canal system and shore buffers, not the open lake.

### 1.3 Orca / marine-mammal rules (Puget Sound cluster)

As of **Jan 1, 2025 (RCW 77.15.740, per WDFW)**: all vessels in Washington waters — motorized and non-motorized — must stay **1,000 yards** from Southern Resident killer whales; if inside 400–1,000 yd, slow to **≤7 knots** and move away; if a SRKW surfaces within 400 yd, disengage transmission. Exemptions cover cargo ships in shipping lanes, government/tribal/research vessels, and actively-fishing vessels — **a commercial passenger commuter vessel is NOT exempt** (commercial whale-watch vessels are explicitly not exempt). Also: 200 yd from Bigg's/transient orcas, 100 yd from other whales. Source: https://wdfw.wa.gov/species-habitats/at-risk/species-recovery/orca/regulations ; https://www.bewhalewise.org/killer-whales/

**Operational meaning: this is an encounter-avoidance rule, not a route speed cap.** WSF and Kitsap fast ferries run daily under the same rule. A Navier operator needs: Whale Report Alert System subscription, trained lookouts, a slow/divert protocol, and schedule padding for whale-presence days (SRKW enter Elliott Bay/central Sound episodically, mostly fall–winter). Lake Washington is unaffected (no orcas).

### 1.4 Other Sound / harbor rules

- Elliott Bay & Puget Sound within Seattle: 7 knots within **200 yards** of any shoreline/pier/restricted area; 3 knots inside marinas (incl. Elliott Bay Marina, named in SMC); open bay unrestricted. Source: SPD page above (SMC 16.20.130).
- 4-kn zone at Locks guide piers; Navy 100-yd exclusion / 500-yd slow zones around Navy vessels (Todd Shipyard, Bremerton approaches). Source: SPD page.
- Colman Dock/Pier 50/Pier 52 is dense WSF + Kitsap + KCWT traffic; Kitsap Transit is building a new Seattle fast-ferry terminal at **Pier 46 North Apron** because Pier 50's two slips are saturated — a slip-availability fact that matters for any downtown landing plan. Source: https://www.kitsaptransit.com/seattle-fast-ferry-terminal-project
- Dock permitting (future work, not researched to primary depth this pass): Seattle shoreline permits, Port of Seattle for piers 46–91, WA DNR aquatic-lands leases for any new overwater structure, city shoreline master programs (e.g., Meydenbauer harbor line WAC 332-28-010: https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAc/default.aspx?cite=332-28-010). Flagged **unknown/unscoped**.

### 1.5 Why nothing launched on the lake (the study record)

- 2020 PSRC Puget Sound Passenger-Only Ferry Study (legislature-commissioned, 45 routes screened to 7): **Kirkland–UW and Kenmore–UW ranked top** — highest ridership at lowest operating cost (~$12–15/passenger vs $40–120 for other routes), ~50% travel-time saving for Kirkland–Seattle; flagged UW dock replacement as prerequisite; capital $10M–high-tens-of-millions with terminals $5–35M; implementation punted to local agencies (King County Metro). Source: https://www.theurbanist.org/kirkland-to-uw-and-kenmore-to-uw-rise-to-the-top-in-passenger-only-ferry-study/
- 2015 King County cross-lake study found costs much higher and ridership much lower than competing bus service. Source: https://seattletransitblog.com/2020/06/02/another-passenger-ferry-study/
- Net: public agencies stalled on capital cost + dock gaps, not on speed law or demand. **Employer-funded seat blocks with marina landings sidestep exactly the two blockers (terminal capex, farebox risk) that killed the public version.**

## 8. Proposed network (verified nodes only — two independent clusters, locks excluded)

**Cluster 1 — Lake Washington ("the Bridge Bypass network")**
