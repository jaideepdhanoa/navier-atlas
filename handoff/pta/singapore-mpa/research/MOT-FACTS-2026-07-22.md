## Claim 1 — MPA and LTA are statutory boards under MOT
**Verdict: VERIFIED**

- MOT's own "Overview" page lists its statutory boards: Civil Aviation Authority of Singapore (CAAS), Land Transport Authority (LTA), Maritime & Port Authority of Singapore (MPA), and Public Transport Council (PTC).
- The Singapore Government Directory (SGDI) lists the same four under MOT → Statutory Boards.
- MOT newsroom also refers to "The Ministry of Transport and its statutory boards, the Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore (MPA), … Land Transport Authority (LTA)".

**Safe slide phrasing:** "MPA and LTA are both statutory boards under Singapore's Ministry of Transport (MOT)."
(Optionally: "MOT oversees four statutory boards: CAAS, LTA, MPA and PTC.")

**Sources:**
- MOT — Overview / Our statutory boards: https://www.mot.gov.sg/who-we-are/about-us/
- SGDI — Ministry of Transport: https://www.sgdi.gov.sg/ministries/mot
- MOT newsroom (Transport Sector (Critical Firms) Bill): https://www.mot.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/introduction-of-the-transport-sector--critical-firms--bill/

## Claim 2 — MSGI: MPA committed S$100 million
**Verdict: VERIFIED (with important update — current figure includes a further S$50M in 2024)**

Exact verified framing per MPA's own MSGI page:
- In **2011**, MPA pledged to invest **up to S$100 million over 5 years** in the Maritime Singapore Green Initiative (MSGI).
- Enhancements were made in **2013, 2019 and 2022**.
- In **2024**, MPA refreshed the MSGI and **committed another S$50 million**; the refreshed MSGI comprises **five programmes**: Green Ship, Green Port Infrastructure, Green Craft, Green Energy and Technology, and Green Awareness.

**Safe slide phrasing:** "Under the Maritime Singapore Green Initiative, MPA pledged up to S$100M (2011) and a further S$50M (2024) to incentivise clean and green shipping."
Shorter: "MPA's Maritime Singapore Green Initiative: S$100M pledged in 2011, plus S$50M more in 2024."

**Correction:** Do NOT present S$100M as the "current" total commitment — the initiative has been enhanced repeatedly and topped up with S$50M in 2024. Avoid summing to "S$150M total" on a slide unless you footnote both tranches; MPA itself does not publish a single combined total.

**Sources:**
- MPA — Maritime Singapore Green Initiative: https://www.mpa.gov.sg/maritime-singapore/sustainability/maritime-singapore-green-initiative
- MPA media release (2011 launch): https://www.mpa.gov.sg/media-centre/details/s100-million-boost-to-clean-and-green-shipping-in-singapore
- MOT — An environmentally sustainable maritime Singapore: https://www.mot.gov.sg/what-we-do/green-transport/maritime-environment-responsibility/

## Claim 3 — MOT division for sea transport policy; "Futures and Transformation" division
**Verdict: PARTLY (Sea Transport Division VERIFIED; "Futures and Transformation" division DOES NOT EXIST under that name)**

Per the official Singapore Government Directory (sgdi.gov.sg), MOT's departments/divisions are:
- Air Hub Division
- Aviation Relations and Systems Division
- Connectivity and Logistics Division
- Corporate Development Division
- Group Chief Internal Audit Office
- International Relations and Security Division
- National Transport Academy
- Public Transport Division
- Private and Shared Mobility Division
- **Sea Transport Division** ← the maritime policy division
- Strategic Communications Division
- **Strategic Planning and Organisational Transformation Division**
- Technology Strategies and Policy Division
- Transport Safety Investigation Bureau

There is **no division named "Futures and Transformation"**. The closest real names are the "Strategic Planning and Organisational Transformation Division" and the Deputy Secretary portfolio "Strategy, Sustainability & Transformation". Do not use "Futures and Transformation" on any slide.

**Safe slide phrasing:** "MOT's Sea Transport Division oversees maritime and port policy." (Exact official name: **Sea Transport Division**.)

**Sources:**
- SGDI — MOT Sea Transport Division: https://www.sgdi.gov.sg/ministries/mot/departments/st
- SGDI — MOT departments list: https://www.sgdi.gov.sg/ministries/mot

Note: SGDI is the official gov.sg directory and reflects the current structure; mot.gov.sg itself does not publish a standalone organisation-structure page (the old URL 404s).

## Claim 4 — Changi Village has no nearby MRT; nearest station and journey time to CBD
**Verdict: PARTLY (no MRT at Changi Village = well supported; specific distance and CBD journey time = UNVERIFIED, no citable primary source)**

What is supportable:
- Changi Village does not have an MRT station. No station named Changi Village exists on LTA's official station list (LTA fare-calculator station list enumerates all MRT/LRT stations; none serves Changi Village). Singapore street-directory records for Changi Village Road list "Nearest MRT Station: Nil".
- Changi Village is served by buses via Changi Village Bus Terminal (services incl. 2, 29, 59, 89, 109).
- Changi Airport Group's own visitor guide states: "The nearest MRT stations are Tanah Merah and Tampines," with bus connections to Changi Village. (CAG is a reputable but not government-transport source.)
- For internal context only (own calculation, not citable): straight-line distances from Changi Village are roughly 3.5 km to Changi Airport MRT (no direct bus link), ~6 km to Tampines, ~8 km to Tanah Merah. Practical rail access is via Tanah Merah or Tampines + a bus ride of ~20–30 min.

What is NOT supportable:
- **Journey time Changi Village → Raffles Place: UNVERIFIED.** No LTA/gov or major-outlet source publishes this figure. Journey planners (Google Maps, OneMap, Moovit) show roughly 60–75 minutes by public transport, but these are live tools, not citable static sources.

**Safe slide phrasing:** "Changi Village has no MRT station — the nearest stations (Tanah Merah, Tampines) are a bus ride away."
If a journey time is essential, either (a) omit the number, or (b) present it as "typically over an hour by public transport (per OneMap/Google Maps journey planner, retrieved [date])" with a screenshot kept on file. Do not state a precise minute figure without that.

**Sources:**
- LTA Fare Calculator (full official station list — no Changi Village station): https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/map/fare-calculator.html
- Changi Airport Group, "Changi Village: A hidden gem…": https://nowboarding.changiairport.com/explore-singapore/changi-village-a-hidden-gem-tucked-away-from-the-bustling-city.html

## Claim 5 — TEL now serves Marine Parade / East Coast (opened June 2024)
**Verdict: VERIFIED**

- LTA news release: "Thomson–East Coast Line 4 to Welcome Commuters from 23 June 2024."
- TEL Stage 4 opened **23 June 2024** with **seven stations**: Tanjong Rhu, Katong Park, Tanjong Katong, Marine Parade, Marine Terrace, Siglap, Bayshore — serving the Marine Parade / East Coast corridor.

**Safe slide phrasing:** "Since June 2024, the Thomson–East Coast Line serves Marine Parade and the East Coast."
**Warning:** Never claim East Coast "has no rail" — that has been false since 23 June 2024.

**Sources:**
- LTA — Thomson-East Coast Line project page (links the TEL4 opening release): https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/upcoming_projects/rail_expansion/thomson_east_coast_line.html

## Claim 6 — Net-zero by 2050 national target; Singapore Green Plan 2030 exists
**Verdict: VERIFIED**

- **Net zero:** On 25 Oct 2022, Singapore (NCCS/MSE joint release) committed to "achieve net zero emissions by 2050" as part of its Long-Term Low-Emissions Development Strategy, alongside a revised 2030 NDC (~60 MtCO2e in 2030).
- **Green Plan:** The Singapore Green Plan 2030, launched February 2021, is described officially as "a whole-of-nation movement to advance Singapore's national agenda on sustainable development," positioning Singapore to achieve net zero by 2050.

**Safe slide phrasing:** "Singapore has committed to net-zero emissions by 2050, and the Singapore Green Plan 2030 sets national sustainability targets for this decade."

**Sources:**
- NCCS press release (25 Oct 2022): https://www.nccs.gov.sg/media/press-releases/singapore-commits-to-achieve-net-zero/
- Singapore Green Plan 2030 (official site): https://www.greenplan.gov.sg/
- MSE — Singapore Green Plan 2030: https://www.mse.gov.sg/resources/sgp-2030/

## Claim 7 — Official sandbox / trial program for maritime or transport tech
**Verdict: PARTLY (MPA Living Lab VERIFIED as a named official test-bedding initiative; the phrase "regulatory sandbox" is NOT an official MPA/MOT program name)**

- **MPA Living Lab** exists and is documented on mpa.gov.sg: it "brings stakeholders together to co-innovate, test-bed new systems, and bring technological and engineering solutions closer to market." MPA media releases group it with PSA Living Lab and Jurong Port Living Lab.
- MPA leadership speeches reference a "sandbox to facilitate the testing of MASS [autonomous ship] technology" and Singapore being "a living lab" — but no program formally named "regulatory sandbox" for maritime was found on mpa.gov.sg/mot.gov.sg.
- Additionally, MSGI's **Green Energy and Technology Programme (GETP)** (mpa.gov.sg) supports pilot trials of green technologies — citable by exact name if the deck needs a green-trials hook.

**Safe slide phrasing:** "MPA operates the MPA Living Lab to test-bed new maritime technologies in real port conditions."
**Correction:** Do not label it a "regulatory sandbox" on a slide; use "living lab" / "test-bedding initiative", which is MPA's own language.

**Sources:**
- MPA — MPA Living Lab: https://www.mpa.gov.sg/maritime-singapore/innovation-and-r-d/milestones-key-projects/mpa-living-lab
- MPA media release (developer space; MPA/PSA/Jurong Port Living Labs): https://www.mpa.gov.sg/media-centre/details/mpa-launches-new-developer-space-initiative-set-to-boost-singapore-s-maritime-technological-capabilities
