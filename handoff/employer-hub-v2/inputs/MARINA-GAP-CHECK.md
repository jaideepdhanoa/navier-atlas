## Verdicts

### Palo Alto
The historic Palo Alto Yacht Harbor is confirmed closed since 1986, with historical silting documented; no verified current dredging or reopening plan was found. A public Baylands Sailing Station dock exists, but low-tide mud/shallow-channel conditions and missing berth-depth/commercial-permission documentation make it unsuitable to count as a 30–45 ft commercial landing without a site survey and approvals.

### Foster City
Public launches exist at Boat Park and Leo J. Ryan Park, but both are explicitly on the interior Foster City Lagoon. The cited USACE notice identifies intake/outfall infrastructure and emergency-rescue-boat ramps—not a public navigable Bay-side passenger landing—so a Bay connection and 30–45 ft commercial berth remain unverified.

### Fremont
Newark Slough has a named refuge boat ramp, but FWS says it is only usable at high tides and describes it in a hunting-access context; no commercial berth, depth, or public passenger access is documented. Fremont’s DBW-listed Central Park/Lake Elizabeth launch is inland and does not address the Bay shoreline gap.

### Long Beach
Reynolds Channel has a municipal hard-surface launch and a public fishing pier, while the nearby Empire Point marina has substantial slip/haul infrastructure; none is documented as a public 30–45 ft commercial passenger berth. Ocean access is technically served by Jones Inlet or East Rockaway Inlet navigation channels, but current depth/maintenance and inlet shoaling are the controlling constraints.
