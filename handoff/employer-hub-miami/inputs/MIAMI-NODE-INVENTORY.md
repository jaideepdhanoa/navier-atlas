# Miami / Fort Lauderdale Node Inventory — Employer Water Network (deep pass)

Status: source-verified desk research, Aug 15 2026. Follows NODE-INVENTORY-SPEC.md (fail-closed). Every node below was checked against a live source (operator page, city/county/marina site, FWC/USCG regulatory document, or news). Walk/shuttle minutes are **map-estimated unless a source is cited**. Landing gate applied: usable pier or marina berth for a 30–45 ft commercial passenger vessel; a full ferry terminal is NOT required.

Model reminder: 20-seat electric hydrofoil, employer seat blocks (non-binding LOIs), launch trigger 60–80 committed seats/corridor, $650–1,200/seat-month, 20–45 min crossings.

**Headline findings of this pass:**
1. **A free public commuter water taxi is live today** (launched Jan 20 2026): Maurice Gibb Memorial Park (Sunset Harbour, Miami Beach) ↔ Venetian Marina & Yacht Club (Edgewater, Miami), 40-ft/55-pax vessels, 20-min crossing, weekdays 7:00–19:30, operated for the City of Miami Beach by **Water Taxi of Miami Beach, LLC** (miamibeachfl.gov/city-hall/transportation/water-taxi/ ; miamibeachfl.gov/take-the-scenic-water-route-to-and-from-miami-beach/ ; cbsnews.com/miami). This proves a bay commuter crossing at ~20 min is regulatorily and operationally possible **today** — but it is free (city-funded pilot), so Navier's premium pitch must be frequency, reach (Brickell/Key Biscayne/Grove), and guaranteed employer seats, not the crossing itself.
2. **The manatee/speed-zone map does NOT kill the Miami network.** Central Biscayne Bay retains designated 30/35-mph channels and large unzoned open-bay areas; the fatal-at-speed water is specific and avoidable (Miami River = idle all year; downtown ICW channel Venetian Cswy→south tip of Brickell Key = idle all year; Government Cut/Fisherman's Channel = USCG slow-speed RNA since Jan 29 2025; Port Everglades = slow all waters year-round; New River = slow all year). Details with citations in Regulatory section.
3. **Brickell has a real, existing commercial landing**: Vice City Marina, 801 Brickell Bay Dr — 36-slip full-service marina in the heart of the financial district, ~2 min walk (map estimate) from Citadel's 1201 Brickell Bay Dr HQ site.

## No landing exists (demand areas with no usable dock — do NOT promote)

- **Brickell Key (Claughton Island):** City commissioners **designated** a water-taxi site on Brickell Key in Aug 2017, and Swire's 2024 master-plan press coverage says it "wants to add a water taxi / ferry service" — but no public landing has been sourced as built. Swire is also in litigation over a $32M seawall assessment (May 2026). Status honest: **designated/planned, not built.** (miamitodaynews.com/2017/08/08/miami-designates-brickell-key-water-taxi-service/ ; facebook.com/bloombergbusiness Swire post; therealdeal.com/miami/2026/05/26/swire-sued-over-32-million-brickell-key-condo-assessment/)
- **Wynwood / Midtown / Design District:** Entirely inland — no water frontage on Biscayne Bay (nearest bay edge is Edgewater). No dock exists or is possible without using Edgewater as the node. Wynwood is ~1.5–2 mi from the Venetian Marina dock = shuttle-tier at best.
- **Edgewater / Margaret Pace Park:** Bayfront park named in the Miami DDA's waterborne-transportation vision and its 2024 possible-dockage-sites map, but **no dock exists today** (miamidda.com/Urban-Planning/Transportation-Mobility/Waterborne-Transportation). Venetian Marina (2 blocks north) is the real node — Margaret Pace is a future infill candidate, paper only.
- **Mount Sinai Medical Center (Miami Beach):** 4,000+ employees on a literally bayfront campus at 4300 Alton Rd (msmc.com/about-msmc/history/), but **no vessel landing at the campus could be sourced.** Nearest real dock is Sunset Harbour (~2.3 mi south, map est.) = shuttle-tier. A campus dock would need Miami Beach + DERM Class I permitting — flagged as the single best dock-infill opportunity in the whole market (hospital shift workers + causeway congestion).
- **North Bay Village:** No public marina/dock. Private condo marinas exist (e.g., Grand Bay Marina slips advertised resident-to-resident) and Shuckers' restaurant dock has closed per Yelp. Nearest public node is Pelican Harbor Marina across the 79th St bridge. (yelp.com Shuckers-closed listing; facebook.com/groups/nbvvoice)
- **Key Biscayne village center:** The village has no public commuter dock; Key Biscayne Yacht Club is private, and Crandon Marina is 2 mi north of the village center. Crandon = park-&-ride, not walk-tier.
- **Fisher Island:** Closed private community served by its own 24-hr ferry from Terminal Island (fisherislandclub.com/island-transportation/). Structurally unavailable to a shared network — do not count its (very high) income base.
- **PortMiami / Royal Caribbean HQ (1050 Caribbean Way):** Royal Caribbean Group is building a ~10-story, ~380–495K sf HQ with ~1,500 workstations, completion 2026 (royalcaribbeangroup.com/major-milestone-in-project-skyline ; bizjournals.com). It is on Dodge Island **inside the seaport's controlled perimeter**, and the adjacent Fisherman's Channel is a USCG slow-speed RNA. A landing would be a port-authority negotiation (like JBAB in the DC scan) — growth-phase, partnership-dependent, not a Phase-1 node.
- **Aventura Mall / Aventura Hospital:** Both ~1.5–2 mi inland from any dock; Loggerhead Aventura Marina is the only real landing and is shuttle-tier to both.
- **Hollywood CBD / Memorial Regional / HEICO (3000 Taft St):** Hollywood's actual employers are 2–3 mi inland from the ICW; the Margaritaville dock serves the beach, not the job base.
- **Government Cut / South Pointe Pier area:** The fishing pier is not a vessel landing; Miami Beach Marina is the real node there.

Miami Beach is residential/hospitality-heavy with one giant unserved employer (Mount Sinai).

## Commute-pain benchmarks

- **Key Biscayne → mainland:** Miami Herald editorial reports commute times of **80–90 minutes** during the Rickenbacker bridge-repair period; the causeway is the island's only road and FDOT bridge work continues in phases (miamiherald.com/opinion/editorials/article287695485.html ; nbcmiami.com FDOT Rickenbacker project; local10.com Apr 2024 "two to over six hours" worst-case closure day). A 20–25 min foil to Brickell is transformational here.
- **Miami Beach → Downtown/Brickell:** up to ~45 min in rush hour vs ~10–15 min off-peak via MacArthur/I-395 (lauriereader.com/blog/driving-miami/); MacArthur also takes cruise-day surges (PortMiami). The city's own water taxi does it in a **consistent 20 min** and advertises "no delays from bridge openings" (miamibeachfl.gov).
- **Region:** INRIX 2025 Global Traffic Scorecard: typical US driver lost 49 hrs (+11% YoY, $894/driver); Miami ranks among the most congested US metros — Miami-specific 2025 figure on INRIX's city page (inrix.com/scorecard-city/?city=Miami, FL) shows a triple-digit hours-lost value that we could not render cleanly — **flag exact 2025 Miami number unverified**; INRIX 2024 put Miami at $1,325/driver congestion cost (prior scan, inrix.com 2024 press release). Secondary 2025 coverage: "average driver in Miami lost over 90 hours" (SecretMiami citing a 2025 study — secondary).
- **North corridor (Aventura/Hollywood → Brickell or FTL):** I-95 peak windows 7–9 AM / 4–7 PM; Broward→Brickell commuter anecdotes run 60–75+ min (reddit.com/r/Broward; facebook Weston group — secondary/anecdotal, flagged).
- **Brickell Ave Bridge (for anyone near the river):** by federal rule the draw stays **closed to boats** 7:35–8:59 AM, 12:05–12:59 PM, 4:35–5:59 PM weekdays and otherwise opens only on the hour/half-hour 7 AM–7 PM (33 CFR 117.305(d), ecfr.gov) — i.e., road traffic on Brickell Ave is protected at peak, and **a water service that stays bayside of the bridge (Vice City Marina, EPIC bay face) never waits for it.**

## Regulatory & operational findings (the corridor-decisive section)

### Manatee protection & speed zones — Miami-Dade (FWC rule 68C-22.025; BRAs 68D-24.013; official FWC map: myfwc.com/media/7310/miamidadempz-bramaps.pdf)

**Fast-capable water (verified from FWC map sheets):**
- **Mid-bay designated channels:** "30 MPH All Year" ICW-channel segments run through the north bay (68C-22.025(1)(g)1 — e.g., marker-defined stretches around Broad Causeway and between 79th St and Broad Cswy) and a **"Meloy/East channel 30 MPH All Year"** segment ((1)(g)2) plus **35 MPH All Year** areas ((1)(i)1–2) between the Julia Tuttle and Venetian basins on the Miami Beach side. Large mid-bay areas between 79th St Cswy and Julia Tuttle, and the open bay north of Rickenbacker Causeway, carry **no FWC zone at all** (white on the FWC map) — normal navigation-rules speed.
- **The bay between Venetian and Julia Tuttle** ((1)(a)8) and **Venetian to MacArthur** ((1)(a)9) are slow-speed **"except as designated"** — the designated 30/35-mph channels are the exception. This is exactly the water the live 20-min city water taxi uses.
- **Open bay Brickell→Key Biscayne:** unzoned mid-bay with 1000-ft slow-speed shore buffers ((1)(a)15, (1)(a)21 etc.) and a 100-yd idle pocket at the Rickenbacker Causeway bridge (68D-24.013(1)(a)7). Foil-speed for ~80% of the run.

**Slow/idle water (avoid or price in):**
- **Miami River: IDLE SPEED ALL YEAR, entire river** from SR-953 to the ICW ((1)(c)4, incl. Palmer Lake, Seybold Canal, South Fork). River service can only ever be a slow circulator; add drawbridge curfews (33 CFR 117.305) on top.
- **Downtown bayfront ICW channel: IDLE SPEED ALL YEAR from 100 yd N of Venetian Cswy to the southern tip of Claughton Is (Brickell Key)** (68D-24.013(1)(a)6) and **slow-speed all year around Brickell Key** ((1)(a)11). The last ~1–2 nm into any Downtown/Brickell landing is a fixed slow segment (~10–15 min) — unavoidable, same for everyone.
- **Government Cut & Fisherman's Channel: USCG Regulated Navigation Area, slow speed for vessels <50 m**, effective Jan 29 2025 (Federal Register docket USCG-2024-0205; news.uscg.mil article 4020783). Kills fast transits past PortMiami and slows any ocean exit via Government Cut.
- **No-Entry zone (all year) in Biscayne Bay west of Virginia Key** ((1)(d)1, yellow on map) — route around, do not cross.
- **North of Broad Cswy / Sunny Isles / Dumbfoundling Bay:** slow-speed-all-year zones that **include the marked ICW channel** in parts of Sunny Isles/Maule Lake ((1)(a)3, (1)(a)5), with the ICW channel exemption being only **seasonal**: (1)(b)2 "ICW channel Slow Speed Nov 15–Apr 30, 30 MPH May 1–Nov 14." **Aventura is winter-slow — Nov 15 to Apr 30 the channel itself drops to slow speed.** This is the market's Seattle-style seasonal geometry trap, and it's why Aventura is growth-phase.

### Manatee protection & speed zones — Broward (FWC rule 68C-22.010; official map: FWC browardmpz maps PDF, mirrored at bythesearealty.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/browardmpz-maps.pdf)

- **General ICW rule (2)(g): 25 mph in-channel with a 50-ft slow-speed buffer** — in force **Apr 1–Nov 14 at all times, and Nov 15–Mar 31 all day on WEEKDAYS plus 7 PM–7 AM weekends/holidays** (FWC map legend). The complementary zone (2)(e) makes the ICW slow-speed only **7 AM–7 PM on weekends/holidays Nov 15–Mar 31**. **Read: a weekday commuter service keeps 25 mph in the Broward ICW channel year-round.** ⚠ Conflict flag: FWC's Nov 2025 news release paraphrases the winter rule as "slow-speed all day on weekdays" (myfwc.com/news/all-news/broward-boating-zone-1125/) — the official map legend says the opposite (25 mph weekdays). **Resolve against the actual rule text 68C-22.010 F.A.C. before scheduling; treat as open item.**
- **New year-round slow zone (Nov 2025):** FWC approved a year-round slow-speed minimum-wake BRA in the ICW **between Oakland Park Blvd and Sunrise Blvd bridges**, superseding the seasonal manatee zone there; the bordering bridges already sit in year-round idle zones (myfwc.com/news/all-news/broward-boating-zone-1125/). Budget this pinch on any Pompano→FTL run.
- **Port Everglades: slow-speed applies in ALL port waters year-round**, plus a 6,000-ft **idle** zone in the ICW (south end Berth 25 to mid-Berth 31) Nov 15–Mar 31 (porteverglades.net/environment/manatee-season/). Every Hollywood↔FTL trip transits this slow segment (~1.5 nm).
- **New River: slow-speed all year, both forks, through downtown FTL** ((2)(d)8 on FWC map). Downtown FTL is a slow-water destination, full stop.
- **Hollywood/Hallandale: ICW channel 25 MPH ALL YEAR** (map sheet 6) — the best fast water in Broward, conveniently covering the Hollywood↔Port Everglades approach.

### Other regulatory/ops items

- **Hydrofoil nuance (argument to make, not assume):** FWC manatee zones regulate speed/wake, not hull type. Whether a foiling vessel at 25–30 kt is treated purely by posted speed, or draws extra scrutiny in an MPP update, is **unknown** — engage FWC + Miami-Dade DERM early. Miami-Dade's Manatee Protection Plan is being updated now and the Miami DDA is formally monitoring it to keep water-taxi stops permissible (miamidda.com waterborne page; facebook.com/MiamiDadeRER draft-MPP notice).
- **Dock permitting:** any work in/on/over tidal waters of Miami-Dade requires a **DERM Class I permit** (miamidade.gov permit page per1723653396938982); City of Miami additionally uses Marine Operating Permits — the Miami DDA board resolved in Mar 2024 to pursue Class I permits/MOPs for preferred water-taxi stop sites and to draft a model water-taxi operator ordinance (miamidda.com) — **Navier should ride this DDA process rather than start cold.** In Broward, any in-water activity needs a **Broward County Environmental Resource License** (broward.org/ePermits, license type 8; county also holds USACE SPGP delegation). Miami Beach requires marine businesses to hold a Certificate of Use + dockage/landing agreement (miamibeachfl.gov/marine-related-business/). **FIND** (Florida Inland Navigation District) manages the ICW right-of-way and runs a waterway-assistance grant program across both counties (aicw.org) — a funding path for dock infill (e.g., Mount Sinai, Margaret Pace).
- **Hurricane season:** Jun 1–Nov 30 exposure — plan seasonal haul-out/berthing contingencies and service-suspension protocol; marinas require them (standard practice; magnitude unquantified this pass). Winter (peak season) is conversely the calmest, highest-demand window — but also manatee season (Nov 15–Mar 31/Apr 30), which is precisely when the seasonal zones bite. **The seasonal trap: peak demand season = peak restriction season on seasonal-zone corridors.** Phase-1 corridors below are chosen to be season-proof (year-round zone status only).
- **Security zones:** PortMiami and Port Everglades are security-sensitive; normal transit allowed but expect USCG activity, cruise-day surges, and the RNA slow rules above.

## Proposed network (verified nodes only)

Two clusters, one deferred inter-city express. Interchange hubs: **Brickell (Vice City Marina/EPIC)** and **Venetian Marina (Edgewater/Omni)** in Miami; **17th St/Convention Center (F4/H2)** in Broward.

### Phase 1 — Miami cluster (launch)
- **Island Line (flagship): Crandon Marina ↔ Brickell (Vice City Marina).** ~20–25 min vs 80–90 min crisis-era drives. Single-road island, $181K median income, park-&-ride at Crandon. Employer anchor: entire Brickell finance cluster within 12 min of the dock. This is the strongest single corridor found in any city scanned to date.
- **Grove Line: Dinner Key ↔ Brickell.** 15–20 min; City Hall + Grove offices southbound reverse-commute; same Brickell hub.
- **Beach Line: Miami Beach Marina (SoFi) ↔ Brickell**, via Norris Cut. 20–25 min; premium SoFi catchment to finance jobs; complements (does not fight) the city's free Sunset Harbour↔Venetian service.
- Hub note: Brickell landing capacity is the binding constraint — Vice City Marina is small (36 slips); secure 2 dedicated berths there AND at EPIC Marina as overflow/second gate. Both are existing commercial facilities; no construction needed.

### Phase 2 — Miami growth
- **North Bay Line: Pelican Harbor (79th St) ↔ Venetian Marina ↔ Miamarina/Bayside.** Uses 30/35-mph mid-bay water; accepts the idle tail into downtown.
- **Gold Coast extension: Bill Bird Marina (Bal Harbour/Haulover) ↔ Venetian/Downtown** (30-mph ICW segments; verify Bill Bird construction status).
- **Museum Park infill:** promote Ferré Park Deepwater Slip berth via Bayfront Park Trust + DDA Class-I process for an A&E-District gate.
- **Aventura (summer seasonal only)** unless FWC/DERM MPP update changes the winter channel rule.
- **Dock-infill advocacy:** Mount Sinai campus dock (4,000+ employees, bayfront, no landing) and Margaret Pace Park (DDA candidate site) — FIND grant-eligible.

### Phase 1 — Fort Lauderdale cluster (can launch independently, smaller)
- **Hollywood Commuter: H1 Margaritaville ↔ F4/H2 17th St ↔ downtown Riverwalk (city docks/F1).** 25-mph Hollywood channel + PEV slow segment + New River crawl; ~35–45 min end-to-end, competitive at peak; existing docks at every stop. AM commuter window is unserved by the incumbent leisure-hours Water Taxi.
- **Isles Shuttle: Bahia Mar/Las Olas Marina ↔ downtown Riverwalk.** Short, slow-water, schedule-certainty product (beach/isles residents to Las Olas offices) — sells on reliability, not speed.
- Positioning: partner with, don't fight, Water Taxi FTL — they own leisure 10:00–22:00; Navier owns 6:30–9:30/16:30–19:00 commuter blocks, potentially using their docks under agreement.

### Deferred — Inter-city express (Miami ↔ FTL)
Not Phase-1-credible. Both routings (inside ICW, outside ocean) are 60–90+ min with hard regulatory slow segments at both ends and either winter speed gating (inside) or open-Atlantic sea-state exposure (outside). Revisit only if (a) an anchor employer pre-commits both ends, and (b) the vessel's offshore sea-state envelope is proven. The Brightline train already serves this city-pair in ~30–40 min — the water product cannot beat it on time.

### Line-name suggestions (plain-English)
Island Line (Key Biscayne), Grove Line, Beach Line (SoFi), Bay Line (Sunset Harbour–Venetian overlay/extension), North Bay Line, Gold Coast Line (Haulover), New River Line (FTL), Hollywood Express (name already in market use by Water Taxi FTL — pick "Hollywood Commuter" to avoid collision).
