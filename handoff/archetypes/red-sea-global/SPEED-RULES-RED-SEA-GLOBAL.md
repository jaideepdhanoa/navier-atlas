## 3 · Operating-speed assumptions for schedule math (stated, DERIVED)
Base schedule math (NODES file) uses:
- **25 kn service speed** in open lagoon water on marked transfer routes (matches RSG's existing speedboat transfer practice — Turtle Bay↔Sheybarah in 30–40 min over ~10 nm implies ~17–20 kn today; foiling at 25 kn is a modest uplift, not a new speed class) [DERIVED].
- **8 kn harbor/reef regime for the first/last 0.5 nm** at every landing, and across flagged Conservation Priority Zones / reef passes / turtle & seagrass areas until RSG's zone maps are overlaid [DERIVED planning assumption — RSG publishes zone categories but not a public navigation chart of them].
- 12-month operating year, summer-heat midday shape (Jun–Sep midday dip, evening peak) per international addendum [canon assumption, stated].

## 4 · What relief unlocks (foiling-native case — label as such, never as a granted exemption)
The rules' own purposes are wake, noise, pollution, anchor/prop damage, and wildlife disturbance:
- Foiling lifts the hull clear of the water: minimal wake at speed, near-silent approach, zero exhaust — directly serving the MSP's stated "avoid pollution and noise" purpose, in the only mode class consistent with RSG's published **electric-only marine vehicles** measure [PRIMARY commitment + DERIVED fit].
- Over seagrass/coral in adequate depth, a foiling pass at height produces less pressure wave and turbidity than a displacement/planing hull at the same speed — position as "the mode the MSP assumed would exist," pitched for RSG's own zone-by-zone approval [DERIVED — no third-party reef-impact study cited; do not render as a measured claim].
- Relief case: where RSG's internal rules slow conventional craft for wake/noise (marina approaches, resort frontages, turtle zones), a foiling vessel may justify higher permitted transit speed **at RSG's discretion** — model only as upside, never in base math.
