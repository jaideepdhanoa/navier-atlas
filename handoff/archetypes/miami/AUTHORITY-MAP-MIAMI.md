# Miami / Fort Lauderdale Authority Map — Public Partners Research

The controlling regime is manatee protection (see `SPEED-RULES-MIAMI.md`) — animal-strike prevention, not wake control — and **no relief is sought or implied for any manatee zone**. ## 2 · Per-authority profiles

### Miami-Dade County DTPW
- **Mandate:** county transportation department — operates Metrorail, Metromover, Metrobus, and county mobility programs. Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/transportation/home.page
- **Controls:** the transit network our stops feed (Metromover at Bayside/Omni; Brickell Metrorail/Metromover), transit fare infrastructure (EASY card), and county transportation planning. No marina landings.
- **Water transit involvement — researched honestly:** the **Poseidon Ferry pilot was a City of Miami Beach program, not (on the evidence found) a DTPW program**. Miami Beach launched a water taxi pilot with Poseidon Ferry (residents free / paid tiers, daily 7:30am–7:30pm), subsidized it at **$50,000/month in a no-bid deal (June 2024)**, and **ended that deal after maintenance problems** (Miami Herald, 2024). A successor **free** service launched **January 20, 2026** under the City of Miami Beach with Poseidon Ferry named on the city's page. Earlier attempts failed in 2017, 2020, and 2022 (secondary). No DTPW-run ferry pilot was located this pass — **if a DTPW water-taxi role exists it is unverified; do not assert it.** Sources: https://www.miamiherald.com/news/local/community/miami-dade/miami-beach/article290868214.html · https://www.miamibeachfl.gov/city-hall/transportation/water-taxi/ · https://www.instagram.com/p/DTvhxYukUYl/ (secondary, for the 2017/2020/2022 history)
- **Posture: OPERATE-capable (it is a large public transit operator), but with no verified water program — engage as the *network-integration* authority (Metromover/Metrorail connections, EASY-card integration), not yet as a water-transit buyer.**

### Miami-Dade County Parks, Recreation and Open Spaces
- **Mandate/controls:** operates the county marina system ("Miami-Dade County Parks has 6 Marinas"), including **Crandon Park Marina** (Key Biscayne stop), **Pelican Harbor Marina** (79th St stop), and **Bill Bird Marina at Haulover Park** (Bal Harbour/Haulover stop) — three of the network's sixteen landings. Sources: https://miamidade.gov/marinas · https://www.miamidade.gov/global/recreation/park/crandon-park.page · https://www.miamidade.gov/global/recreation/park/pelican-harbor-marina.page · https://www.miamidade.gov/global/recreation/park/haulover-park.page · https://www.facebook.com/CrandonParkMarina/posts/miami-dade-county-parks-has-6-marinas-to-choose-from-theres-no-need-to-park-and-/2529265193753182/
- **Posture: ENABLE.** Marina landlord/access grantor; does not run transit.

### Miami-Dade County DERM
- **Mandate:** county environmental regulator for Biscayne Bay; publishes the county's manatee-protection guidance and coordinates with FWC on vessel-speed rules. Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/environment/ecosystems/manatees.page
- **Controls:** county environmental permits for coastal works (Class I-type permitting for dock modifications) — **specific permit classes not document-verified this pass; flagged for legal review before any landing modification.**
- **Posture: ENABLE only (permitting gate).**

### PortMiami (Miami-Dade Seaport Department)
- **Mandate:** county seaport department; "world's leading cruise port," $43B/yr economic contribution; Director/CEO Hydi Webb. Sources: https://www.miamidade.gov/portmiami/home.page · https://www.miamidade.gov/global/government/biographies/seaport.page
- **Controls:** port waters context around Government Cut/Fisherman's Channel (with USCG); the MIA-3 Beach Line transits the RNA at its mouth. No network landings on port property.
- **Posture: ENABLE (stakeholder/consultation for Government Cut transits; not a landing grantor in this network).**

### FDOT District Six
- **Mandate:** state DOT district for Miami-Dade and Monroe counties (2.55M people). Source: https://www.fdot.gov/info/moredot/districts/dist6.shtm
- **Controls:** the causeways whose congestion is the network's core pitch (Rickenbacker access via county, MacArthur/Julia Tuttle state roads), state transportation funding programs.
- **Published plans:** none located this pass specific to water transit — **unverified; do not claim FDOT water-transit planning.**
- **Posture: ENABLE.**

### FWC
- **Mandate/controls:** adopts and enforces manatee protection zones (68C-22.025 Dade, 68C-22.010 Broward) and boating restricted areas (Nov 2025 Lauderdale Beach slow-speed minimum-wake BRA). The speed regime is the operating envelope; FWC is a regulator to respect, not a partner to pitch. Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-68C-22-025 · https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-68C-22-010 · https://myfwc.com/news/all-news/broward-boating-zone-1125/
- **Posture: regulator — no Enable/Operate classification. No manatee-zone relief case (see SPEED-RULES-MIAMI.md).**

### FDEP — Biscayne Bay Aquatic Preserve
- **Mandate:** Biscayne Bay is a state aquatic preserve (§258.397, F.S.); FDEP manages preserve resources and (with the Trustees) state submerged-lands authorizations that dock modifications typically require. Sources: https://floridadep.gov/rcp/aquatic-preserve/documents/biscayne-bay-aquatic-preserve-management-plan · https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2025/258.397
- **Posture: ENABLE only (permitting gate). Using existing marina berths as-is minimizes exposure; any structural change triggers this layer — flag for legal review.**

### City of Miami
- **Mandate/controls:** owns and operates the city marina system: **Dinner Key Marina** (Florida's largest wet-slip marina, 587 slips; Coconut Grove stop) and **Miamarina at Bayside** (Downtown/Bayside stop) are both city facilities. Sources: https://www.miami.gov/Parks-Public-Places/Marinas · https://www.miami.gov/Parks-Public-Places/Marinas/Dinner-Key-Marina-Mooring-Facility
- **Published plans:** none pulled at document level this pass (Miami Forever climate/resilience programs exist but were not verified here — **unverified, cite nothing yet**).
- **Posture: ENABLE.** Two of three Phase-1 Miami-cluster origin landings (Grove) plus the Phase-2 Bayside interchange run through this one city landlord — the highest-leverage municipal relationship on the Miami side.

### City of Miami Beach
- **Mandate/controls:** Transportation Department **operates a free commuter water taxi across Biscayne Bay since January 20, 2026** — Maurice Gibb Memorial Park (18th St & Purdy Ave, our Sunset Harbour stop) ↔ downtown Miami at the Venetian Marina dock, M–F 7am–4:30pm hourly (second vessel every 30 min 4:30–7:30pm per press), ~40-ft/55-passenger vessels, "consistent travel time of just 20 minutes," "no delays from bridge openings," explicitly framed as "a scenic, sustainable and convenient alternative to driving across Biscayne Bay." The city also owns the land under **Miami Beach Marina** (South Beach stop) — leased since 1983, operated by Suntex, redevelopment under negotiation (2026). Sources: https://www.miamibeachfl.gov/city-hall/transportation/water-taxi/ · https://www.miamiherald.com/news/local/community/miami-dade/miami-beach/article314347057.html · https://therealdeal.com/miami/2026/04/22/terra-suntex-marinas-plan-miami-beach-marina-redevelopment/ · https://www.facebook.com/cityofmiamibeach/posts/1373023478205728/
- **History (honest):** water-taxi attempts failed in 2017, 2020, 2022 (secondary source); the 2024 Poseidon deal ($50K/month subsidy, no-bid) was ended amid maintenance problems before the current free service launched. Sources above.
- **Posture: OPERATE (purchase-of-service).** The clearest live Operate precedent in this market: a city paying a private operator to run scheduled water transit. It proves demand, sets the regulatory template, and defines the complement/displacement line — hub.json already commits to complementing, never displacing, this service.

## 6 · City classification: Enable vs Operate

**Dual, Enable-weighted.** Reasoning:
- The **landing layer is Enable**: county and municipal marina landlords (Miami-Dade Parks, City of Miami, City of Fort Lauderdale, City of Pompano Beach) plus private marinas grant access; no terminal construction needed anywhere in the network.
- The **Operate flavor in this market is municipal purchase-of-service, not agency fleet ownership**: Miami Beach (free water taxi via contracted operator, after a paid pilot), Fort Lauderdale (free water trolley via Water Taxi partnership), and Pompano Beach (procured Water Taxi expansion) all pay private operators to run water service. There is no WETA/MBTA-style public vessel-owning operator here, and DTPW has no verified water program.
- **Page weighting recommendation:** lead Enable (access + private capital + employer demand), with an Operate track framed as *purchase-of-service/service-contract* ("cities here already buy water transit by the month — Miami Beach, Fort Lauderdale, and Pompano Beach all do it today"), not as bus-style vessel procurement. Procurement language should stay available but secondary.

## 7 · Open flags for Jaideep

1. **The Poseidon Ferry pilot attribution needs correcting upstream:** evidence shows it was a **City of Miami Beach** subsidy deal ($50K/month, ended 2024) and the current free service is also Miami Beach's; no DTPW water pilot was found. Any copy or brief that says "Miami-Dade DTPW ran the Poseidon Ferry pilot" should be fixed.

Sequence outreach so the city hears "network extension" not "competitor."

5. **Vessel certification / USCG and state submerged-lands permitting were not researched in depth** — legal review before any pilot commitment (same standing flag as Boston).
6. **FDOT District Four (Broward) not profiled** — close this gap if the FTL cluster leads outreach.
7. **No published Miami-Dade or FDOT water-transit funding program was found** — the funding story here is federal (FTA) plus municipal purchase-of-service precedents; don't imply a state grant pipeline that isn't sourced.
