# Miami / Fort Lauderdale speed & wake rules — corridor-controlling instruments

**Date:** 2026-08-16 · **Status:** research-complete, primary-source pass (rule text read, not paraphrased from press) · **Scope:** the 16-stop / 5-line network in `miami/hub.json` (MIA-1 Island · MIA-2 Grove · MIA-3 Beach · MIA-4 North Bay · FTL-1 Fort Lauderdale). Access date for all sources: 2026-08-16.

> **Standing honesty rule for this market (supersedes the generic §4b relief pillar where they conflict):** the controlling speed regime on these corridors is overwhelmingly **manatee protection**, plus one **navigation-safety** federal RNA and one **congestion-safety** state BRA. Manatee zones exist to prevent vessel strikes on animals — not to control wake. A foiling Navier's low wake is **irrelevant to that purpose**, and strike risk from foil struts at speed is an **open, unstudied question**. **No relief case is made or implied for any manatee zone anywhere in this market. Base schedule math respects every posted zone as-is, and hub.json corridor times already reflect them.** The "your speed rules were written for boats that make wake" pillar must NOT be localized to Miami's manatee zones; if used at all here it applies only to the two non-wildlife instruments below, and even there no exemption process is claimed.

## 3 · Who sets and enforces, and the stated purposes

- **FWC** adopts and amends manatee zones under §379.2431(2), F.S. (formerly §370.12(2)); process described in Rule 68C-22.001 (see https://myfwc.com/wildlifehabitats/wildlife/manatee/rulemaking/). Purpose: preventing vessel collisions with manatees. Local governments can add ordinance zones only with FWC approval (https://myfwc.com/wildlifehabitats/wildlife/manatee/protection-zones/).
- **USCG COTP Sector Miami** enforces the Port of Miami RNA; "slow speed" there is defined by vessel attitude (fully off plane, settled, not creating excessive wake), with no numeric limit, and the rule explicitly preserves manatee-zone obligations (33 CFR 165.792(b)–(c)).
- **FWC (Nov 2025 Broward BRA)**: stated purpose is public safety amid vessel congestion — a wake argument does not serve this purpose either.

## 4 · Relief posture — stated plainly

1. **Manatee zones: no relief sought, claimed, or modeled.** The zones' purpose is animal protection. Foiling reduces wake and noise, but a foil strut moving at 25+ kn is a strike surface; no study or precedent was located suggesting hydrofoils qualify for manatee-zone exemptions, and none is asserted. Corridor times in hub.json respect these zones and must continue to.
2. **What legally exists (for completeness, not as a pitch):** FWC's rulemaking process (68C-22.001) periodically reviews county zones; 68C-22.003 provides permits for **events** (e.g., 68C-22.010(3) allows powerboat-racing permits on a defined FTL ICW stretch, Apr 1–Oct 31 only). Neither is a scheduled-transit relief mechanism. No transit-specific exemption or variance process was located in either county rule — **fail closed**.
3. **Non-wildlife instruments:** the Port of Miami RNA and the 2025 Lauderdale Beach BRA are safety/congestion rules; a low-wake argument does not serve their stated purposes, and no exemption pathway is claimed. The RNA's slow-speed definition is wake-referenced ("not creating excessive wake"), which a foiling vessel arguably satisfies in attitude terms — but the rule regulates *speed posture in a congested cruise-port channel*, and Navier will comply as written.
4. **Where Miami is actually favorable, honestly:** unlike Boston's 6-mph headway regime, large parts of Biscayne Bay's transit water are **unzoned open bay or numeric 25/30/35 MPH channels** — at or near N45 cruise speed. The network's speed advantage here comes from routing around restrictions (as hub.json already does), not from seeking relief.
