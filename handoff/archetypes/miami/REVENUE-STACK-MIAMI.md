## 1 · Frame

Same reframe as Boston (D6′, locked 2026-08-15): a commuter-only vessel monetizes ~15% of its available hours; the same N45 runs AM/PM commute peaks plus midday/evening/weekend **experiences and charters**, with **sponsorship** and **overnight cargo** as upside-only lines. No anchor tenant anywhere. Miami-specific twist: **the experiences market here is year-round and deep** (Island Queen and the charter/experience operators run multiple daily sailings twelve months a year), while **the commute seat price derives lower than Boston** because Miami's premium substitutes are cheaper (Brightline passes, $26 rideshare legs, $145–284/mo parking) and the Beach corridor's public comparator is *free*. Both effects are carried honestly below.

## 2 · Convergence check

Boston's stack landed at mid ~4.3 yr / upside ~2.1 yr against canon partner-corridor 2.49 yr. Miami's same-grammar stack lands at **mid ~6.2 yr / upside ~3.1 yr**. The gap is not a modeling choice; it is three sourced facts: (a) DERIVED seat band $750–1,050 vs Boston's approved $950–1,200; (b) loaded crew $100/hr vs Boston $85 (BLS Miami metro means are ~18% above Boston — see `CREW-COST-MIAMI.md`); (c) Miami experience/charter market prices sit below Boston's ($35–69/pp vs $49–98/pp; $450–1,500/hr charter market entered conservatively vs $550/hr Boston floor). Offsets that keep Miami financeable: a genuinely year-round L3 season (8 weekend operating days/month is a realistic *base* here, not a summer artifact) and short corridors (≈4.5 nm legs vs Boston's ~10 nm) that cut energy cost per seat-leg by more than half. **Mid (~6.2 yr) is the honest Miami headline unless Jaideep approves stronger seat pricing at the Brickell end.**

## 5 · Worked per-vessel model (N45, Miami/Fort Lauderdale)

**Honest reads:**
- **Conservative is cash-negative here (−$19K/yr)** — unlike Boston's marginally positive conservative — because the derived seat price is lower while crew costs are higher. Conservative remains deliberately punitive (10% spot fill, 12 experience sailings/month in a market where incumbents run multiples daily), but the sign matters: at the bottom of the derived price band, one Miami N45 does not carry itself on thin fills. The trigger discipline (60–80 committed seats before launch) is what keeps this scenario theoretical.
- **Mid (~6.2 yr) is the honest Miami headline** — financeable only for patient fleet capital, and visibly worse than Boston's 4.3 yr. The three sourced drivers are in §2. If Jaideep approves pricing the Brickell-anchored corridors at the Boston-style top of band ($950–1,200 — arguable on the 80–90-min causeway pain and the Citadel-tier employer base), mid-case L1 moves +$3.2–9.6K/mo and payback compresses toward ~4.5–5 yr; **that is a pricing decision, not a research finding — not taken here.**
- **Upside (~3.1 yr) needs no sponsorship/cargo heroics** — it is mostly L3 depth (44 sailings/mo is still ~1.5/day vs incumbents' multiple-daily year-round cadence) plus top-of-derived-band seats.
- Crew is the swing cost again ($170–302K/yr at full stacking) and Miami's rate is benchmark-validated ~18% above Boston's. Split-shift structure for a 16-hr stacked day remains an ops-walkthrough item, not a rate item.
- The FTL cluster inherits the same stack but with slower water (all FTL-1 legs speed-constrained) — per-corridor economics there will run below the Miami-cluster numbers above; this model is the blended single-vessel template, honest at network level only if flagship deployment leads on Biscayne Bay.
