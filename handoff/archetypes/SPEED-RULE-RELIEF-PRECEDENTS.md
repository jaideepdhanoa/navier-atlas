# Speed-rule relief precedents (Navier)

Research status: source-checked 15 Aug 2026. **Fail closed:** “unverified” means the underlying order/rule or current status was not located; press language is not treated as legal proof.

## 1. Stockholm / Candela P-12 Nova

- **Route and service:** Candela’s Nova operated SL public-transport route 89 between Tappström (Ekerö centrum) and Stockholm City Hall/Klara Mälarstrand. Region Stockholm confirms the 2024 electric hydrofoil pilot; Candela reports 120 departures and 2,326 passengers in its operating data. [Region Stockholm (Oct. 2024)](https://www.regionstockholm.se/nyheter/2024/10/tyst-snabb-och-miljovanlig-resa-med-elektrisk-barplansbat-i-stockholm/); [Candela results](https://candela.com/newsroom/the-worlds-first-flying-electric-ferry-is-a-success-stockholm-expands-service/)
- **Published relief claim (not the legal order):** Candela says the direct Tappström–City Hall trip took about 30 minutes “with speed exemption at 22 knots from Gröndalsbron to City Hall.” This is the clearest source for the reported exempted operating speed and segment. [Candela](https://candela.com/newsroom/the-worlds-first-flying-electric-ferry-is-a-success-stockholm-expands-service/)
- **Reported cap/speeds conflict:** Candela says the exemption was from Stockholm’s **12-knot** limit to **22 knots** (on the Gröndalsbron–City Hall segment). A Feb. 2026 Lighthouse final-report article quotes Candela’s Brigitte Junker saying the boat normally ran at **8 knots** and received dispensation to run at **25 knots** because it produced no wash. These may reflect different periods, segments, or rule descriptions; the controlling order was not found, so do not collapse them into one definitive cap/speed. [Candela](https://candela.com/newsroom/candela-p-12-enables-public-transport-faster-than-cars-and-subway/); [Lighthouse (16 Feb. 2026)](https://lighthouse.nu/sv/aktuellt/nyheter/rapport-elektrisk-baerplansbat-slar-konventionell-faerjetrafik-pa-alla-punkter)
- **Important source nuance:** A 2026 maritime report’s caption says Nova was foiling at 22 knots, while its text says the low-wake P-12 operated at higher city speeds “under a speed exemption”; this corroborates operation, not the formal instrument. [Maritimt](https://www.maritimt.com/stockholms-electric-flying-ferry-successful)
- **Who granted it:** The Lighthouse article quotes Candela identifying the grantor as **Länsstyrelsen** (Swedish County Administrative Board), which is strong secondary attribution. A signed decision, legal instrument, conditions, and exact county/office were not located. **Formal grant details: unverified.** Region Stockholm should not be described as the legal grantor solely because it operated/enabled the pilot. [Lighthouse](https://lighthouse.nu/sv/aktuellt/nyheter/rapport-elektrisk-baerplansbat-slar-konventionell-faerjetrafik-pa-alla-punkter)
- **Current status:** Lighthouse says Nova was tested in regular SL traffic on line 89 during 2024–25; Region Stockholm confirms the 2024 pilot, and Candela reports expansion/planned daily operations. A current, operative exemption order (expiry, conditions, and route scope) was not located. **Current legal validity: unverified.** [Lighthouse](https://lighthouse.nu/sv/aktuellt/nyheter/rapport-elektrisk-baerplansbat-slar-konventionell-faerjetrafik-pa-alla-punkter); [Region Stockholm](https://www.regionstockholm.se/nyheter/2024/10/tyst-snabb-och-miljovanlig-resa-med-elektrisk-barplansbat-i-stockholm/); [Candela](https://candela.com/newsroom/the-worlds-first-flying-electric-ferry-is-a-success-stockholm-expands-service/)

## 2. Legal basis of US harbor speed rules

### Massachusetts / Boston

- Massachusetts distinguishes outcome-based wash/wake duties from numerical or headway limits. 323 CMR 2.02 defines “headway speed” as the minimum speed needed for steerage, **not over 6 mph**. [Massachusetts regulation (Cornell rendering)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/323-CMR-2-02)
- 323 CMR 2.07 prohibits operation that throws wash into or causes excessive rocking of other vessels/rafts/floats; it also requires headway speed in listed situations (e.g., obscured vision, near marinas/floats/swimmers, and channels unless an authorized marine-enforcement agency permits otherwise and markers show the permitted speed). [323 CMR 2.07](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/323-CMR-2-07)
- The same rule separately requires a speed reasonable and proper for safety, visibility, traffic, maneuverability, weather/current, and hazards; it is not a blanket “no-wake = X mph” rule. [323 CMR 2.07(11)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/323-CMR-2-07)
- **Boston Inner Harbor:** Waterway Guide reports a Boston Harbor “no wake” area and a 6-mph figure, but I did not locate the controlling Boston Harbor Master, City, Massachusetts, or USCG order. Treat the geographic scope, 6-mph figure, and issuing authority as **secondary/unverified**, not as a legal conclusion. [Waterway Guide alert](https://www.waterwayguide.com/nav-alert/4-12151/ma-boston-harbor-no-wake-zone)
- Therefore, a low-wake hydrofoil may have a factual compliance argument under a genuinely wake/wash-based prohibition, but that does not override safe-speed, headway, channel-marker, or enforcement interpretations. A numeric cap requires permission under the rule’s stated mechanism; Massachusetts 323 CMR 2.07(3)(d) expressly contemplates an authorized marine-enforcement agency permitting otherwise in a marked channel. [323 CMR 2.07](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/323-CMR-2-07)

### Washington / Seattle / Lake Washington

- I found **no authoritative blanket “Lake Washington 8-knot” rule**. The operative federal rule located is narrower: 33 CFR 165.1341 applies to specified waters south of the I-90 bridge and is enforced around Seafair. [eCFR §165.1341](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-33/section-165.1341)
- During enforcement, all vessels must proceed at a speed creating minimum wake, **7 mph or less**, unless a higher speed is necessary to maintain bare steerageway. This is a hybrid rule: an outcome (“minimum wake”) plus a numeric ceiling, with only a steerageway exception. Low wake alone is not the stated exception. [33 CFR 165.1341(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-33/section-165.1341); [2024 USCG enforcement notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/07/31/2024-16820/regulated-navigation-area-lake-washington-seattle-wa)
- The notice identifies the Coast Guard/Captain of the Port, Sector Puget Sound, as the enforcing authority; the rule does not create a general low-wake speed variance. Relief would require the responsible federal authority to change/authorize under the particular rule or issue, not an assumed citywide exemption. [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/07/31/2024-16820/regulated-navigation-area-lake-washington-seattle-wa)

### Federal framing

- USCG Navigation Rules impose a safe-speed duty; they do not establish a nationwide fixed speed at which every “no wake” zone begins. Local/state rules and specific 33 CFR Part 165 RNAs can add wake or numeric restrictions. [USCG Navigation Rules](https://www.navcen.uscg.gov/navigation-rules-amalgamated); [33 CFR Part 165](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-33/part-165)
- Under 33 CFR 165.7, an individual limited-access/RNA notice normally states the method for obtaining authorization to enter and any special navigation rules. That is not a universal variance process for state/local limits. [33 CFR 165.7](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-33/section-165.7)

[Candela](https://candela.com/newsroom/the-worlds-first-flying-electric-ferry-is-a-success-stockholm-expands-service/); [Lighthouse](https://lighthouse.nu/sv/aktuellt/nyheter/rapport-elektrisk-baerplansbat-slar-konventionell-faerjetrafik-pa-alla-punkter)

The public page verifies deployment/operation, but does not document an authority-granted speed waiver or variance. [Fullers/Vessev](https://www.fullers.co.nz/destinations-and-experiences/experiences/ferry-experiences/electric-hydrofoil-experience/)
- **Artemis Technologies, Belfast/Bangor:** Artemis describes its 100% electric foiling ferry and Belfast-area development/deployment. The cited materials do not establish a speed-limit exemption, pilot authorization, or variance. [Artemis](https://www.artemistechnologies.co.uk/news/artemis-technologies-unveils-worlds-most-advanced-100-electric-foiling-ferry); [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c5y9y79vvdpo)
- **Bottom line on other cases:** no independently verified authority-issued speed-rule waiver for Vessev/Auckland, Artemis/Belfast, or a US Candela deployment was located in this pass. Do not present deployment, testing, or operator approval as legal speed relief.

## Implications for Navier

1. **Wake-based rule:** where the controlling text prohibits causing a specified wash/wake, a low-wake foil can argue compliance at speed, backed by independent wake measurements; safe-speed and enforcement judgments still apply. [323 CMR 2.07](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/323-CMR-2-07)
2. **Speed-based rule:** a low wake does not itself authorize exceeding a numeric cap. Obtain explicit relief from the authority named in the controlling rule/order (in Massachusetts, use the permitted-and-marked channel mechanism where applicable; in a federal RNA, engage the COTP/USCG under that instrument). [33 CFR 165.1341](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-33/section-165.1341); [33 CFR 165.7](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-33/section-165.7)
3. **Engagement/pilot pathway:** map the exact corridor and rulemaker first; request a route-specific pilot/authorization; submit measured wake, maneuvering/stop performance, traffic interaction, safety case, operating profile, and monitoring/enforcement plan. Treat operator procurement or a press release as insufficient proof of legal relief. [33 CFR 165.7](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-33/section-165.7)
