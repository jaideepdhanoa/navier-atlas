## Governing landscape (who controls what)

### 1. Transport General Authority (TGA / الهيئة العامة للنقل) — THE licensing authority
- Mandate: "The Transport General Authority is responsible for regulating and developing the land, rail, and maritime transport sectors in Saudi Arabia." — tga.gov.sa/en [PRIMARY] (site geo-blocks non-KSA scrapers; wording captured via search index of the primary domain)
- National portal confirmation: "TGA is the regulatory and legislative body for the rail, maritime, and land transport industries in the Kingdom… by licensing rail, land, and [maritime transport activities]." — https://my.gov.sa/en/content/transport [PRIMARY]
- Specific services relevant to Navier:
  - "Navigation Permit and Operating License (for Ships and Marine Units)" — "a certificate confirming the vessel's seaworthiness and compliance with local and international standards for maritime safety and environmental [protection]" — https://www.tga.gov.sa/en/activities-services/get-content?categoryId=10 [PRIMARY]
  - "Issuance of Operation License for a marine unit" — https://my.gov.sa/en/services/19472 [PRIMARY]
  - Marine vessels & units services hub (ship registration, navigational licenses, maritime safety standards): https://tga.gov.sa/en/activities-services/activities/10 [PRIMARY]
- Posture fit: Enable. TGA licenses operators; it does not operate. A passenger-transport operating license + vessel registration/navigation permits for a novel foiling class is the central regulatory workstream.
- No published TGA marine passenger-transit initiative for the Gulf coast was found [UNSOURCED — recorded as absent, treat EP as regulatory greenfield].

### 2. Saudi Border Guard (General Directorate of Border Guard, MOI) — operational gatekeeper on the water
Regulation: "Regulations of security and safety for those involved in Maritime activities in the Marine Areas in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia" (Ministerial; English text via Qanoniah legal database) — https://qanoniah.com/en/File/DmWqyAxNgpoPOjpnP26zvBXaE-... [PRIMARY-TEXT via legal database]
Key provisions verified in text:
- "Border Guard … is the authority concerned with implementing the requirements of maritime security and safety."
- Border Guard is in charge of: "A. Determining and identifying the places permitted for marine boat/vessels launching and dry-docking…" and "B. Determining and identifying the navigational/shipping lanes for marine boats/vessels, THEIR SPEEDS, departure and return times, and the duration of sailing." (emphasis ours — speed control on coastal lanes sits with Border Guard, not a published code)
- Border Guard issues/approves boat driving licenses, sailing permits, launching/dry-docking permits; searches and inspects boats at sail, return, and at sea.
- Territorial sea = 12 nm from baselines; contiguous zone a further 12 nm (same text).
- SAR coordination: Dammam Search and Rescue Coordination Center (DMRCC) covers the Gulf coast.
- Posture fit: Enable (permits + lane/speed determinations). Every corridor, timetable, and landing needs Border Guard concurrence. This is the single most operationally binding authority for a greenfield service.

### 3. Mawani — Saudi Ports Authority (King Abdulaziz Port, Dammam)
- "Saudi Ports Authority (Mawani) was established in 1976 … constructing and managing Saudi ports." — mawani.gov.sa / IAPH member page [PRIMARY/SECONDARY]
- King Abdulaziz Port (KAAP), Dammam: the Kingdom's main Gulf port, 26.48861°N 50.20111°E, 39 berths — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdulaziz_Port [SECONDARY]
- Cruise Saudi (PIF): "Cruise Saudi developed the infrastructure and cruise facilities for King Abdulaziz Port to become Saudi's cruise gateway on the Arabian Gulf." — https://www.cruisesaudi.com/ports/z6b8woorp6zxsd3skp0qy8jp [PRIMARY]
- Second cruise terminal + four new berths planned at KAAP — maritime-executive.com [SECONDARY]
- Posture fit: Enable (berthing rights inside port limits; harbourmaster control of port waters). A corniche network only touches Mawani where it calls at the cruise terminal.

### 4. Eastern Province Municipality (Amanat al-Mintaqah al-Sharqiyah) — corniche & waterfront landlord

### 8. Ministry of Environment, Water and Agriculture (MEWA)

## Boundary verification: Saudi Red Sea Authority (SRSA) does NOT govern the Gulf coast
- SRSA is "an enabler and regulator of marine and navigational tourism activities within the geographical scope of Saudi Arabia's RED SEA" (est. 30 Nov 2021) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Red_Sea_Authority [SECONDARY, quoting founding decision]
- Its seven 2023 regulations self-scope to the Red Sea, e.g. Private Visiting Yachts: "regulate the entry and exit of private foreign yachts visiting the Saudi territorial waters ON THE RED SEA COAST"; Large Yacht Chartering: "chartered in Saudi territorial waters on the Red Sea coast" — regulation texts archived from redsea.gov.sa (links in the Wikipedia article; redsea.gov.sa geo-blocked from research environment) [PRIMARY-TEXT via archive]
- Conclusion: on the Arabian Gulf coast the applicable regime is TGA (vessel + operator licensing) + Border Guard (security/safety, lanes, speeds, permits) + Mawani (port waters) + Amanah/SDA (landings). SRSA's marine-tourism licensing regime is a Red-Sea-only template — useful precedent to cite, not applicable law in EP. [VERIFIED BOUNDARY]
