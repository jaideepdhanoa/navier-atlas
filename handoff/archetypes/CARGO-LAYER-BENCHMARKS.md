# Boston Harbor cargo-layer benchmarks — cited market rates

**Research date:** 15 August 2026.  **Scope:** real, publicly available tariffs and published provider benchmarks relevant to an off-peak cargo layer on a 45-ft / ~2,000-kg-payload electric passenger ferry. Prices below are **benchmarks, not a Navier quote or forecast**. A ferry crossing is only one part of an end-to-end shipment; pickup, terminal handling, last-mile, cold chain, security, and labor may be material extra costs.

## Confidence key

* **Published tariff** — operator/government tariff or public rate card with an effective date.
* **Industry survey** — a provider’s stated aggregate/internal benchmark with methodology, not an individual customer quote.
* **Reported** — credible published description of a live service/volume but no public price.
* **Weak** — vendor content, unverified market commentary, or non-comparable rate; do not use as an underwriting anchor without quotes.

### Boston market availability — capability, not rate evidence

Boston has local 24/7 medical/STAT supply (for example, Allstate Courier Systems, Couriers Boston, Bocsit, and Boston Courier advertise medical/specimen or STAT service), but the reviewed Boston provider pages require a quote and publish **no usable price schedule**. They are evidence of a local competitive market, not evidence of a price.

* Allstate Courier Systems: https://allstatecouriers.com/
* Couriers Boston medical service: https://www.couriersboston.com/services/medical
* Bocsit Boston: https://bocsit.com/
* Boston Courier: https://www.boscourier.com/

### Fit / operating requirements

Medical cargo is potentially the best value-density fit, but it is **not simply “small boxes on an idle boat.”** Dropoff identifies temperature stability, HIPAA/PHI, BBP training, digital chain-of-custody, timing SLAs and audited custody as cost drivers. Its posted typical add-ons are **after-hours $20–35**, **temperature control $15–45**, **weekend/holiday 1.5×**, and **wait time $1.25–2.25/min**. A water leg must preserve the customer’s collection window and chain of custody; a long terminal handoff or weather cancellation can erase the clinical value proposition. See also CDC specimen handling guidance linked from the source.

**Practical benchmark:** For an initial economic sensitivity, treat **$60–115 per scheduled 28-mi lab route** and **$90–160 per sub-10-mi STAT job** as the strongest public healthcare price anchors found, explicitly tagged as a national provider’s own benchmark, not a Boston contract rate. Do **not** turn these per-job figures into $/kg: typical specimen weights are tiny and the rate pays for urgency/compliance, not payload.

## 4) Short-sea and passenger-ferry freight tariffs — strongest waterborne evidence

### A. Casco Bay Lines / Casco Bay Island Transit District (Portland, Maine)

| Grocery cartons by certified weight (Item 300I) | **$2.50 / $3.75 per 100 lb** | **$0.055 / $0.083 per kg**; 2,000 kg arithmetic = **$110 / $165**. Commodity-specific, hence not a general cargo price. | **Published tariff** |

Important constraints in the same tariff: it excludes pickup/delivery; freight is space-available; carrier does not guarantee a particular vessel/trip; palletized goods must be shrink-wrapped, <6 ft high and **≤2,000 lb**; refrigerated/frozen rates **do not obligate the carrier to provide refrigeration/heating**; dangerous goods require Coast Guard-compliant handling. This is unusually close to the proposed vessel’s 2,000-kg payload concept, but Casco’s route length, vessel/terminal configuration, subsidy context, commodity mix, and end-to-end scope differ sharply from Boston Harbor.

## 5) Seafood / fresh-catch logistics — rate evidence and fit decision

### What is actually supportable

* **Casco Bay (2026)** publishes fresh/frozen seafood/fish at **$2.50 off-peak / $3.75 peak per box** (Item 300M), but provides no box weight. It is a real waterborne seafood handling tariff, **not** a Boston dock-to-market price per kg. [Tariff](https://www.cascobaylines.com/uploads/Att_5_Tariff_effective_2_28_26-1-1.pdf) — **Published tariff**.
* **Steamship Authority (2026)** provides up to a **50% vehicle-fare discount** for qualifying trucks carrying at least **500 lb** fresh island-produced fish/shellfish (and certain produce). [Tariff](https://www-steamship-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/versioned_downloadable_forms/path/tariff_freight_mdpu_no._f-120_replaces_mdpu_no._f-118.pdf) — **Published tariff**.
* **Svenfish (New England DTC retailer; page accessed 2026)** advertises overnight UPS shipping in its region, including a **$10 flat rate / $50 threshold**. That is a consumer subsidy/promotion, not a freight-market rate or a seafood linehaul price; retain only as **Weak** contextual evidence: https://svenfish.com/pages/shipping-delivery.

### What this means for Boston Harbor

No defensible, public Boston/New Bedford/Gloucester fresh-seafood **carrier rate per kg** was located. New Bedford’s port describes itself as handling “millions of pounds” in the supply chain, but not transportation prices: https://portofnewbedford.org/doing-business/.

Seafood is a poor first fit for a **premium passenger cabin** unless the vessel carries sealed, washable, odor-contained insulated totes and has a formal cleaning/spill process. The operational source is especially clear:

* Casco requires seafood/frozen items separated from dry groceries for expediting and says its tariff does not provide refrigeration.
* It requires proper packaging and warns freight may face rough seas/weather; it caps standard pallets at 2,000 lb.
* Steamship Authority requires shipments that could be objectionable or damage cargo/vessel to be pre-booked and can refuse them; its solid-waste terms specifically require containment to prevent leakage/minimize odor, an analogous passenger-experience concern.

**Decision implication:** seafood has demonstrated waterborne demand and tariff precedent, but absent a temperature-controlled, segregated cargo hold and dock handling it creates odor, drip, sanitation, and passenger-brand risk. It should be a later, specialized product rather than the first overnight revenue layer.

## What did **not** survive scrutiny

1. **“Boston medical courier rate” as a public local tariff.** Local providers were identified, but none reviewed published a Boston STAT, scheduled-route, per-mile, or per-kg price. Do not attribute Dropoff’s national ranges to MedSpeed, Lab Logistics, USPack, or Boston providers.
2. **MedSpeed, Lab Logistics and USPack price cards.** These named healthcare logistics companies are relevant competitors/capability references, but no citable public Boston pricing was found in this research. Their prices should be quote-requested, not assumed.
3. **DHL Amsterdam canal boat $/parcel economics.** Frequently repeated water-logistics claims could not be tied to a primary DHL source with a current public rate. Excluded as a pricing anchor.
4. **NYC Blue Highways $/parcel.** The live Dec. 2025 pilot publishes 300–400 parcels/day but no price, subsidy, revenue, vessel capacity utilization, or unit cost. Do not infer one.
5. **Paris/London/Venice urban river freight $/parcel or $/pallet.** No primary, public, comparable price survived this pass.
6. **Boston/New Bedford/Gloucester seafood $/kg carrier rate.** No credible public lane rate was found. The available ferry seafood tariffs are per box or discounted vehicle fare, without a standardized kg conversion.
7. **Generic web “industry averages” (including undated blogs and marketplace commentary).** Not used as core anchors where a dated operator tariff/provider methodology exists.

### Bottom-line pricing implication

The cleanest public **waterborne base-rate** comparator is Casco’s $0.108–0.162/kg generic freight tariff, yielding only about **$216–324 per fully loaded 2,000-kg one-way sailing** before collection, loading, terminal, last-mile, compliance, and vessel-operating costs. That is a useful low-value consolidated-freight reference, not a suggested price. The more promising revenue layer is a limited number of **scheduled, high-value B2B jobs** (medical/pharma or clean event/urgent-parts flows) priced to service level, while recognizing that a vessel’s water leg must be integrated into a compliant pickup/last-mile network. Seafood should be excluded from a premium passenger cabin until segregated cold-chain/odor/cleaning operations are designed and priced.
