## 2 · Per-authority profiles

### WSDOT / Washington State Ferries — **OPERATE (the anchor operator), ENABLE at co-located terminals**
- **Mandate:** state ferry system — the largest in the US; **19.1M riders in 2024 (+2.6%), walk-ons +5.7%**; Seattle/Bainbridge was the busiest route at **4.9M total riders**, Edmonds/Kingston second at 3.7M. Source: https://wsdot.wa.gov/about/news/2025/back-board-2024-brought-half-million-more-state-ferry-riders
- **Controls:** Colman Dock (Seattle), Bainbridge (Eagle Harbor) and Edmonds/Kingston auto-ferry terminals; also owns **Pier 48**, which Kitsap Transit selected as its preferred new Seattle passenger-only terminal site. Sources: https://www.ktheadways.com/blog/kitsap-transit-pier-48-fast-ferry-terminal-seattle · https://wsdot.wa.gov/travel/washington-state-ferries
- **Published plans:** WSF 2040 Long Range Plan (https://wsdot.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2021-10/WSF-LongRangePlan-2040Plan.pdf — passenger-only-ferry partnership language **not quote-verified this pass**; pull before citing verbatim) and the **System Electrification Program** (https://wsdot.wa.gov/construction-planning/major-projects/ferry-system-electrification) — the state's flagship maritime-decarbonization commitment; secondary coverage says roughly half of 2025 Climate Commitment Act transportation spending went to WSF electrification (https://www.thecentersquare.com/washington/article_ae7a9398-30ca-48c8-a819-39d666ac0838.html — secondary).
- **Posture:** OPERATE at system scale; for Navier the practical relationship is **complement-and-coexist**: WSF is capacity-constrained, walk-on demand is its fastest-growing segment, and its own terminals anchor two of our stops' catchments. Never frame as competitor.

### King County Metro — Marine Division (King County Water Taxi) — **OPERATE (the county precedent)**
- **Mandate/controls:** operates the two-route passenger-only Water Taxi (West Seattle 10–15 min; Vashon ~22 min) from **Pier 50** (two slips, shared with Kitsap) and the **Seacrest Park dock — our SND-1 West Seattle stop is their terminal** (Seattle Parks facility, KC Metro operation). Sources: https://kingcounty.gov/en/dept/metro/travel-options/water-taxi · Wikipedia operator detail: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_County_Water_Taxi
- **Fares:** West Seattle $5.25 ORCA / $6.25 cash; Vashon $6.00 / $7.00. Sources: https://kingcounty.gov/en/dept/metro/travel-options/water-taxi/west-seattle · .../vashon
- **Demand proof:** Vashon route ridership jumped **55% in H2 2024** (69,470 vs 44,945) almost entirely from new **midday** sailings — latent all-day demand, directly supporting utilization-stack logic. Source: https://www.vashonbeachcomber.com/2025/01/14/water-taxi-surges-as-advocates-plan-for-legislative-session/
- **Posture: OPERATE**, with expansion studies on record (Ballard etc. — secondary references; not quote-verified). Pier 50 slip scarcity (see Kitsap below) is the structural reason our downtown gate is **Bell Harbor, not Pier 50** — matches hub.json's decision ledger.

### Kitsap Transit (Kitsap Fast Ferries) — **OPERATE (the strongest US fast-ferry story, and the best hydrofoil hook in the country)**
- **Mandate:** sales-tax-funded transit authority (local sales tax + fares fund operations; state/federal grants fund capital — https://www.kitsaptransit.com/agency-information/funding) running Bremerton/Kingston/Southworth ↔ Seattle fast ferries since 2017/2018/2021.
- **Ridership:** record **1.22M riders in 2024** (https://www.ktheadways.com/blog/kitsap-ferries-carried-122-million-riders-in-2024); 1,187,800 in 2025 (APTA Q4 2025 via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsap_Fast_Ferries).
- **Fares:** $2 eastbound / $13 westbound; monthly fast-ferry pass **$210** (effective Oct 1, 2025). Source: https://www.kitsaptransit.com/fares
- **Hydrofoil procurement precedent (the headline):** Kitsap Transit is running an FTA-funded research program to test an **electric hydrofoil ferry** on the Bremerton route; in 2024 the state contributed **$5.2M toward design of a 150-passenger hydrofoil**, with a 20-passenger prototype phase still seeking ~$15M as of 2025. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsap_Fast_Ferries (secondary — pull Kitsap board/WA budget docs before external use). A US transit agency is already trying to buy what Navier builds.
- **Wake-relief-by-design precedent:** *Rich Passage 1* (see SPEED-RULES file §4).
- **Terminal expansion:** selected WSDOT's **Pier 48** as preferred Seattle terminal because Pier 50's two slips are saturated — independent confirmation that downtown passenger-dock capacity is the scarce asset. Source: https://www.ktheadways.com/blog/kitsap-transit-pier-48-fast-ferry-terminal-seattle
- **Posture: OPERATE.** Kingston stop overlap: our SND-2 Kingston stop is a Kitsap Fast Ferries market (~40-min crossing, https://www.kitsaptransit.com/service/fast-ferry/kingston-fast-ferry) — positioning must be complementary (different landing pair: Kingston↔Edmonds/Bell Harbor overlay, premium seats), never a subsidized-fare comparison.

### PSRC — **ENABLE (regional planning)**
- MPO for the four-county region; ran the **Puget Sound Passenger-Only Ferry Study** assessing new POF routes region-wide. Source: https://www.psrc.org/our-work/puget-sound-passenger-only-ferry-study. The hub.json decision ledger already cites the PSRC record on Tacoma↔Seattle run-times needing a larger vessel class. Engagement value: the standing regional framework into which a private, demand-gated network slots without asking for subsidy.

### Port of Seattle — **ENABLE (our downtown landlord candidate)**
- **Mandate:** port authority (aviation + maritime); owns **Bell Harbor Marina, the only marina in downtown Seattle** — our SND-1/SND-2 downtown gate. Guest moorage is published and year-round: **$2.15–2.64/ft/day under 50′** (peak Sun–Thu $2.50/ft/day; Fri–Sat $2.64), power $7.14–28.56/day. Sources: https://www.portseattle.org/maritime/bell-harbor-marina · https://www.portseattle.org/page/bell-harbor-marina-moorage-rates-and-info
- **Published plans (verified quotes):** Century Agenda — **Objective 7: "Advance maritime industries through innovation, strategic investment and capable management of Port facilities"; Objective 11: "Reduce air pollutants and carbon emissions."** Source: https://www.portseattle.org/page/century-agenda-strategic-objectives. Also a signed MOU with Washington Maritime Blue "to accelerate sustainable blue economy" innovation: https://www.portseattle.org/news/maritime-blue-and-port-seattle-sign-mou-accelerate-sustainable-blue-economy
- **Posture: ENABLE.** A commercial berthing/landing relationship at a published-rate public marina, aligned with the Port's own innovation and emissions objectives. (Commercial-operation terms at Bell Harbor beyond guest moorage — **unverified**; a moorage agreement conversation is the actual unlock.)

### City of Seattle (SDOT · Seattle Parks · SPD Harbor Patrol) — **ENABLE**
- SDOT owns the **Seattle Transportation Plan** (adopted 2024: https://www.seattle.gov/transportation/about-us/seattle-transportation-plan — water/maritime language **not quote-verified this pass**; pull the plan PDF before citing). ### City of Des Moines — **ENABLE, demonstrated ferry appetite (paid for its own pilot)**
- City-owned 840-slip marina; ran the **2022 Des Moines↔Bell Harbor passenger-ferry pilot** itself: council approved Aug 10–Oct 9, 2022 service on the 63-passenger *Chilkat Express* (Puget Sound Express), **$87,500/month contract**, ~40-minute crossing — exactly our SND-2 Des Moines geometry (marina-to-marina). Sources: https://waterlandblog.com/des-moines-council-approves-passenger-ferry-pilot-project-to-seattle-launches-aug-10/ · https://b-townblog.com/des-moines-fast-ferry-pilot-project-ends-may-return-in-the-spring/ · final report: https://jcharrisfordesmoines.com/wp-content/uploads/Des-Moines-Demonstration-Project-FInal-Report-120122-GAD.pdf (PDF not parsed this pass — ridership totals **unverified**; council-critic context in sources).
- **Posture: ENABLE-plus** — a city that has already spent general funds to prove the corridor; the honest pitch is "your pilot, without your subsidy" (demand-gated private seats).

### Port of Kingston — **ENABLE, with a cautionary history to respect**
- Owns the Kingston marina/dock (https://portofkingston.org/moorage-amenities/). Ran its own **SoundRunner** Kingston↔Seattle passenger ferry (2010–2012), which failed financially; the port sold its last SoundRunner vessel in 2018 for $250K. Source: https://www.kitsapsun.com/story/news/local/2018/01/14/port-kingston-sells-kingston-express-last-soundrunner-ferries/1030531001/
- **Posture: ENABLE.** The port has operated and been burned; Kitsap Transit now serves Kingston successfully. Pitch must be landing-access + private capital, explicitly *not* asking the port to underwrite service.

## 6 · Open flags for Jaideep

1. **Enable-vs-Operate classification: Seattle is genuinely DUAL, tilted Operate.** This market has *three* public water-transit operators (WSF, KC Metro Marine, Kitsap Transit) — the deepest Operate bench of any city in the program — plus port landlords (Seattle/Kingston/Edmonds) and cities as enablers. Weight the page accordingly: Operate track is credible here in a way it is not in Boston.
2. **Kitsap's hydrofoil program is the single highest-leverage fact in the market** — a sales-tax-funded US transit agency with FTA research money and a $5.2M state design appropriation for a 150-passenger hydrofoil, still needing ~$15M for its prototype phase. Recommend a direct Kitsap Transit conversation before the microsite ships; the N45/N30 may be a faster path to their stated goal than a clean-sheet design.
3. **Downtown dock scarcity is confirmed and is our moat-and-risk:** Pier 50's two slips are saturated (Kitsap is buying its own terminal at Pier 48). Bell Harbor is the right gate; lock the Port of Seattle relationship early.
4. **Des Moines already paid $87.5K/month for exactly our corridor** — strongest municipal demand signal on the map; parse the final-report PDF for ridership before quoting outcomes.
5. **Three landing verifications open** (Bainbridge berth, Gig Harbor document level, Tacoma commercial terms) — all Phase 2/3 stops; close before those phases render.
6. **Never** present the network as a WSF/KCWT/Kitsap replacement or run subsidized-fare comparisons; all three are potential partners and the microsite's honesty gates (hub.json `two_networks`, complement language) already encode this.
7. **Quote-verification debt:** WSF 2040 LRP and Seattle Transportation Plan water-transport language cited at page level only — pull PDFs before any in-page quotation.
