# Eastside Lake Washington municipal speed/wake rules — primary-source verification pass

**Date:** 2026-08-16 · **Status:** research complete for Bellevue, Kirkland, Renton, Mercer Island · **Scope:** this pass verifies (or flags UNVERIFIED) the controlling municipal code sections for the four Eastside jurisdictions a ~20-passenger low-wake hydrofoil ferry route would touch — Meydenbauer Bay (Bellevue), Kirkland Marina Park / Carillon Point (Kirkland), Gene Coulon Memorial Beach Park / Southport (Renton), and mid-lake transit of Mercer Island's waters.

**Already verified (not re-checked this pass, per brief):**
- SMC 16.20.130.A = 7 kn within 100 yd of shore, Lake Washington (Seattle waters)
- KCC 12.44.090 = 8 mph within 100 yd of shore, King County waters of Lake Washington
- 33 CFR 165.1341 = seasonal Seafair minimum-wake Regulated Navigation Area, south of the I-90 bridge, enforced only around the Seafair event
- WA state layer = RCW 79A.60.030 / WAC 352-60 safe-speed standard; no numeric lake-wide cap

**Fail-closed rule applied here:** every operative-rule quote below carries a citation to a codepublishing.com / municode.com / municipal.codes (General Code's hosting platform) URL, or is explicitly marked UNVERIFIED with the reason. No blogs, real-estate sites, or Wikipedia were used as verification for any rule text; where only a city's own PDF/webpage (not a code-host) was findable, that is flagged as a lesser tier of primary evidence and called out explicitly.

The only speed-related enactment found in Bellevue's ordinance archive is **Ordinance No. 540** ("Setting Speed Limits for Boats on Water," adopted 7/24/1962), whose disposition on the official code host is explicitly listed as **"Not codified."** (https://bellevue.municipal.codes/enactments/Ord540). **(a) Buffer vs. broader regulation:** Bellevue regulates only within a shore/dock/swimming-area buffer (300 ft = 100 yd), not more broadly — consistent with the other three jurisdictions.
**(b) Marina cap relevant to a stop at Meydenbauer Bay:** none beyond the general 7-kn buffer rule; no wake-specific or lower marina-approach speed found (contrast with Seattle's SMC 16.20.130.F 3-kn marina-breakwater rule — no Bellevue analog located).
**(c) Enforcement:** Bellevue Parks & Community Services (facility rules) and Bellevue Police; on-water marine patrol in this stretch of the lake is provided under contract by the **Mercer Island Police Department Marine Patrol Unit**, which the department's own page states serves "Mercer Island and the cities of Bellevue, Renton, Medina, and the Towns of Hunts Point, and Yarrow Point" (https://www.mercerisland.gov/police/page/marine-patrol).

## 2 · Kirkland (Kirkland Marina Park / Carillon Point)

| Rule | Operative text (quote) | Scope | Citation / URL | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| KMC 14.24.030 — Speed regulations on waters within city jurisdiction | "It is unlawful for any person to operate any watercraft or vessel at a speed in excess of seven nautical miles per hour within one hundred yards of any shoreline, pier, restricted area or shore installation, except as provided in Section 14.20.030 [water skiing]." | Shore/pier buffer only — 100 yd, 7 kn. No lake-wide cap. | Kirkland Municipal Code (KMC), current through Ord. 4595 §1, 2017; Ord. 2052 §1, 1969; Ord. 800 §7, 1960: https://kirkland.municipal.codes/KMC/14.24.030 | **VERIFIED** — primary source, General Code host, section-numbered |
| KMC 14.24.020 — Reckless operation | "...Nor shall any such person operate any watercraft at a rate of speed which causes waves to damage docks, wharves, bulkheads, or watercraft moored to docks or wharves in the Kirkland Harbor." | Applies harbor-wide (not just the 100-yd buffer) as a wake-damage/reckless-operation standard, not a numeric cap | https://kirkland.municipal.codes/KMC/14.24.020 | VERIFIED |

**(a) Buffer vs. broader:** Kirkland regulates a numeric 7-kn cap only within the 100-yd shore/pier buffer; outside that buffer, only the negligent/reckless-operation standard (KMC 14.24.010/.020) applies — no numeric harbor-wide cap.
**(b) Marina/pier cap relevant to Kirkland Marina Park / Carillon Point:** both are piers/shore installations, so the general 7-kn/100-yd rule applies at those stops; no separate, lower marina-specific numeric cap was found in KMC Title 14 (unlike Seattle's marina-breakwater 3-kn rule).
**(c) Enforcement:** the "port warden" (Kirkland Police Chief or designee) under KMC 14.12.010/14.48.030, which also allows the City to contract enforcement to the King County Sheriff's Office: https://kirkland.municipal.codes/KMC/14.48.030 (via Ord. 4406, 2013 recodification, confirmed from the ordinance text at https://docs.kirklandwa.gov/CMWebDrawer/Record/192435/File/document).

**Note on the internal "RMC 9-3-7" reference for Kirkland:** that citation format is Renton's numbering scheme (RMC = Renton Municipal Code), not Kirkland's (KMC). The prior internal note conflated the two cities' harbor-code section numbers — Kirkland's equivalent and functionally identical provision is **KMC 14.24.030**, not "RMC 9-3-7." (Renton's actual 9-3-7 is below in §3, and it sets 8 kn, not 7 kn — a materially different number, so this is not a harmless renumbering.)

## 3 · Renton (Gene Coulon Memorial Beach Park / Southport)

| Rule | Operative text (quote) | Scope | Citation / URL | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RMC 9-3-7.A — Speed Regulations | "Within the harbor limits of the City of Renton it shall be unlawful for any person to operate any watercraft or vessel at a speed in excess of eight (8) nautical miles per hour within one hundred (100) yards of any shoreline, pier, restricted area or shore installation. The operation of a vessel with a designed planing hull while being operated on a plane shall be prima facie evidence of speed in excess of eight (8) miles per hour." | Shore/pier buffer only — 100 yd, **8 kn (not 7 kn)** | Renton Municipal Code, Ch. 9-3 Harbor Regulations, current through Ord. 6192 (2026), text at Ord. 4987 (10-21-02): https://www.codepublishing.com/WA/Renton/html/Renton09/Renton0903.html#9-3-7 | **VERIFIED** — primary source, codepublishing.com host, section-numbered |
| RMC 9-3-7.B — wake ban | "It shall be unlawful for any watercraft to create a wake within one hundred (100) yards of any shoreline or bridge, or shore installation." | Same 100-yd buffer, plus explicitly **bridges** — relevant given Gene Coulon Park sits near the I-405 crossing at the south end of the lake | Same citation as above | VERIFIED |
| RMC 9-3-1 / 9-3-4 — Authorization & jurisdiction | Renton "assumes control and jurisdiction over all waters within its limits" ("the Harbor"), applicable to "all vessels and watercraft operating in the harbor of this City," supplementing (not overriding) federal/state law | Renton's portion of Lake Washington generally | https://www.codepublishing.com/WA/Renton/html/Renton09/Renton0903.html#9-3-1 | VERIFIED |

**(a) Buffer vs. broader:** 8 kn within 100 yd of shore/pier/restricted area/shore installation; the wake ban extends the same 100-yd buffer to bridges specifically. No numeric cap beyond the buffer — RMC 9-3-5 (negligent operation) is the standard for open water.
**(b) Marina/park cap relevant to Gene Coulon / Southport:** the general 8-kn/100-yd + no-wake rule applies at these docks; no additional, lower numeric cap specific to Gene Coulon Park was found.
**(c) Enforcement:** Renton Police Department (RMC 9-3-3, 9-3-53); violations are decriminalized to civil infractions except specified misdemeanors/gross misdemeanors (RMC 9-3-60 through 9-3-62).

**Correction to the internal note:** RMC 9-3-7 is confirmed as the correct, current Renton section — but the operative number is **8 nautical miles per hour**, not 7. This is one knot higher than Seattle's (SMC 16.20.130.A, 7 kn) and Kirkland's (KMC 14.24.030, 7 kn) shore-buffer speed, and matches KCC 12.44.090's 8-mph figure (though "8 nautical miles per hour" and "8 statute miles per hour" are not numerically identical — 8 kn ≈ 9.2 mph — so Renton's own code is internally using "nautical miles per hour" as a synonym for knots, distinct from KCC's plain "8 miles per hour." For hydrofoil-ferry planning, treat Renton's cap as **8 kn (≈9.2 mph)**, marginally higher than Seattle's/Kirkland's 7 kn, within the 100-yd buffer.)

## 5 · Sanity check — numeric cap on the open mid-lake crossing (SR-520 to I-90, outside 100-yd/300-ft shore buffers, outside Seafair windows)?

**Answer: CONFIRMED — no numeric speed cap applies.** Evidence, all consistent:

- Seattle: SMC 16.20.130.A caps 7 kn only "within 100 yards" of shore/pier/installation on Lake Washington (already-verified context).
- King County: KCC 12.44.090 caps 8 mph only "within 100 yards" of shore/pier/installation (already-verified context).
- Kirkland: KMC 14.24.030 caps 7 kn only "within one hundred yards" of shore/pier/restricted area/shore installation (verified this pass, §2 above).
- Renton: RMC 9-3-7.A/.B cap 8 kn / ban wake only "within one hundred (100) yards" of shore/pier/restricted area/shore installation or bridge (verified this pass, §3 above).
- Mercer Island's own police department (via the Yarrow Point notice, §4 above) states explicitly: "Outside of these speed buoys, there is no speed limit. You may go as fast as conditions allow without being negligent or reckless to others."
- 33 CFR 165.1341's minimum-wake RNA (already-verified context) is seasonal/event-triggered (Seafair enforcement window only), not a standing mid-lake rule.
- The only standing mid-lake standard is the state negligent-operation rule, RCW 79A.60.030 (already-verified context) — a conduct standard, not a numeric cap.

No jurisdiction reviewed imposes a numeric speed cap on the open mid-lake crossing between the SR-520 and I-90 bridges outside the 100-yd/300-ft shore buffers and outside the seasonal Seafair RNA window.
