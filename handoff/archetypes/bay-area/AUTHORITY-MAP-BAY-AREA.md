## 2 · Per-authority profiles

### WETA / SF Bay Ferry — the flagship relationship (named Navier long-term public partner per existing canon)
- **Mandate:** regional public transit agency created by SB 976 (2007, as amended by SB 1093) to operate and expand Bay Area ferry service and coordinate water-transit emergency response; effective Jan 1 2008 it replaced the prior WTA and consolidated existing city services. Sources: http://leginfo.ca.gov/pub/07-08/bill/sen/sb_0951-1000/sb_976_cfa_20070911_203911_sen_floor.html · https://d262kwcyxl71on.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/20241024104225/FY-2025-2027-Title-VI-Program.pdf · https://sanfranciscobayferry.com/about-us/
- **Controls:** vessels, service contracts, and terminal assets across the network — currently operates in Alameda, Oakland, Richmond, South San Francisco and Vallejo (https://sanfranciscobayferry.com/our-ferry-future/). Built the $20M Richmond terminal at Ford Point (https://sanfranciscobayferry.com/project/richmond-ferry-terminal/); assumed the SF–Oakland/Alameda, SF–Harbor Bay and Vallejo Baylink services from prior operators (https://www.facebook.com/CityofSouthSanFrancisco/posts/1275760124587362/ — secondary; service history also at https://sanfranciscobayferry.com/our-history/).
- **Published plans (their own words):** the **2050 Service Vision and Expansion Policy** "sets standards for enhancement and expansion of existing ferry service… we aim to grow the existing service into more communities in the East Bay, South Bay, San Francisco and along the Carquinez Strait" (https://sanfranciscobayferry.com/our-ferry-future/ · adoption: https://sanfranciscobayferry.com/sf-bay-ferry-commits-to-major-enhancement-expansion-and-electrification-of-regional-water-transit/). Current expansion projects named on that page: **Mission Bay Ferry Landing, Berkeley Pier/Ferry Study, Redwood City Ferry Study** — three of which are hub.json stations. The **REEF program** (Rapid Electric Emission-Free Ferry) is electrifying floats and building "the nation's first high-speed, high-capacity zero-emission vessels," supported by a **$55M EPA Clean Ports grant** (same page; UCSF corroboration: https://www.ucsf.edu/news/2025/01/429306/ferry-service-mission-bay-when-youll-benefit-new-commute-option — all-electric Mission Bay service targeted 2027).
- **Posture: OPERATE (with ENABLE elements).** WETA owns vessels, procures electric newbuilds, and runs routes — the strongest OPERATE precedent in any US market this program has mapped, and it is actively electrifying. It is simultaneously an Enable-side partner where others own landings (Port of SF at Mission Bay/Downtown Terminal, Port of Redwood City). Alignment note: WETA's own expansion map (Carquinez Strait, South Bay, East Bay) overlaps BA-1 and BA-5 geography — the network extends, and never contradicts, their published direction.

### Golden Gate Bridge, Highway and Transportation District (GGBHTD)
- **Mandate/controls:** operates Golden Gate Ferry between the SF Ferry Building and Larkspur, Sausalito, Tiburon and Angel Island (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Gate_Ferry · https://www.goldengate.org/ferry/schedules-maps/). Owns and operates the Sausalito terminal **on property leased from the City of Sausalito** (District's own project page: "The District owns and operates the ferry terminal on property leased from the City of Sausalito" — https://www.goldengate.org/district/district-projects/sausalito-ferry-terminal-improvements-project/). Larkspur Ferry Terminal is the District's own flagship facility (https://www.goldengate.org/ferry/schedules-maps/). Tiburon commute service runs via a **sublease arrangement involving Blue & Gold Fleet and underlying private owner AC Ventures** (https://www.goldengate.org/ride-ggfs-new-weekday-commute-service-between-tiburon--san-francisco/ · https://www.ktvu.com/news/golden-gate-ferry-to-begin-tiburon-san-francisco-service).
- **Posture: OPERATE.** A second full public ferry operator in the same market — BA-2's three stations are all in its service territory. Any Marin Line conversation must respect that GGBHTD runs these exact corridors today (honesty gate: never trash-talk incumbent ferries; we feed and extend, premium-tier).

### BCDC (San Francisco Bay Conservation and Development Commission)
- **Mandate:** created by the **McAteer-Petris Act**; regulates fill, dredging, and shoreline development in the Bay and a 100-foot shoreline band; also administers the Suisun Marsh Preservation Act. Sources: https://www.bcdc.ca.gov/resources/laws-and-regulations/ · https://acceleratingrestoration.org/agencies/san-francisco-bay-conservation-development-commission-bcdc/
- **Controls:** permits, not operations — the Bay Area's Chapter-91 analogue. Any new float, gangway, or dock modification at any of the 22 stations runs through a BCDC permit. Confirmed on-network precedent: BCDC Permit 1977.001.19 (amended) for the **Oyster Point Marina dock replacement** (https://www.smharbor.com/files/fbb07268b/3_Issued+BCDC+Permit+No.+1977.001.19_Oyster+Point+Marina+Dock+Replacement.pdf).
- **Posture: ENABLE only** (permitting gate).

### MTC (Metropolitan Transportation Commission)
- **Mandate:** "the transportation planning, financing and coordinating agency for the nine-county San Francisco Bay Area" (their words — https://mtc.ca.gov/about-mtc); the region's MPO.
- **Controls:** regional funding. **Regional Measure 3** bridge-toll funds are "a critical operating funding source for WETA's planned expansion services at Mission Bay, Berkeley, and Redwood City" (WETA SRTP via MTC: https://es.mtc.ca.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2023-01/WETA_SRTP_Report.pdf) and RM3 allocations have funded three 149-passenger electric vessels ($18M — https://mtc.legistar.com/View.ashx?M=F&ID=13781769&GUID=561870BE-B551-418D-ABE3-BB16966C67FE) and ferry enhancement/electrification lines (https://blog.bayareametro.gov/posts/regional-measure-3-delivers-35-million-toward-three-transit-projects-latest-dispersal-toll · https://mtc.ca.gov/funding/regional-funding/regional-measure-3).
- **Posture: ENABLE** (funder/planner; does not operate).

### Port of San Francisco
- **Mandate/controls:** city port authority controlling the SF waterfront. Owns and operates **South Beach Harbor at Pier 40** (https://www.sfport.com/maritime/harbors-berthings/south-beach-harbor) — the hub.json South Beach stop. Co-leads the **Mission Bay Ferry Landing** with WETA: "The Port of San Francisco and Water Emergency Transit Authority (WETA) are leading the project with the support of other City and regional agencies"; two-berth landing, up to 6,000 passengers/day capacity, Phase 2b construction 2026/2027 (https://www.sfport.com/projects-programs/mission-bay-ferry-landing) — the network's day-one Mission Bay hub. Partnered with WETA on the Downtown SF Ferry Terminal expansion ($100M project — https://www.sfcta.org/blogs/weta-and-sf-port-partner-expand-downtown-ferry-terminal).
- **Enable precedent (directly relevant):** the Port already sanctions **private water-taxi operators** on its waterfront — Tideline is "an official Port of San Francisco water taxi service" and the Port has publicly framed water taxis as congestion relief (https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g33062-d10179476-Reviews-Tideline_Water_Taxi-Sausalito_Marin_County_California.html · https://abc7news.com/post/bay-area-water-taxi-company-expands-fleet/370611/ · https://www.sfport.com/visit/getting-around). A permitted-private-operator pathway on Port docks **already exists** — the cleanest Enable template in the market.
- **Published plan language (their own words):** Mission Bay Ferry Landing "is essential to alleviate current regional transportation overcrowding and provide transportation resiliency in the event of an earthquake, BART or Bay Bridge failure, or other unplanned events. Ferry service will reduce our community's carbon footprint…" (https://www.sfport.com/projects-programs/mission-bay-ferry-landing).
- **Posture: ENABLE** (landing owner/permitter/co-developer; WETA operates).

### Port of Oakland
- **Controls:** owns **Jack London Square**, the mixed-use waterfront district containing the Oakland Ferry Terminal (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_London_Square · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Ferry_Terminal); WETA operates the ferry service there (https://sanfranciscobayferry.com/terminal/oakland-ferry-terminal/).
- **Posture: ENABLE** (landowner; WETA operates). No port-published water-transit expansion plan identified this pass (**unverified** — follow-up).

### Port of Redwood City
- **Controls:** the Port Commission has "supervision and control of the Port area, waterfront properties and adjacent lands and Redwood City Marina" (https://www.redwoodcityport.com/port-commission · https://www.redwoodcity.org/city-hall/advisory-bodies-and-committees/regulatory-and-advisory-boards-commissions-and-committees/port-commission) — the hub.json Redwood City stop.
- **Published activity:** actively partnering with WETA/SF Bay Ferry "on design and permitting for a potential future permanent ferry landing," with a ballpark pilot service already run (https://sanfranciscobayferry.com/our-ferry-future/ · https://sanfranciscobayferry.com/sf-bay-ferrys-redwood-city-ballpark-pilot-service-launches-july-28/); WETA's Redwood City Ferry Study is an official expansion project.
- **Posture: ENABLE, actively pro-ferry** — the strongest South Bay opening on the network.

### City of South San Francisco + San Mateo County Harbor District (Oyster Point interchange)
- **Controls:** the Oyster Point Marina land is owned by the City of South San Francisco; the **San Mateo County Harbor District has operated Oyster Point Marina/Park under a Joint Powers Agreement with the City since 1977** (https://www.smharbor.com/frequently-asked-questions-oyster-point-marina-park · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_San_Francisco_Ferry_Terminal · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyster_Point_Marina/Park — Wikipedia notes the JPA has a termination date; current JPA status **unverified this pass**). The WETA South San Francisco Ferry Terminal (2012) sits at the marina, funded in part by SMCTA (https://www.smcta.com/funding-programs/ferry).
- **Live development:** WETA is running a **South San Francisco Study** with proposed service/operational changes (public meetings Sept 2025; schedule optimization from March 2026), working with stakeholders including major employers (https://sanfranciscobayferry.com/south-sf-study/ · https://www.facebook.com/CityofSouthSanFrancisco/posts/1275760124587362/). Employer shuttles (Commute.org, Genentech) already meet ferry arrivals (https://commute.org/route/oyster-point-ferry/ · https://mtc.ca.gov/news/weta-launches-new-east-bay-south-san-francisco-ferry).
- **Posture: ENABLE** — and the SSF study is direct evidence that the public operator is actively seeking employer-anchored demand on exactly this interchange. (Internal note only; no employer-anchoring language renders.)

### City & County of San Francisco — SFMTA + TIDA
- **SFMTA:** city transportation agency; controls landside connections (the T-Third/Central Subway is one block from Mission Bay Ferry Landing per the Port — https://www.sfport.com/projects-programs/mission-bay-ferry-landing). No water-transit landing control identified; water assets sit with the Port. Posture: **ENABLE (landside integration partner)** — direct SFMTA water-transportation policy **unverified this pass**.
- **TIDA / Treasure Island:** the Treasure Island Ferry runs between the Ferry Building and Treasure Island, operated by **PROP SF under contract with Treasure Island Community Development (TICD)**, the island's private developer; **WETA is slated to take over the service (announced for 2026)** (https://sanfranciscobayferry.com/treasure-island-ferry-service/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Island_Ferry · https://www.seamlessbayarea.org/blog/2024/12/5/sf-bay-ferry-to-take-over-treasure-island-ferry-service-by-2026). TIDA is the city authority for the island; exact ferry-landing ownership (TIDA vs TICD) **unverified this pass**.
- Precedent value: Treasure Island proves a **private-operator commuter ferry launched under a development agreement** in this market — an Enable-track precedent for private service on city-controlled landings.

## 6 · City classification: DUAL — Operate-weighted

The Bay Area is the program's clearest **dual-posture** market, weighted toward **Operate**: two full public ferry operators exist (WETA — a named long-term Navier partner with an adopted expansion-and-electrification policy and active procurement of electric vessels; GGBHTD on the Marin corridors), so vessel-procurement conversations have living precedent and budget lines (RM3, EPA). Simultaneously the **Enable** track is unusually mature: the Port of SF already licenses private water-taxi operators on its docks, Berkeley hosted a private commuter ferry, and Treasure Island launched a private developer-funded commuter route — three separate precedents for privately financed service at public landings. Page weighting per the Public Partners brief: lead Operate-friendly (WETA/GGBHTD), with the Enable track presented as proven, not hypothetical.

## 7 · Open flags for Jaideep

1. **WETA South San Francisco Study is live** (proposed service changes from March 2026, employer stakeholders at the table) — the single highest-leverage engagement window on the network's flagship interchange. Recommend a touchpoint before the study's changes settle. https://sanfranciscobayferry.com/south-sf-study/
