## 1 · What is codified (verified)

### 1.1 Legal framework
- **Legislative Decree No. (61) of 2006 — Ports and Marine Navigation Affairs Law**: governs "all ports, docks, harbours, piers, wharves (including any private jetty), fairways, embankments and related structures"; the Minister of Transportation issues the Implementing Regulations and operational orders. Navigation conduct inside port limits is therefore set by MTT/PMA regulation, not municipal code. Source: https://bahrainbusinesslaws.com/laws/Ports-and-Marine-Navigation-Law — **PRIMARY-TEXT** (unofficial reproduction).
- **Royal Decree No. (32) of 2020 — Small Ship Registration and Safety Conditions** (<150 GT): registration, safety inspection, master licence, navigation licence for commercial/passenger boats. Source: https://www.mtt.gov.bh/small-ship-registration — **PRIMARY**.
- **Navigation permit** mandatory for anyone maneuvering a Bahraini-flag boat. Source: https://services.bahrain.bh/wps/portal/en/BSP/GSX-UI-EServiceDetails?esID=2090 — **PRIMARY**.

### 1.2 Coast Guard (MOI) operational restrictions — the binding constraint actually found
- **Night-time maritime movement ban (2025, security-driven):** MOI announced a ban on sea movement in territorial waters "from 6:00 pm (1800) until 4:00 am (0400), starting today and until further notice" (June 2025), later revised to **6:30 pm–4:00 am effective July 16** per news reposts. Sources: https://www.policemc.gov.bh/en/news/77453/ — **PRIMARY URL** (page unreachable at scrape time; wording per search index); revision via News of Bahrain / Bahrain This Month reposts — **SECONDARY**. **Status as of research date: unresolved — re-verify before any schedule work.** If still in force, this eliminates evening service entirely and is the single biggest operating-envelope risk in Bahrain. Flag to Jaideep.
- **Temporary 3-knot zones:** MOI notices have imposed temporary local speed restrictions (3 kn) in defined areas during works/events — seen only via social reposts of MOI announcements — **SECONDARY**; treat as a recurring notice mechanism, not a standing limit.
- Enforcement: MOI Coast Guard; announcements via policemc.gov.bh and official MOI channels. Purpose stated: "maritime safety and security" (security-driven, not wake-driven).

### 1.3 Port limits — Khalifa Bin Salman Port (KBSP) and Mina Salman
- KBSP operates under PMA supervision with a **vessel traffic management system (VTMS)**; pilotage for commercial traffic. Source: https://www.mtt.gov.bh/ports-and-maritime, http://www.mtt.gov.bh/khalifa-bin-salman-port — **PRIMARY**.
- A port-industry directory lists the Bahrain approach channel as "Speed: no restrictions" for arriving ships — **SECONDARY, low reliability; do not rely on it**.
- **No published numeric harbour speed limit for small craft at KBSP/Mina Salman was found** on MTT/PMA public pages — **fail closed**: assume port-control-directed movement and displacement-speed transit inside port limits; confirm with PMA in feasibility phase.

### 1.4 King Fahd Causeway restricted zones
- KFCA mandate confirmed (kfca.sa — PRIMARY), but **no codified public navigation rule for the causeway's marine passages was found** (bridge-opening transit lanes, exclusion zones). **UNSOURCED — fail closed:** treat causeway approaches as restricted; obtain the rule from KFCA/Coast Guard directly. Do not schedule west-of-Bahrain routings across the causeway line in any rendered material.

### 1.5 Protected areas
- Hawar Islands are widely reported as a protected nature reserve (Ramsar-listed); **not primary-verified in this pass — UNSOURCED here; verify with SCE before scheduling Hawar frequencies.** Wildlife (dugong, socotra cormorant) implies a low-wake/low-noise expectation on Hawar approaches regardless of codified limits.

## 2 · What is NOT codified (plain statement)
**No publicly codified, numeric speed or no-wake limits for small passenger craft in Bahraini coastal waters, marinas, or the Manama–Muharraq channel were found** in MTT, MOI, or legislation sources accessible online. Bahrain regulates via (a) MTT/PMA implementing regulations and licence conditions, and (b) MOI Coast Guard notices. ## 4 · "What relief unlocks" (foiling case — internal framing only)
The rules found are **security- and safety-purposed**, not wake-purposed. Where future PMA licence conditions impose harbour slow zones, a foiling vessel's near-zero wake at speed serves the underlying purpose (wake damage/moored-vessel protection) better than displacement craft — a credible basis to negotiate foil-borne transit in the outer harbour. Label any such claim "what relief unlocks"; never present as current permission.
