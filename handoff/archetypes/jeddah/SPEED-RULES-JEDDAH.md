# SPEED-RULES — Jeddah (Red Sea coast)

Internal audit file. Research date: 2026-08-16. Base schedule math at 25 kn service speed only where no posted limit constrains the leg; fail closed elsewhere.

## Controlling fact (same regime as Eastern Province — national)
**No publicly codified numeric speed or no-wake limits were found for Jeddah coastal waters, the corniche, Obhur Creek, or the JIP approaches.** Speed control is administrative: the Border Guard "determin[es] and identif[ies] the navigational/shipping lanes for marine boats/vessels, THEIR SPEEDS, departure and return times, and the duration of sailing" — Regulations of Security & Safety for Maritime Activities (text via Qanoniah legal DB) [PRIMARY-TEXT, national scope]. Limits are therefore NEGOTIATED INTO THE SAILING PERMIT per lane, set by Border Guard sector command, not published numeric code.

## Red Sea layer on top (does not exist in EP): SRSA
- SRSA holds the published mandate to "identify areas, routes, and navigational maps for navigational and marine tourism activities, in coordination with relevant entities," and produced the **first geographical navigation map of the Saudi Red Sea** with 19 government entities (incl. GEOSA) [SECONDARY citing SRSA — see AUTHORITY-MAP]. Marine-tourism vessel operations additionally sit under SRSA's Marine Tourism Watercraft Classification + Marine Tour Operator regulations (archived PRIMARY texts linked in AUTHORITY-MAP). Numeric operating speeds are not published in the regulation summaries reviewed [UNSOURCED — full regulation texts should be pulled via the archive links for operating clauses].
- Practical consequence: in Jeddah a route filing has TWO route authorities (Border Guard lanes + SRSA tourism routes/navigation map) — but also a ready-made framework designed to say yes to tourism vessels.

## Jeddah Islamic Port — southern constraint
- Navigational channel ~14 mi × 400 m; pilotage compulsory/24-7 for ships — arabseaports.com [SECONDARY]. Port area 12.5 km², 62 berths, >5,000 ship calls/yr [SECONDARY citing Mawani]. Port coordinates 21.4838 N, 39.1734 E [SECONDARY, Wikipedia].
- No published numeric small-craft speed limit for port waters found [UNSOURCED].
- **Existing proof of coexistence:** the JTC water taxi serves Al-Balad via the port today (JTC CEO: "the easiest and fastest route was through the port") [PRIMARY-quality report, Arab News 06 Mar 2025]. Small passenger craft operate in port waters under harbourmaster coordination — the precedent and the coordination channel already exist.
- Schedule assumption: harbour-speed regime (assume 8–10 kn) inside port limits / cruise-terminal approach; foil-borne transit only outside the fairway; cross the fairway perpendicular at designated points per harbourmaster instruction. [DERIVED assumption, stated]

## Obhur Creek (Sharm Obhur)
- ~10 km sheltered inlet lined with private marinas and resorts; Red Sea Marina (300 berths, yachts to 60 m) operates on the creek [PRIMARY operator, redseamarina.com]; SRSA-licensed marinas (Al Ahlam, Red Sea Marina) are on/near the creek [SECONDARY citing SRSA]. Navigability for N45-class craft is proven by resident 60 m yachts.
- No published numeric speed/no-wake rule for the creek found [UNSOURCED]. It is a dense leisure waterway (swimmers, moored craft, small boats).
- Schedule assumption: **displacement/low-wake regime inside the creek — 8 kn planning value**, foil-borne only seaward of the creek mouth. [DERIVED assumption, stated]

## Corniche / near-shore
- 30 km recreational waterfront with swim zones, jet-ski and leisure-craft activity [SECONDARY, multiple]. Al-Nawras Island and Jeddah Waterfront are Amanah recreational zones [SECONDARY citing Saudipedia].
- No codified numeric near-shore limit found [UNSOURCED]. Assumption: stand off ≥0.5 nm from bathing beaches at service speed; approach landings at ≤6 kn. [DERIVED assumption]
- **F1 race weekend (Jeddah Corniche Circuit, March/April):** assume temporary marine security/exclusion zones along the circuit shoreline and JYC basin; no public notice text found [UNSOURCED — plan around event NOTAM/Notice-to-Mariners; JYC hosts superyachts race weekend, so managed access exists].
- Reef note: Jeddah's coast is fringed by coral reefs; corridors must follow marked passes per the SRSA/GEOSA navigation map; foiling draft (foils down) must be respected in reef passes. [DERIVED planning note]

## What relief unlocks (foiling case — argument, not a claim)
Border Guard speed powers exist for safety (collision, wake, swimmer protection); SRSA's regime exists for environmental protection of the reef coast. A foil-borne N45 produces minimal wake and low noise and, at height, near-zero hull wash — the rules' own purposes are served at higher transit speed. As in EP, this is a **permit negotiation, not a rule change**; SRSA's enabler mandate makes it the natural sponsor. [DERIVED argument — internal]
