# San Diego Bay — Node Inventory & Network Design (Employer Water Network v2)

Status: research-complete, source-verified desk pass (web, Aug 15 2026). Follows NODE-INVENTORY-SPEC.md. Landing gate = usable pier/marina berth for a 30–45 ft commercial passenger vessel (full ferry terminal NOT required). Fail-closed: everything below is checked against a live source; unknowns say "unknown."

Model reminder: 20-seat electric hydrofoils, employer seat blocks, launch trigger 60–80 committed seats/corridor, $650–1,200/seat-month.

**Headline verdicts**
- San Diego Bay is a ~12 nm long, protected, ferry-proven water system with a **single-landlord dynamic**: the Port of San Diego (SDUPD) controls nearly all landings across 34 miles of tidelands in five cities. One landlord relationship unlocks most of the map.
- **Incumbent respect:** Flagship Cruises & Events runs the Coronado ferry (since 1993 as a commuter program, free 4:50–8:30 AM weekday crossings subsidized by the City of Coronado), a brand-new Chula Vista ferry (launched June 1, 2026), and an on-call water taxi — and has announced a switch to **electric ferries by fall 2026** (CBS8/News8). Navier's posture must be complement/partner, not head-to-head on Broadway↔Coronado.
- **Speed answer:** the central/north bay main channel has **no general posted speed limit** for a passenger vessel (5 mph applies only in Glorietta Bay, the Bay Bridge mooring/roadstead and designated anchorages per SDUPD Code §4.35(c)(3), plus marina basins); a foiler can run at speed mid-bay. **South Bay south of Loews Resort / Sweetwater Channel is a posted 5 mph area** — this caps the Chula Vista corridor's last ~2–3 nm.
- **Navy answer:** base docks are controlled (32 CFR-level security zones); no civilian service can land on-base. Navy commuters are reachable only via public landings near gates: Coronado Ferry Landing (NASNI, ~1 mi to gates — bike/shuttle tier) and, weakly, Pier 32 Marina (NBSD 32nd St, ~1.5–2 mi — shuttle tier). The famous shipyard-shift market (NASSCO/BAE, Barrio Logan) currently has **no usable civilian landing** — that corridor dies unless a new float is permitted.

## No landing exists (demand areas with no usable dock)
- **Barrio Logan / NASSCO–BAE shipyards.** Cesar Chavez Park has a public **viewing pier** (fishing banned since 2008; no berthing float). Tenth Avenue Marine Terminal is TWIC-secure cargo. The shipyard-shift market (see commute pain) has no civilian landing; a new permitted float at Cesar Chavez Park would be the unlock — Port is currently taking community input on the park's next improvement phase (Instagram/Port, 2026). Sources: portofsandiego.org/experiences/where-go/barrio-logan-park · ihpsd.github.io (fishing ban) · sandiego.gov Shipyard District Parking Structure Feasibility Study PDF.
- **Naval Base San Diego (32nd St) waterfront.** Waters within the base are a federal security zone (33 CFR 165.1101 — entry prohibited). No public-adjacent landing exists on that shore; nearest is Pier 32 Marina across Sweetwater Channel (~1.5–2 mi to gates, estimate).
- **NASNI / Naval Base Coronado docks.** Fleet landings are base-controlled; the north-shore waters carry a security zone (33 CFR 165.1105). Civilian service cannot land on base — NASNI commuters must be served via Coronado Ferry Landing + base shuttle/bike (NASNI runs a shuttle per Military OneSource).
- **Liberty Station / NTC boat channel.** Kayak/paddleboard launch ramp only (2455 Cushing Rd); the channel was only recently remediated and transferred from the Navy (U-T, Sep 2022). No dock for a 30–45 ft vessel. Liberty Station's 100+ arts-district organizations and Point Loma offices are unservable by water today.
- **San Diego International Airport (SAN).** No landing serves the terminals. Harbor Island marinas are across N Harbor Dr but ~1 mi+ from T1/T2 (estimate) — shuttle-tier only. New airport entrance road (opened Aug 1, 2025) diverts 45,000 vehicles/day from N Harbor Dr and materially reduces the pain a water link would have solved (san.org).
- **Embarcadero Marina Parks North/South & Seaport Village.** Parks and a fishing pier; no transient passenger berthing (nearest usable = Marriott Marquis Marina / Fifth Avenue Landing). Seaport San Diego redevelopment may change this — planned, not existing.
- **Mission Bay (honest check — DIES).** Separate water system locked to an ocean run around Point Loma: ~7 nm of open Pacific separates the two bay entrances (captaincurran.com sailing distances) plus in-bay transit on both ends, and Mission Bay itself is a City-regulated 5 mph/special-use zone patchwork (sandiego.gov boating regs; seattleyachts.com guide). No credible Mission Bay↔San Diego Bay commuter link. Killed.
- **Oceanside Harbor (honest check — DIES as commuter).** Real municipal harbor with guest slips (ci.oceanside.ca.us), but ~30 nm of open ocean north of San Diego Bay (trawlerforum.com). Even at 25-kn foil cruise that is ~1h15m+ before dock time — far outside the 20–45 min sweet spot. Park as a future long-haul/seasonal idea only.

**Shelter Island / America's Cup Harbor / Point Loma:** sportfishing fleet + marine industry (largest sportfishing fleet on the West Coast — sportfishing.org), boatyards, hotels. **NIWC Pacific** (5,000+ staff per NAVWAR/Facebook 80th-anniversary post — secondary) sits up the Point Loma hill, NOT walkable from any landing. ## Commute pain (sourced)
- **Coronado Bridge — the singular chokepoint.** ~75,000 vehicles/day (Wikipedia, 2009 figure). Busiest northbound hour 3–4 PM averages 4,176 cars; peak southbound hour 6–7 AM averages 3,780 (San Diego Magazine, "Inside the Icon"). Caltrans still moves a physical barrier twice daily (1 PM/7 PM) to flip a lane with the commute — and the barrier mover repeatedly breaks (Caltrans D11 Facebook/Threads posts, 2025–26). Base gate closures back traffic up over the bridge (coronadotimes.com, Nov 2023); a carrier in port makes it materially worse (r/navy). The 4th & Orange bottleneck on the Coronado side is a live civic complaint (timesofsandiego.com, Jan 2026).
- **Shipyard shift change (Harbor Drive/Barrio Logan).** The City's own Shipyard District Parking Structure Feasibility Study proposes a pedestrian bridge across Harbor Drive specifically to "eliminate conflicts of pedestrian and vehicular traffic during shift change" (sandiego.gov PDF). NBSD's ~35,000 personnel plus NASSCO/BAE workers share the same Harbor Drive spine; SANDAG has been re-striping southbound Harbor Dr at 32nd St (NavalBaseSD Facebook, 2025). Famous, real, and currently unservable by water (no landing).
- **I-5 south corridor (Chula Vista→downtown).** ~10 mi, 15–20 min free-flow (meetways.com) but northbound morning I-5 through National City is "worse — the merge…" and southbound gets ugly 4–6 PM (sddriverlife.com); rush hour can span Sorrento Valley to Chula Vista (San Diego Magazine).
- **SAN airport access.** Historically bad on N Harbor Dr; HONESTY FLAG: the airport's new signal-free entrance road opened Aug 1, 2025 and diverts ~45,000 vehicles/day off N Harbor Dr (san.org; timesofsandiego.com) — airport-access pain is now materially reduced, and no landing serves the terminals anyway. Do not build the SD story on airport access.

## Regulatory / operational findings (internal dock-track context)
- **Speed — where a foiler can run.** SDUPD Port Code §4.35(c)(3): 5 mph limit applies **only** in Glorietta Bay, the Bay Bridge Mooring Area and the Central Bay anchorage (Port-Code-Section-435.pdf). South San Diego Bay **south of Loews Resort and the Sweetwater Channel is a posted 5 mph area** marked by cement pylons, with wildlife refuges (sea turtles, nesting birds) and shoals outside channels (portofsandiego.org General Bay Information; SDUPD Port Code index re §4.30). Water skiing is expressly permitted south of the bridge east of the Glorietta Bay entrance — i.e., mid-bay speed is normal. Cargo-ship 12-knot VSR program is **voluntary and cargo-only** (portofsandiego.org VSR). City 5-mph rules near swimmers/beaches apply at edges. **Net: full-speed foiling is available in the main channel and central bay along the entire downtown–Coronado–National City run; the Chula Vista run loses its last ~2–3 nm to the 5 mph zone; Glorietta Bay itself is a short 5 mph pocket.**
- **Navy security zones (33 CFR):** §165.1101 — waters within Naval Station San Diego (32nd St): entry prohibited. §165.1102 — Naval Base Point Loma (sub base): defined zone along the Point Loma shore; entry prohibited without COTP/Navy permission (the main channel passes outside it). §165.1103 — Naval Mine ASW Command zone. §165.1105 — NBC north shoreline zone. 33 CFR 334.870 restricted areas: Bravo Pier (ammunition pier, North Island) — transit through the area allowed by most direct route, never within 100 ft of the pier; Degaussing Station (Point Loma) — transit allowed, direct route, no loitering; Ballast Point–Zuniga Point area at the harbor entrance — no mooring/anchoring, transit rules as posted. §334.880 — naval anchorage reserved for government vessels; others may navigate through. **Net: all corridors below are legally navigable today by staying in/near the marked channels; none require entering a prohibited zone.**
- **Single landlord.** The Port of San Diego (SDUPD) controls tidelands across all five bay cities; charter/commercial passenger operations from any marina require operator permission + Port process (SDUPD Code §4.37 charter vessel regulations; portofsandiego.org charter-vessel page). One Port relationship covers Broadway Pier, Fifth Avenue Landing area, Coronado Ferry Landing, Chula Vista, National City, Shelter/Harbor Island. Exceptions: Glorietta Bay Marina (City of Coronado) and private club docks.
- **Incumbent.** Flagship Cruises & Events (family-owned, operating since 1915): Coronado ferry 20x/day incl. City-of-Coronado-funded free commuter runs since 1993; Convention Center↔Coronado every 30 min; Chula Vista ferry (June 1, 2026, 6x/day, 45 min, 32-pax); on-call water taxi; **electric ferry fleet announced for fall 2026** (News8/CBS8 video; coronadotimes.com). Posture: partner or differentiate on employer seat-blocks, speed (foil vs displacement), and unserved pairs (Chula Vista frequency, Point Loma, Glorietta Bay). Do NOT pitch Broadway↔Coronado head-to-head — it is subsidized and free at commute hours.
- **Marine mammals/wildlife in-bay:** no whale-transit rules inside the bay (whale VSR/encounter rules are offshore); the binding in-bay constraint is the South Bay wildlife refuges + sea-turtle habitat inside the 5 mph zone (portofsandiego.org bay info). NOAA viewing guidelines apply generally.

## Proposed network (verified nodes only — metro-style, honest phases)

**Line names (plain English):**
- **Bridge Line** (bridge bypass): Glorietta Bay ↔ Fifth Avenue Landing (Convention Center) ↔ Broadway Pier. ~1.5–2.5 nm legs, 10–15 min. Serves Coronado residents/Navy-adjacent commuters into the Convention Center/Gaslamp and downtown core without the bridge — deliberately using **Glorietta Bay** as the Coronado anchor to complement (not clone) Flagship's Ferry Landing service. Caveat: short 5-mph pocket inside Glorietta Bay; crossing is below the 20-min sweet spot — bundle as a feeder, not a standalone corridor.
- **South Bay Line** (flagship corridor): Safe Harbor South Bay (Chula Vista) ↔ Fifth Avenue Landing ↔ Broadway Pier. ~7 nm; foil the central-bay portion at speed, 5 mph through the posted South Bay zone → realistic ~30–40 min dock-to-dock vs Flagship's 45-min displacement run and a 15–60 min I-5 drive. Anchor employers: Gaylord Pacific (walk-tier, ~800–4,000 jobs) reverse-commute + downtown employers forward-commute. This is the launch corridor: brand-new proven demand signal (Flagship added the route in 2026), thin incumbent frequency (6x/day, 32 pax, no restrooms, not wheelchair accessible), and an employer big enough to seed 60–80 seats.
- **Point Loma Line**: Shelter Island Guest Docks / Point Loma Marina (America's Cup Harbor) ↔ Harbor Island (Harbor Island West or Safe Harbor Sunroad) ↔ Broadway Pier. ~3.5–4.5 nm, 15–25 min, all full-speed water. Serves the highest-income verified catchment (92106, $142,897) into the downtown core; no incumbent scheduled service at all on this pair.
- **National City Line (watchlist)**: Pier 32 Marina ↔ Fifth Avenue Landing/Broadway. ~4.5 nm, all-speed water — but walk-tier employment at both ends is weak (NBSD is shuttle-tier) and catchment income is unverified. Hold until an NBSD shuttle partnership or a Barrio Logan float exists.
- **Shipyard Shuttle (dead until a dock exists)**: the NASSCO/BAE shift-change story is real and sourced, but there is **no civilian landing in Barrio Logan** — state plainly in any external material that this line requires a new permitted float at/near Cesar Chavez Park (Port park-improvement process is live in 2026 — a concrete dock-track ask).

**Phases:**
1. **Phase 1 — South Bay Line** (Chula Vista↔downtown), employer-anchored by Gaylord Pacific + downtown/Convention hospitality. Trigger: 60–80 seats between Gaylord, Hilton Bayfront/Marriott Marquis, and downtown employers.
2. **Phase 2 — Point Loma Line** (Shelter Island/America's Cup↔Broadway Pier), premium residential catchment play; add Harbor Island stop opportunistically.
3. **Phase 3 — Bridge Line feeder** (Glorietta Bay↔Convention Center) in cooperation with City of Coronado/Flagship; **National City** and **Shipyard** lines gated on new dock access.

**Killed honestly:** Mission Bay link (open-ocean geometry), Oceanside (30 nm — out of range), SAN airport landing (none exists; pain reduced by 2025 road), Broadway↔Coronado head-to-head (subsidized incumbent going electric).
