# DC / Potomac Node Inventory — Employer Water Network (deep pass)

Status: source-verified desk research, Aug 15 2026. Follows NODE-INVENTORY-SPEC.md (fail-closed). Every node below was checked against a live source (operator page, city/NPS/port authority, marina site, or news). Walk-minute figures are **map-estimated unless a source is cited** — treat all walk/shuttle minutes as estimates, not measurements. Landing gate applied: usable pier or marina berth for a 30–45 ft commercial passenger vessel; a full ferry terminal is not required.

Model reminder: 20-seat electric hydrofoil, employer seat blocks, launch trigger 60–80 committed seats/corridor, $650–1,200/seat-month, 20–45 min crossings.

**Headline advantage found in this pass:** an electric hydrofoil proposal for this exact market already exists — in 2025 Candela (Sweden) publicly proposed electric-hydrofoil commuter ferries for a Georgetown ↔ Reagan National corridor, explicitly arguing hydrofoils defeat the wake-restriction problem that killed every prior Potomac ferry study (virginiaplaces.org/transportation/ferrypotomac.html, item 28). Navier would not be introducing the concept cold — but also would not be first to pitch it.

## No landing exists (demand areas with no usable dock — do NOT promote)

- **National Landing / Amazon HQ2 / Crystal City / Pentagon City (THE kill-risk item — investigated hard):** Confirmed: **no water landing exists anywhere at National Landing.** The district is separated from the Potomac by the CSX rail embankment, the GW Memorial Parkway, and the Mount Vernon Trail. The nearest water features are Roaches Run Waterfowl Sanctuary and the DCA shoreline — no dock at either. Nearest REAL docks: Washington Sailing Marina/Daingerfield Island (~2.5 mi from Met Park, shuttle-tier, est. 45–55 min walk / ~10 min shuttle) and Columbia Island Marina (~2 mi from Pentagon City, shuttle-tier). **No planned dock/marina project at Long Bridge or Potomac Yard could be sourced.** The $2.3B Long Bridge project is rail + a bike/ped bridge only (vapassengerrailauthority.org; shikunusa.com); the $57.2M CC2DCA project is a pedestrian/multimodal bridge from Crystal Drive over the CSX/GW Pkwy to DCA (arlingtonva.us) — it improves shoreline access but includes no dock. A 2024 Alexandria EDP suggestion of water-taxi access via Four Mile Run for the (since-dead) Potomac Yard arena was publicly questioned because Four Mile Run is "rarely more than four feet deep" (virginiaplaces.org, item 27). **Verdict: HQ2 is shuttle-tier from every real dock today. A new NPS-permitted dock near Daingerfield/Roaches Run + CC2DCA is the only path to walk-tier, and none is planned.**
- **Rosslyn / Key Bridge:** No landing on the Virginia side. Key Bridge Boathouse (3500 Water St NW) is on the **Georgetown** side and is an NPS paddlecraft facility (kayak/SUP rentals and launches only) — fails the 30–45 ft vessel gate (nps.gov/places/000/key-bridge-boathouse.htm). Boeing HQ (929 Long Bridge Dr — actually in Crystal City, see HQ2 note) and RTX HQ (1000 Wilson Blvd, Rosslyn — rtx.com/locations) are therefore unservable by water today. Note also Rosslyn is upstream of Memorial Bridge = inside the permanent Memorial→Chain Bridge no-wake zone; even with a dock this leg would be slow.
- **Reagan National Airport (DCA):** No public vessel dock at the airport; the 2013 NVRC study and the 2025 Candela proposal both treat DCA as a target stop, but no dock exists or is in a funded plan that we could source. The shoreline is MWAA/NPS controlled and sits at the boundary of security Zone 1 (33 CFR 165.508).
- **Roaches Run & Gravelly Point:** NPS parkland directly across from Crystal City/HQ2; Gravelly Point has a small-craft boat ramp only, Roaches Run is a shallow lagoon — no berthing structure at either. Inside/adjacent to security Zone 1 boundary.
- **JBAB (Joint Base Anacostia-Bolling) & DHS St. Elizabeths:** The single biggest documented commuter demand pool on the river — the NVRC 2018 gap analysis found ~70% of the Woodbridge ferry market wanted JBAB/DHS, and the M-495 RFI counted 3,522 of 5,195 daily home-based AM trips to JBAB/DHS (virginiaplaces.org, items 16, 19). But JBAB is a controlled military installation (DHS required security clearances to disembark there under the ferry plan) and St. Elizabeths has no dock. Poplar Point (nearest civilian shoreline) has no landing — it appeared only as a *proposed* "DC River Circulator" terminus in the 2023 NVRC business plan. Track as growth-phase only, government-partnership-dependent.
- **Buzzard Point Marina (the former NPS marina at 1st & V St SW):** CLOSED Dec 31, 2015 at concession expiry; boats removed (nps.gov/anac/learn/news/buzzard-point-marina-closing.htm; wjla.com). FAILS — do not confuse with James Creek Marina next door, which is open.
- **Belmont Bay / North Woodbridge ferry terminal:** proposed in county small-area plans (two candidate dock sites mapped) but never built; operator lease talks ran 7 years without agreement. Former/planned — status honest: paper only.
- **Jones Point Park (Alexandria):** named in 2014 FTA-grant-era reporting as a commuter-ferry origin to JBAB; never built. Paper only.

## Residential catchments (near each landing)

- **Old Town Alexandria (City Marina):** the strongest residential node on the river — Old Town/Waterfront census tract median HH income $157,573 (Alexandria EDP dashboard, prior scan); dense, walkable, ferry-habituated (5,000 daily riders during the 2019 Metro shutdown — see Commute pain).
- **SW Waterfront/Wharf:** new high-rent towers directly on the piers; resident count unverified.
- **Navy Yard/Capitol Riverfront:** high-growth young-professional rental market at Diamond Teague/Yards; count unverified this pass.
- **Georgetown:** classic high-income catchment at the dock; poor Metro access makes water access unusually valuable here.
- **National Harbor:** on-site condos plus **Fort Washington/Tantallon** affluent PG County suburbs behind it; Fort Washington Marina extends the same catchment 6 mi downriver.
- **Belle Haven/Fort Hunt (VA):** affluent single-family along the GW Parkway south of Old Town; Belle Haven Marina is their dock.
- **Woodbridge/Occoquan/Belmont Bay:** ~6,000 security-clearance holders within 15 min of Occoquan Harbour Marina (PRTC M-495 RFI) — big, but their #1 destination (JBAB/DHS) has no civilian landing.

## Commute pain (what the water beats)

- **Region-level:** DC ranked #1 worst US traffic in 2025 (Consumer Affairs via WTOP, Jul 2025 — prior scan): avg commute 33.4 min, congestion 6h35m/day.
- **Wilson Bridge / I-495 (National Harbor ↔ Alexandria/DC):** bridge designed for 75K vehicles/day, carrying ~195–200K/day (FHWA case study; roadstothefuture.com). This is the corridor the existing National Harbor↔Alexandria↔Wharf water taxi already bypasses.
- **I-395 / 14th St Bridge (Old Town & National Landing ↔ downtown):** the proven one. During the 2019 Metro platform shutdown, the Potomac Riverboat Co. Old Town↔Wharf commuter run carried **5,000 passengers/day** with an 80% state fare subsidy, and kept running after the stations reopened (virginiaplaces.org, item 14). That is the single best demand datapoint for a premium Alexandria↔Wharf commuter product anywhere in this file.
- **I-95 south (Woodbridge):** ferry studies consistently found the boat *slower* than car/VRE at conventional speeds because of wake restrictions above Wilson Bridge and the shallow Occoquan channel (2000 VDOT study; 2009 route-proving exercise). VRE Woodbridge→L'Enfant ≈ ferry time to Navy Yard; ferry needed ~$15/trip vs VRE $10 (virginiaplaces.org). A foiling vessel attacks exactly the wake-restriction penalty — but see Regulatory before believing a fast Woodbridge run.
- **Node-pair drive-time benchmarks:** not independently measured this pass — **unknown**; recommend pulling live Google/INRIX peak matrices (Old Town↔Wharf, National Harbor↔Wharf, Ft Washington↔Old Town, Woodbridge↔Navy Yard) before the microsite quotes minutes.

## Regulatory & operational findings

**Security zones (33 CFR 165.508 — Potomac & Anacostia, Washington DC; source: law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/33/165.508, ecfr.gov):**
- Zones 1–3 blanket the whole operating area: Zone 1 = Potomac shoreline-to-shoreline from Key Bridge down to a DCA↔Hains Point line (incl. Boundary Channel, Pentagon Lagoon, Tidal Basin, Roaches Run); Zone 2 = Anacostia from Pennsylvania Ave bridge down, incl. Washington Channel; Zone 3 = Potomac from that line down to the Wilson Bridge. **Critically, these are NOT permanently closed:** they are enforced "only upon issuance of a notice of enforcement" by the USCG Captain of the Port Maryland-NCR (event-based: VIP movements, July 4, State of the Union, etc.). When activated, entry requires COTP permission (tel 410-576-2693 / VHF 16) and vessels must proceed at "the minimum speed necessary."
- Fixed annual enforcement: Zone 4 (Georgetown Channel east shore, 75 yd, Long RR Bridge→Roosevelt Bridge + Tidal Basin) every July 4; Zone 5 (Boundary Channel/Pentagon Lagoon — i.e., **Columbia Island Marina's water**) on Memorial Day, Sep 11, Nov 11; Zone 6 (= Zones 1–3) on State of the Union day.
- **Operating consequence:** a commuter schedule WILL take service interruptions on presidential-movement days and marquee events; Columbia Island (Pentagon) is the most exposed node. This constrains reliability guarantees, not routine routing. Recurring event-specific zones also exist (e.g., 33 CFR 165.557 and annual FR notices).
- 33 CFR Part 334 restricted areas around naval facilities (Navy Yard, NRL, JBAB reaches) were **not verified section-by-section this pass — unknown**; check before final route geometry on the Anacostia and below Giesboro Point.

**Speed / no-wake (the historical ferry-killer):**
- Memorial Bridge → Chain Bridge: designated DC **no-wake zone** covering the entire Georgetown waterfront (Potomac River Safety Committee, potomacriversafetycommittee.org/safety-guidelines/traffic-patterns/). Georgetown legs will be displacement-speed for ~1.5 mi regardless of vessel.
- Washington Channel (the Wharf's water): strict no-wake (wharfdcmarina.com boater guidance).
- Anacostia River: **6 mph speed limit from the river entrance to Benning Rd Bridge — 4.4 miles** (harbor regulations per usharbors.com/harbor/maryland/washington-dc/). This caps the Navy Yard approach (~1 mi from Hains Point to Diamond Teague) and would cripple any fast JBAB/upriver service unless DC amends the limit. Note these are posted speed limits, not just wake rules — **a zero-wake hydrofoil does not automatically get an exemption; a DC Harbor Patrol/DDOE/USCG engagement is required. This is the #1 regulatory workstream.** (The 2000 VDOT study's exact kill line: competitive speed "is not attainable at present due to wake and safety related speed restrictions.")
- Main-stem Potomac below the 14th St bridges to Wilson Bridge and beyond: no blanket no-wake found (DC regs map shows unrestricted segments; Facebook/DC-regs map is a secondary source — verify against DCMR Title 19 before publishing speeds).

**Dock control / permitting map:**
- DC: Wharf piers private (Hoffman-Madison); Diamond Teague piers public (DC); The Yards Marina private; James Creek = NPS concession (Guest Services). New structures need USACE Baltimore District + DC (DOEE) permits — permitting-path detail unverified.
- Virginia: City of Alexandria owns/operates the City Marina and explicitly docks commercial vessels (alexandriava.gov/Marina). Everything else on the VA shore in scope = **NPS GW Memorial Parkway** (concessioners: Guest Services at Daingerfield & Columbia Island; Marine Evolutions at Belle Haven). A National Landing dock means an NPS deal.
- Maryland: National Harbor Marina private (Peterson-developed); Fort Washington Marina on NPS Piscataway Park land, Trident-managed; Bladensburg = M-NCPPC (PG Parks).

## Proposed network (verified nodes only)

**Interchange hub: Old Town Alexandria (City Marina)** — geographic center, city-owned commercial dock, proven 5,000-rider/day precedent. Secondary hub: The Wharf Transit Pier.

**Phase 1 — Launch (all nodes active water-taxi landings today; zero new infrastructure):**
- **Monument Line (trunk):** National Harbor ↔ Old Town Alexandria ↔ The Wharf ↔ Georgetown. Every stop is a current City Cruises landing.
