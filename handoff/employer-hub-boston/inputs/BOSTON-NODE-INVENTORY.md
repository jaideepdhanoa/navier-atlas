# Boston Harbor Node Inventory — Employer Water Network v2

Status: research-complete, source-verified desk pass (web, Aug 15 2026). Follows NODE-INVENTORY-SPEC.md fail-closed rules. Every node row cites at least one live source. Unknown fields say "unknown". This precedes microsite construction; no Grok geometry/BP seal has been run.

Model: 20-seat electric hydrofoils, employer seat blocks via non-binding LOIs, $650–1,200/seat-month, launch trigger 60–80 committed seats/corridor. Positioning in this market: **premium employer-sponsored tier on top of a proven public ferry system, plus marina-grade nodes the MBTA does not serve. MBTA/Massport are respected incumbents and potential partners (WETA-style), never displacement targets.**

Market context (all sourced): the MBTA ferry system carried **1,498,200 riders in 2025 (~2,700/weekday, Q1 2026)** across nine routes, has the **highest on-time performance and farebox recovery of any MBTA mode**, and in 2016 its three then-funded routes alone carried **5,070 passengers/weekday** (Wikipedia: MBTA ferry, citing APTA). A new year-round F10 inner-harbor loop (Lovejoy Wharf–Logan–Commonwealth Pier–Central Wharf) was added in 2026 (same source). This is a "add a premium tier to a proven market" story, exactly like the NEXT-CITIES-SCAN kill-risk note anticipated.

## No landing exists (do NOT promote to nodes)

- **Assembly Row (Somerville)** — no passenger dock exists; the only funded Mystic crossing here is a planned bicycle/pedestrian bridge to Everett (target ≈2029). Any Assembly Row landing is net-new Chapter 91 work. (https://mass.streetsblog.org/2025/10/22/tuesday-massdot-hosts-design-hearing-for-new-mystic-river-car-free-bridge · https://www.bostonmagazine.com/news/2021/10/22/footbridge-encore-gondola/)
- **Chelsea (public)** — Boston Harbor Now's dock report states there is "no existing or proposed ferry service" dock; the Mary O'Malley State Park landing slip is documented as decayed. Only the private Admiral's Hill marina (listed above, watchlist) exists. (https://www.bostonharbornow.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Water-Transportation-Comprehensive-Study-Dock-Report-for-Chelsea.pdf)
- **Kendall Square / Cambridge (Charles River basin)** — the Charles is impounded behind the Charles River Dam at North Station; harbor access is only via the Gridley Locks (~30,000 boat lockings/yr), each transit requiring a lock cycle, plus a MBTA commuter-rail drawbridge whose openings are restricted during peak rail hours (M–F ~6:15–9:10 AM per boater documentation). **Kendall is NOT reachable from the harbor at commuter frequency; treat the entire Charles basin (Kendall, MIT, Esplanade, CambridgeSide) as off-network.** A separate in-basin Charles electric ferry concept exists but is a proposal, not a service. (https://www.mass.gov/doc/04uses013020pdf/download · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Q51UsfLZ80 · https://www.instagram.com/reel/DbDeUzeNV64/)
- **GE Aerospace River Works, Lynn** — ~2,500-employee plant at the mouth of the Saugus River; no passenger dock documented at the plant (site is rail-serviced). Nearest real landing is Blossom Street Pier (distance roughly a mile — unverified). Reverse-commute anchor only if GE funds a shuttle link or a plant dock is built. (https://www.geaerospace.com/lynn · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Works)
- **Salem Hospital (Mass General Brigham Salem)** — major North Shore employer (1,001–5,000 employees per LinkedIn) but at 81 Highland Ave, ~2 mi inland from Salem Wharf; not walk-tier. Shuttle-dependent. (https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/patient-care/services-and-specialties/locations/salem-hospital)
- **MGH / West End** — no dedicated landing; nearest is Lovejoy Wharf at ~12–15 min walk (walk-tier boundary; distance not precisely verified). MGH (25,000+ employees) is credited to the Lovejoy node with this caveat, not its own node. (https://www.massgeneral.org/quality-and-safety/about)
- **Fort Point Channel interior (Gillette/232 A St frontage)** — no usable scheduled-service landing inside the channel identified this pass; channel bridge clearances and navigation limits not sourced this pass (unverified). Gillette is credited to Seaport docks at 12–18 min walk instead.

## Commute pain benchmarks (sourced)

- **I-93 South (Charles River → Route 3)**: 2nd-most-congested road in the US (INRIX via CBS Boston); PM rush drivers lose ~109 extra hours/yr on it; Boston ranked 4th-worst US congestion, ~$1,400/driver/yr (INRIX 2024 via country1025.com). https://www.cbsnews.com/boston/news/boston-traffic-study-2024/ · https://country1025.com/2025/01/06/boston-traffic-drops-to-fourth-worst-in-u-s-costs-drivers-1400-each-year/
- **Route 1A / Sumner Tunnel (North Shore + East Boston)**: full 8-week closures in 2023 and repeated 2024–26 closures for restoration; MassDOT itself pushed Blue Line + free ferry as the mitigation — the state has already trained this market to substitute water for the tunnel. https://www.nhpr.org/new-england-news/2023-07-06/sumner-tunnel-closure-detour-alternative-blue-line-free-ferry-logan-airport · https://nahant.org/sumner-tunnel-closure-information/
- **Route 3 / Braintree Split (South Shore)**: chronic chokepoint; regional study framing (CTPS congestion study; Braintree Split mode-shift discussion). https://www.ctps.org/data/html/studies/other/Traffic-Congestion-in-the-Boston-Region/Traffic-Congestion-in-the-Boston-Region.html

## Regulatory & operational findings

- **Inner-harbor speed**: Boston Inner Harbor is a No Wake zone beginning at the NW corner of Logan Airport — minimum-wake, ≤6 mph over ground (MA Environmental Police notice; Waterway Guide alert). Secondary sources cite harbor limits of 5 kt within 300 ft of shore / 10 kt within 1,000 ft. **Consequence: a hydrofoil is foil-borne only outside the inner harbor (President Roads / Nantasket Roads / Broad Sound); inner-harbor legs run displacement-speed like everyone else. Trip-time math must model this split.** https://www.waterwayguide.com/nav-alert/4-12151/ma-boston-harbor-no-wake-zone · https://www.facebook.com/MAEnvironmentalPolice/posts/1024373356604719
- **Mystic/Chelsea reaches**: entirely inside the no-wake zone, plus the Alford Street drawbridge (documented failures). Encore corridor is therefore a slow displacement shuttle — hydrofoil advantage ≈ zero; only worth running for network coverage/branding.
- **Dock permitting**: new or modified piers/floats require a **Chapter 91 Waterways License from MassDEP** (public-trust act covering docks, piers, wharves, marinas); plus municipal harbormasters, and Massport controls the Logan dock; DCR controls Squantum Point Park; Boston waterfront redevelopment also runs through BPDA review. https://www.mass.gov/guides/chapter-91-the-massachusetts-public-waterfront-act · https://www.mass.gov/guides/waterways-permitting-frequently-asked-questions
- **Winter/ice**: year-round operation is proven — MBTA runs F1, F2H, F4, and F10 all winter (Wikipedia route table). Harbor core does not ice-lock in normal winters (Mystic upper reaches unknown). North Shore and outer routes are the weather constraint, not ice.
- **North Shore sea state (honest)**: the Salem→Boston run exits Salem Sound past Marblehead/Nahant into open Massachusetts Bay — semi-open Atlantic. The current Salem Ferry cancels for rough seas multiple days at a time (documented Oct 2025 multi-day cancellations; city cites "rough waters from Marblehead to Boston"). A 30–45 ft foiler will face real sea-state days; the honest product answer is a published weather-cancellation policy with commuter-rail fallback (Salem/Swampscott/Beverly all have CR; Marblehead does not). https://www.facebook.com/cityofsalemma/posts/1125522089743404 · https://patch.com/massachusetts/salem/rough-seas-cancel-salem-ferry-service-3rd-straight-commute
- **Logan dock security/access**: the Water Transportation Dock is a public Massport facility explicitly serving scheduled ferries AND on-demand water taxis, with the free Route 66 shuttle to terminals — no special security regime beyond normal airport landside documented. https://www.massport.com/logan-airport/getting-to-logan/water-transportation

## Proposed network (verified nodes only)

Positioning: premium employer-sponsored tier + unserved marina-grade origins. MBTA/Massport are incumbents-slash-partners (WETA-style); every line below either serves node pairs the MBTA does not, or adds a guaranteed-seat premium layer where it does.

**Interchanges**: Long Wharf/Central Wharf (downtown), Fan Pier + Commonwealth Pier (Seaport), Hingham Hewitts Cove (South Shore park-and-sail), Salem Wharf (North Shore).

### Launch phase (existing landings, no new permits)

1. **North Shore Line** (Navier's flagship edge — MBTA does not serve it): Marblehead (Tucker's/State St, watchlist berth) → Salem Wharf → Long Wharf → Fan Pier. Foil-borne across Broad Sound, displacement inside the harbor. Target ~35–45 min Salem→Seaport (modeled, unverified). Premium catchments with weak/no rail (Marblehead). Weather-cancellation policy required.
2. **South Shore Express**: Hingham (Hewitts Cove) / Hull (Pemberton) → Rowes Wharf → Fan Pier. Premium guaranteed-seat overlay on the proven F1/F2H market — the differentiation is the Seaport leg (MBTA South Shore boats terminate downtown; Seaport workers today walk/transfer) plus seat guarantees at shoulder-peak. Partner posture with MBTA, explicitly not displacement.
3. **Quincy Line**: Marina Bay/Squantum Point → Fan Pier → Long Wharf, year-round weekday (vs. MBTA F7 seasonal). 686-slip Safe Harbor marina co-located for O&M berthing.

### Growth phase

4. **Beverly–Salem link**: extend North Shore Line to Glover Wharf Beverly (municipal marina ready today).
5. **Riverside Shuttle** (coverage/branding, displacement-speed only): Encore dock → Lovejoy Wharf → Long Wharf → Fan Pier. Honest note: entirely no-wake water + drawbridge; no hydrofoil advantage; run only if Encore/Everett employers sponsor it.
6. **Scituate/Cohasset feeders**: road-feed to Hingham interchange first (Cohasset harbor is 7-ft, tide-constrained; Scituate is an open-water run). Direct Scituate→Boston water service only if a corridor LOI block demands it.
7. **Lynn/Winthrop**: Blossom St Pier and Winthrop Landing joins as feeders if GE Aerospace or Logan-adjacent employers anchor seats; both landings are real today.
8. **Gloucester**: watchlist only — landing constraints (Solomon Jacobs commercial-use limits) and ~28 nm distance put it outside the 20–45 min sweet spot.

### Held / decision-flagged

- **Logan Airport corridor**: the landing is real, public, and already multi-operator (Massport dock, free terminal shuttle) — so Logan QUALIFIES as a node on landing-gate grounds. But Boston↔Logan is held in partner/operator decks under public-value framing in other Navier materials. **Decision for parent: include Logan stops on employer lines (e.g., North Shore Line calling at Logan) or keep Logan out of the employer microsite entirely.** This inventory lists the node; the microsite call is flagged, not made.
- **Provincetown**: excluded — not a commute.
- **Kendall/Charles basin**: excluded — locked (see No-landing section).

### Sweet-spot check (20–45 min)

Proven today at displacement speed: Charlestown 10 min; East Boston ~10 min; Hingham direct ~35–40 min; Hull ~20–25 min (MBTA schedules). Salem today ~55–60 min on the existing cat; a foiler's open-water leg savings should bring Salem/Marblehead→downtown into the low-40s (modeled, unverified — needs route-geometry pass with the no-wake boundary drawn). Quincy/Winthrop/Lynn all fit inside 45 min on current schedules.
