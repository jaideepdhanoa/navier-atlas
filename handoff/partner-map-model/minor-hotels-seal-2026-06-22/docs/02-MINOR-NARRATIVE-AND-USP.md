## The thesis (slide-2 `network_thesis`)

**Headline:** *"Your guests already arrive by boat. Navier makes that arrival the best part of the stay —
and a margin line you own."*

**Body:** Minor operates 100+ coastal and island resorts where the **last mile is water**: a Maldives villa,
a Bazaruto private island, a Phuket headland, a Palm Jumeirah crescent. Today that transfer is a diesel
speedboat or a seaplane — loud, fume-heavy, weather-fragile, and operated by someone else. Navier's
electric hydrofoil turns the transfer into a **silent, flat-water, zero-emission signature experience** that
Minor **controls end-to-end**: the boat is branded, the schedule is the resort's, and the transfer revenue
stays inside the portfolio instead of leaking to third-party launch operators.

Because Minor's network is **captive** — guests who book the resort take the resort's boat — the economics
are not a contested fight for a city's ride share. They are a **high-capture annuity** that scales with two
levers Minor already pulls: **occupancy** and **new property openings**.

## The four USP value props (the "Three C's + one")

1. **Captive revenue, not leaked.** Every guest transfer is today operated by a third party or a depreciating
   diesel fleet. Navier lets Minor **own the transfer as a branded margin line** — gateway pickups,
   inter-resort hops, and curated excursions all on Minor's own electric fleet.

2. **Calm — the experience upgrade.** Hydrofoil = no slap, no spray, no fumes, no engine roar. A foiling
   arrival to a private island is a **brochure moment**, not a transit chore. This is the differentiator
   diesel and seaplane can't match and the reason guests will *prefer* it (and pay for it).

3. **Clean — the ESG/brand line.** Zero-emission transfers retire the diesel-launch carbon and the seaplane
   noise footprint over sensitive reef and lagoon environments. Quantified CO₂ avoided per cluster feeds the
   proof slide and Minor's sustainability reporting — a concrete, sellable ESG story tied to luxury, not
   austerity.

4. **Continuity — weather & reliability.** Foils ride above chop, extending the **operable-sea-state window**
   vs. small diesel tenders and grounded seaplanes — fewer cancelled transfers, fewer stranded guests, a more
   reliable arrival promise the resort can actually guarantee.

## Narrative guardrails (don't drift)

- **Captive, not contested** language throughout — never describe Minor as competing for a city's mobility
  share. It owns its guests' transfers.
- **WIDTH is the growth story** — more keys, more openings, more clusters; never "capture maturing upward."
- **Floors are floors** — always present the grounded SOM as the honest sell; label any greenfield/WIDTH
  upside as labelled template upside, not measured.

  minimal gold accents.** Slide-2 distinct image. Territory/no-named-3rd-party cover cases (French Polynesia,
  Caribbean) stay Navier-only / no-logo — but **Minor is a named partner**, so its cover carries the **Minor
