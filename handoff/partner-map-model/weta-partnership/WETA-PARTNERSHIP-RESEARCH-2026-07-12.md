# WETA × Navier partnership research

**Date:** 12 July 2026  
**Purpose:** Source-led foundation for rebuilding the WETA × Navier deck as a long-term Bay Area passenger-and-package partnership proposal.  
**Research standard:** WETA/San Francisco Bay Ferry, California Legislature, MARAD, and Grants.gov are controlling for public claims. Navier repository records are internal planning inputs only.

## Executive conclusion

The credible proposition is a **long-term, WETA-shaped partnership** in which Navier explores a complementary small-vessel layer for the Bay: augment selected existing services, help WETA evaluate published expansion markets, test genuinely new links only after joint validation, and examine whether a customer-backed package service can safely share capacity. That is aligned with WETA's legislated regional/emergency role, its 2050 Service Vision, its use of pilots and public/private operators, and its need to expand while electrifying under financial, infrastructure, permitting, and resilience constraints.[W1][W3][W5][W6][W7][W13]

The FY26 U.S. Marine Highway Program (USMHP) can be mentioned only as an **optional, evidence-gated accelerator**. No WETA source reviewed establishes a package/freight service or customer. Navier has no cargo customer. Do not say that WETA, Navier, any vessel, or any proposed Bay service is eligible; do not imply WETA support, route-sponsor endorsement, terminal rights, match, procurement, certification, or award probability.[M1][M2][M3]

## Non-negotiable claim discipline

1. Use the legal name **San Francisco Bay Area Water Emergency Transportation Authority (WETA)** for the authority and **San Francisco Bay Ferry** for its passenger service/brand.
2. Separate four states on every corridor: **existing**, **published expansion**, **new candidate**, and **package/grant pathway**.
3. Treat WETA's 2050 figures as **plan outcomes/targets**, not current operating facts.
4. Treat all dates in REEF and project pages as planned/expected and, where WETA says so, dependent on funding and permitting.
5. Treat Navier performance, capacity, range, wake, accessibility, certification, operating cost, and emissions as unverified until a controlled technical diligence package is supplied.
6. Do not transfer internal Atlas route geometry, distances, demand language, timelines, or economics into partner-facing copy as public facts.
7. “People and packages” means passenger service first and a **customer-gated, separately validated package extension**—not a cargo launch commitment.

## 1. Mandate and emergency role

### Statutory position

- California established WETA as “a local governmental entity of regional government” with jurisdiction throughout the Bay Area.[W2]
- The governing act finds that an expanded and coordinated regional water transportation system is a matter of statewide interest for emergency response and recovery. It describes the need for preparedness, mitigation, response, and recovery rather than improvising a water system after a disaster.[W1]
- Government Code §66540.6 authorizes WETA to operate a comprehensive emergency public water transportation system and gives it authority/control over public transportation ferries in the region, subject to stated exceptions, consultations, consistency requirements, and the California Public Utilities Commission's authority.[W3]
- Section 66540.6(b) is important for partnership design: WETA determines entry, within its jurisdiction, of water transportation service or facilities that affect public lands or receive/benefit from public funds, with the stated Golden Gate exception.[W3] This is a reason to present Navier as seeking a WETA-led path, not terminal access by assertion.

### Operational emergency role

WETA's current emergency page says it coordinates emergency movement of survivors, first responders, and disaster service workers; assesses terminals, vessels, fuel, and crews; coordinates mutual aid/contracted resources; and works with regional, state, federal, port, transit, shelter, and local partners. It also increases ferry service during disruptions such as bridge or BART Transbay Tube closures.[W5]

**Implication for Navier:** emergency usefulness can be an evaluation workstream, but no Navier vessel should be counted as emergency capacity until WETA and relevant agencies accept its certification, berthing, crew, communications, fueling/charging, accessibility, interoperability, and operating concept.

## 2. WETA's 2050 Service Vision

WETA frames the vision through four priorities—the **4 Es**:

- **Enhance** the current network and service;
- **Expand** access and connections;
- **Electrify** toward a 100% zero-emission fleet and upgraded terminals/infrastructure; and
- **Emergency Response** through more vessels, seats, and network readiness.[W6]

Published vision outcomes include 20–30 minute peak headways on most routes, more all-day/evening/weekend service, 14 routes, 25% more residents and jobs connected, 38,000 tons of annual carbon emissions avoided, and a 61% increase in evacuation capacity.[W6] These are **WETA plan outcomes**, not current-state claims and not promises attributable to Navier.

The implementation language is directly relevant:

- incremental rollout based on market potential, zero-emission vessels, fare consistency, emergency readiness, terminal access, and environmental/operational standards;
- a pilot program launched in 2023 to test emerging technologies and routes;
- local-jurisdiction MOUs for shared funding, implementation, responsibilities, facilities, vessels, and terminal infrastructure;
- a possible regional network-manager role including WETA-operated services and services by other public or private operators; and
- community/stakeholder input calling for pilots and consideration of smaller vessels or private operators for lower-demand routes.[W6]

**Nuance:** “consider smaller vessels or private operators” appears in WETA's summary of engagement recommendations, not as a procurement decision or commitment to Navier.[W6]

## 3. Zero-emission fleet and REEF

WETA's **Rapid Electric Emission Free (REEF) Ferry Program** is a suite of vessel and terminal electrification projects intended to move the fleet toward zero-emission propulsion while preserving safe, fast, reliable service.[W7]

Published program elements include:

- San Francisco Emerging Neighborhood Service linking Treasure Island and Mission Bay with Downtown San Francisco;
- Downtown San Francisco terminal electrification and universal charging floats;
- procurement of three 150-passenger electric vessels expected in early 2027;
- procurement of two 400-passenger electric vessels;
- East Bay terminal and Central Bay Operations and Maintenance Facility charging infrastructure;
- staged geography: Inner Central Bay; Central Bay; Long Run Central Bay; and long runs including Vallejo, Carquinez Strait, and Redwood City; and
- a currently published milestone sequence including a 2027 150-passenger vessel delivery and Treasure Island launch, followed in 2028 by the first 400-passenger vessel, Mission Bay opening, Alameda Seaplane electrification, and first Universal Charging Float.[W7]

WETA expressly conditions future new service on **funding and permitting approvals**.[W7] Therefore, call these “published plans/milestones” rather than approved, funded, or guaranteed service.

WETA's **Sea Change** hydrogen-fuel-cell ferry is relevant as evidence that WETA tests emerging clean technology. It is not evidence that WETA has approved Navier, hydrofoils, package carriage, or any new route.[W17]

**Partnership fit:** Navier should be positioned as a potentially complementary tool for specific market/service problems—not as a replacement for REEF or WETA's procured 150- and 400-passenger vessels. Any zero-emission or low-wake claims must be documented by Navier before use.

It is not a precedent for packages, hydrofoils, terminal rights, or a Navier procurement.

## 5. Explicitly documented challenges and constraints

WETA's own materials identify the following:

1. **Changing travel patterns and financial sustainability.** The lower-fare, more equitable post-pandemic model relies more heavily on non-fare revenues; changing travel patterns, rising costs, and ambitious service goals create fiscal pressure.[W6]
2. **Construction and labor cost escalation.** Expansion and zero-emission transition raise both capital and operating challenges.[W6]
3. **Limited funds and need for additional mechanisms.** WETA lists public-private partnerships and future funding measures among options to evaluate, not committed sources.[W6]
4. **Electrification complexity.** Utility coordination, electricity costs, terminal upgrades, complex permitting, and maintaining operations are explicit implementation issues.[W6][W7]
5. **Climate and seismic resilience.** Sea-level rise, weather, earthquakes, and essential-facility standards shape terminal and network design.[W6][W10]
6. **Demand uncertainty and market fit.** WETA proposes incremental prioritization, demand forecasts, pilots, and service criteria; it does not assume every new pair supports the same vessel or service level.[W6]
7. **Multi-agency coordination.** Emergency service and new facilities require coordination with cities, ports, MTC, Caltrans, USCG, state/federal emergency agencies, and other transit operators.[W3][W5]
8. **Preserving passenger quality.** REEF stresses safe, fast, reliable service without sacrificing existing quality.[W7]

**How Navier should respond:** propose a joint validation method—market, vessel, terminal, operations, equity, emergency, environmental, and financial gates—not an unsupported promise that small boats automatically solve these constraints.

## 6. Passenger experience, access, equity, and regional connectivity

- A 2022 WETA passenger survey (963 respondents, collected July–August 2022) reported 99% satisfaction and 42% new riders since 2020.[W16] Cite the year/sample every time; do not present it as a 2026 measurement.
- SF Bay Ferry states that its vessels and facilities are accessible, with wheelchair-accessible ramps, doorways, and restrooms; it also publishes disability fare and reasonable-modification policies.[W14]
- Current fare policy includes discounts for seniors, youth, eligible low-income riders through Clipper START, and transfer discounts with other transit agencies.[W15]
- The 2050 vision prioritizes seamless regional transit connections, affordable fares, nontraditional commute hours, public awareness, equity/diversity/inclusion, and high passenger experience.[W6]

**Deck implication:** accessibility, boarding assistance, Clipper/fare integration, transfers, service span, bike handling, weather, reliability, and complaint/communications processes are design requirements. Do not claim a Navier vessel meets WETA's existing accessibility standard until verified.

## 7. Freight/package, small-vessel, pilot, and grant findings

### What WETA says

- Official sources reviewed provide clear passenger, emergency, small-vessel/pilot, public/private-operator, and alternative-service references.[W5][W6][W8][W13]
- WETA's REEF page also catalogs awarded public grants for electrification, including a $55 million EPA Clean Ports award and state/federal awards announced in 2022–2024.[W7] This establishes grant-management precedent for WETA's own zero-emission program; it does **not** imply USMHP eligibility, available match, support for Navier, or a package project.
- The reviewed WETA sources did **not** identify an existing WETA freight/package service, freight customer, package pilot, or package-specific grant strategy. This is a “not found in reviewed public sources” result, not proof that no internal work exists.
- Emergency materials refer to people—survivors, first responders, and disaster service workers—and resource coordination. They do not establish an everyday package service.[W5]

### What FY26 USMHP requires at a high level

The FY26 NOFO says an eligible project must be on a designated Marine Highway Route and involve “Marine Highway Transportation,” defined as the carriage by a documented vessel of cargo or cargo and passengers between points in the United States.[M1] It defines cargo to include freight, vehicles, and other commodities, including packages handled individually.[M1]

The NOFO lists eligible governmental and certain private applicants; private operators/owners require a current Marine Highway Route Sponsor endorsement. It encourages coalitions including shippers and logistics providers. It also imposes vessel-documentation, route, project, cost-share, procurement, and other federal requirements.[M1]

**No eligibility conclusion is made here.** Before the grant appears beyond a footnote/optional pathway, jointly verify:

1. a real package/freight customer and shipper use case;
2. commodity/package definition, volume, origin/destination, schedule, chain of custody, security, insurance, terminal handling, hazardous-material exclusions, and unit economics;
3. designated-route fit for the exact corridor and the current route sponsor;
4. eligible applicant/lead and any required endorsement;
5. vessel documentation and USCG/certification status for passenger-plus-cargo carriage;
6. eligible scope/costs, environmental readiness, procurement route, project schedule, and operations authority;
7. non-federal share source and commitment; and
8. WETA and terminal/jurisdiction approvals.

### FY26 record-control warning

- The live Grants.gov/Simpler record lists **693JF725R000010** and a closing date of **31 August 2026**.[M2]
- The PDF summary extracted from MARAD's posted FY26 NOFO displays **MARAD-USMH-693JF725R000009**.[M1]
- The live Grants.gov record also displays award fields that do not cleanly match the NOFO summary. Treat the current Grants.gov opportunity/version history and the posted PDF as controlling documents to reconcile before any application work.[M1][M2]

The deck should name the program and deadline only if needed, then state “subject to current NOFO and Grants.gov confirmation.” Do not feature an amount, match, or award range until reconciled.

### Route designation warning

MARAD publishes the M-5 West Coast route and M-580 connecting Oakland with the San Joaquin/Sacramento River system.[M4] That does **not** establish that a proposed intrabay package corridor is eligible or identify the necessary sponsor/endorsement. Confirm exact endpoints and route status directly with MARAD.

## 9. Facts versus hypotheses

### Source-backed facts usable with citations

- WETA is a regional local governmental entity with an emergency/public-water-transport role.[W1][W2][W3]
- WETA operates San Francisco Bay Ferry and publishes six regular route groups plus special-event/short-hop/pilot services.[W8]
- WETA's 2050 vision uses Enhance, Expand, Electrify, and Emergency Response and contemplates pilots, smaller vessels, and public/private operators.[W6]
- REEF includes electric vessels, terminal charging, and published future-service phases/milestones.[W7]
- Mission Bay, Berkeley, and Redwood City are official development/planning projects with different statuses.[W10][W11][W12]
- OAWS is an official small-vessel/public-private pilot precedent.[W13]
- WETA publishes accessibility, discount, transfer, and passenger-experience policies/data.[W14][W15][W16]
- FY26 USMHP requires actual cargo or cargo-plus-passenger Marine Highway Transportation and other statutory/NOFO gates.[M1]

### Navier hypotheses requiring joint validation

- A small foiling vessel can cost-effectively add frequency on a specific existing route.
- A Navier service can selectively replace a conventional trip without reducing accessibility, capacity, reliability, or emergency readiness.
- A Navier vessel can use a particular WETA or partner terminal and charging/energy system.
- A specific expansion market supports a Navier operating model.
- Packages can be co-carried without harming the passenger experience or violating certification, security, handling, insurance, or labor requirements.
- Package demand is sufficient and qualifies the exact project for USMHP.
- A Navier vessel can be credited toward emergency response capacity.
- Any stated Navier range, speed, wake, emissions, capacity, cost, or delivery date.

## 11. Principal risks and mitigations

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Cargo-first narrative without a customer | Misstates reality and weakens WETA trust | Passenger-first deck; package path behind customer/operational gates |
| Confusing WETA plans with approvals | Creates implied commitments | Exact project-status labels and source dates |
| Implied terminal rights | WETA/ports/cities control different facilities and approvals | Terminal diligence workstream; no route launch language |
| Procurement/partnership ambiguity | Public entities require lawful structures | Ask WETA to define the permissible pilot/procurement/MOU route |
| Technical overclaim | Certification, accessibility, wake, range, charging, and weather are critical | Supply auditable Navier technical evidence; independent review where appropriate |
| Displacing WETA fleet narrative | Could conflict with REEF investments and labor/service needs | Start with augmentation; replacement only after WETA-led evidence |
| Equity regression | Small-capacity/premium service could weaken access | Fare/Clipper/ADA/service-span and geographic-equity gates |
| Emergency overclaim | WETA's role is formal and multi-agency | Treat emergency support as a separately accepted operating plan |
| USMHP timing/eligibility | Deadline is near; legal and customer prerequisites may not exist | Use only if a real shipper and all gates are quickly validated; otherwise pursue later funding |
| NOFO metadata discrepancy | Wrong opportunity number/amount can invalidate work | Confirm live Grants.gov version and MARAD PDF before submission |
| Atlas contamination | Internal candidate network contains unsupported pairs/statuses | Build visuals from official WETA network first; label candidates distinctly |

## 12. Recommended diligence sequence

1. **WETA listening session:** priorities, pain points, current pilot criteria, permissible partnership path, and areas where small vessels could add public value.
2. **Corridor screen:** WETA supplies/approves data and evaluation criteria; separate augmentation, expansion, and new candidates.
3. **Navier technical disclosure:** vessel configuration, capacity, range reserve, sea-state/weather envelope, wake/noise, accessibility, USCG status, charging/fueling, crew, maintenance, cybersecurity, and emergency integration.
4. **Terminal/operations diligence:** berthing, tides, gangways, charging, layover, passenger flow, bikes, ADA, dispatch, labor, and environmental approvals.
5. **Passenger/equity plan:** fare policy, Clipper/transfers, service span, language/communications, accessibility, and rider research.
6. **Package discovery:** only with real prospective customers; define commodity, flow, operations, safety, and economics.
7. **Pilot design:** WETA-selected corridor and structure; baseline, counterfactual, KPIs, stop/go gates, incident plan, and public reporting.
8. **Funding decision:** evaluate FY26 USMHP only after customer, route, vessel, applicant, sponsor, match, scope, and schedule gates pass.
