# WETA × Navier partner deck brief

**Date:** 12 July 2026  
**Format:** 15 slides  
**Audience:** WETA leadership and relevant Bay Area public partners  
**Narrative:** shared Bay commitment → WETA mandate/challenges → Navier's differentiated fit → passenger network/corridor portfolio → people-and-packages architecture → pilot/partnership → federal funding as optional accelerator → evidence-gated ask

## Deck-wide rules

- **Partner first:** WETA's mandate, public value, planning process, and passenger standard lead. Navier is a prospective tool, not the protagonist of WETA's plan.
- **Passenger first:** everyday public transportation is the core proposition. Package movement is a separately validated extension.
- **No unsupported commitments:** no route approval, WETA commitment, terminal rights, schedule, fare, budget, match, procurement path, certification, vessel eligibility, demand, or grant eligibility.
- **Exact status labels:** show every corridor as existing service, published expansion, new candidate, or package/grant path.
- **No model economics:** omit internal fare revenue, CO2, passenger-minutes, demand, or timing outputs.
- **WETA nomenclature:** “San Francisco Bay Area Water Emergency Transportation Authority (WETA)” for the agency; “San Francisco Bay Ferry” for passenger service.
- **Visual discipline:** official WETA network/project geography is the base. Atlas geometry may be a labeled conceptual overlay only; internal route IDs never appear partner-facing.
- **Source control:** bracketed codes refer to the research file's source list.

### Claim-status legend

- **FACT** — direct official source.
- **PUBLISHED PLAN** — WETA goal, forecast, project status, or milestone; not current service/approval.
- **NAVIER PROPOSAL** — suggested partnership design, not WETA position.
- **HYPOTHESIS / VALIDATE** — requires WETA data or technical diligence.
- **CONTINGENT** — only after named prerequisites are satisfied.
- **NAVIER TO SUBSTANTIATE** — company/platform claim requiring auditable evidence.

## Slide 2 — A partnership, not a point solution

**Purpose**  
State the full proposition early: support WETA's evolving passenger network, examine selective replacement only when evidence supports it, and add a customer-gated package layer.

**Source-backed copy**

> **One partnership. Four ways to create public value.**
>
> 1. **Augment existing service** where added frequency, span, or smaller increments solve a WETA-defined problem.  
> 2. **Support published expansion** with a vessel/service option evaluated against WETA's market, terminal, equity, and operating standards.  
> 3. **Validate genuinely new corridors** through pilots—not assumptions.

> Selective trip or vessel replacement is a possible outcome of evidence—not the opening promise.

WETA's 2050 materials contemplate enhanced and expanded service, pilots, smaller vessels, and services by public or private operators.[W6]

## Slide 9 — Where augmentation may be worth testing

**Purpose**  
Show a disciplined way to identify augmentation or selective replacement opportunities on current service.

**Source-backed copy**

> **Begin with WETA's operating questions—not our favorite route.**
>
> For each existing route, jointly test:
>
> 1. Is the problem frequency, service span, capacity granularity, reliability, or cost?  
> 2. Can a smaller vessel meet the same passenger, accessibility, terminal, labor, weather, and emergency requirements?  
> 3. Does an added trip grow useful access without fragmenting fares or operations?  
> 4. If replacement is considered, does it outperform the current trip on WETA-selected public-value and operating measures?

> **Default posture: augment. Replacement is an evidence-gated decision for WETA.**

Use the six official regular route groups as the screening universe—not as six recommendations.[W8]

## Slide 12 — A WETA-shaped pilot and partnership model

**Purpose**  
Offer a respectful governance model that WETA can reshape and place into its lawful process.

**Source-backed copy**

> **Discovery before demonstration. Demonstration before deployment.**
>
> **WETA / public partners define:** public need, corridor, passenger/equity standard, terminal and emergency requirements, data/KPIs, and permissible partnership/procurement structure.

> **Jurisdictions / terminal owners contribute:** facility, access, community, permitting, and landside integration requirements.

Suggested gates:

1. problem and corridor accepted for study;
2. technical/regulatory/terminal feasibility;
3. passenger and equity plan;
4. operating and financial case;
5. pilot authorization through WETA's selected process;
6. measured decision on augment, replace, expand, or stop.
