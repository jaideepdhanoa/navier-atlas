# India market sub-proposal blocks — Tasklet-owned slice 2 (2026-06-20)

Status: **draft / source-backed / economics-pending**. These blocks are intended for later partner-specific JSON assembly. Route IDs stay `null` until sealed. Markets with exact-bind gaps are kept brief-only.

## 1. Goa — marquee tourism expansion

**Hero:** Convert Goa’s airport-to-coast tourism flows into premium electric water mobility.

**Summary:** Goa is the cleanest India tourism wedge: official Goa Tourism data shows 10.8M tourist arrivals in 2025, with 517K foreign tourists and charter flights still anchoring inbound leisure demand. The Navier pitch should frame Goa as a premium transfer layer between airports, resort clusters, beaches and marina/waterfront nodes, not as a commuter-only system.

**Why now:** Goa has two airport gateways, dense beach/hotel clusters, a high-value leisure economy and visible road-congestion pain around peak tourism periods. Premium water legs can turn a fragmented resort transfer into a differentiated arrival product.

**Local use cases:**
1. Dabolim/Mopa airport → coastal resort/beach cluster premium transfers.
2. Hotel/marina/beach-club transfers for international charter and domestic premium travelers.

**Partner fit:** Rapido and Ola can supply short-hop pickup/drop distribution; Uber India derivative can use official city rows where exact Goa localities match Atlas.

**Phases:**
- Prove: one airport/resort water-transfer package plus hotel distribution partner.
- Scale: add beach-club/marina loops and event/charter bundles.
- Mature: connect Goa into a western-coast premium tourism corridor only after sealed routes.

**Proof points:** Goa Tourism 2025 arrivals; charter-flight inbound tourism; accepted India baseline.

**Objections:** Seasonality and terminal access. Response: launch around premium tourism peaks and use partner app distribution for first/last-mile certainty.

**Status:** narrative-ready, economics-pending.

## 2. Mumbai / Navi Mumbai / Elephanta / Mandwa — marquee commuter + tourism corridor

**Hero:** Make Mumbai’s water crossings bookable, premium and integrated into city mobility.

**Summary:** Mumbai already has an official water-taxi precedent: PIB records a service connecting Mumbai and Navi Mumbai from DCT to Nerul, Belapur, Elephanta and JNPT, with Belapur enabling Bhaucha Dhakka, Mandwa, Elephanta and Karanja movements. Navier’s story is to add premium, reliable, low-emission legs over the same problem set: cross-harbour time savings and tourism access.

**Why now:** Mumbai’s road network and cross-harbour movements create high time-value demand. The public infrastructure precedent reduces market-education risk, while premium operators can serve travelers who pay for certainty, comfort and speed.

**Local use cases:**
1. Mumbai ↔ Navi Mumbai / Belapur / Nerul / JNPT terminal transfer and commuter time savings.
2. Elephanta / Mandwa / Karanja / Raigad leisure and premium weekend movement.

**Partner fit:** Ola/Rapido terminal feeders; Uber India derivative for official Mumbai/coastal Maharashtra rows; Reliance overlay only for corporate waterfront/captive use cases.

**Phases:**
- Prove: DCT/Gateway/Belapur-style premium route package.
- Scale: add Elephanta and Mandwa leisure products.
- Mature: corporate/captive and western-coast extensions after exact routes.

**Proof points:** PIB Mumbai water taxi announcement; Sagarmala/Maharashtra coastal project context.

**Objections:** Existing water taxis/ferries. Response: Navier is premium electric mobility and partner-integrated distribution, not generic ferry replacement.

**Status:** narrative-ready, economics-pending.

## 3. Kerala / Kochi / Vembanad — proven water-transit market

**Hero:** Build the premium partner layer around India’s strongest electric water-transit proof point.

**Summary:** Kochi Water Metro validates the behavior: official operator data reports over 7.0M joined passengers and project materials cite 75+ e-boats, 15 routes and 75+ km, while PPP project material describes 78 boats, 38 terminals, 10 islands and 76 route-km. Navier should not compete with the public Water Metro; it should wrap premium routes, hotel transfers and partner-distributed services around the same waterway logic.

**Why now:** Kochi’s islands, port city, backwaters and tourism economy already understand water mobility. The market has the rare combination of existing user behavior, sustainability narrative and premium leisure adjacency.

**Local use cases:**
1. Island-city commuter adjacency and terminal feeder/premium overflow routes.
2. Backwater, hotel, port and airport-linked premium tourism transfers.

**Partner fit:** Rapido/Ola for terminal feeders; Uber India derivative via exact Kerala rows; hospitality partners later via property-origin rules.

**Phases:**
- Prove: premium terminal-adjacent hotel/backwater route.
- Scale: island-city and airport/hotel packages.
- Mature: wider Kerala backwater network after route seal and economics.

**Proof points:** Kochi Water Metro ridership and network scale; PPP project statement on islands and route-km.

**Objections:** Public transit competition. Response: Navier provides premium, partner-distributed, route-specific services that complement mass transit.

**Status:** narrative-ready, economics-pending.

## 4. Andaman & Nicobar / Port Blair / inter-island — island mobility and tourism

**Hero:** Use Navier to create premium, reliable legs inside a true island-mobility market.

**Summary:** Andaman is structurally marine-first: DSS publicly lists foreshore, inter-island and mainland voyages, harbour passenger ferry service, monthly passes, daily ticketing, freight services and tourism cruises such as Barren Island and Dweep Darshan. This is not a speculative water market; the waterway is already the transport spine.

**Why now:** Island travelers and tourists already rely on scheduled vessels, but premium app-distributed water mobility could address time-sensitive hotel, airport, excursion and harbour transfers.

**Local use cases:**
1. Port Blair harbour / foreshore / inter-island premium passenger transfer.
2. Tourism excursion and hotel-connected water legs for heritage/volcano/island products.

**Partner fit:** Keep Rapido/Ola/Uber claims conservative unless local availability is sourced; use India derivative prose and marine demand proof separately.

**Phases:**
- Prove: Port Blair harbour/hotel transfer pilot.
- Scale: excursion and inter-island premium schedule overlays.
- Mature: multi-island network only after exact geometry, operating permissions and demand anchors.

**Proof points:** DSS official voyage, ticketing, monthly-pass and cruise product infrastructure.

**Objections:** Operating complexity and permitting. Response: keep as future proposal-ready only after local partner and route evidence clear.

**Status:** narrative-ready but local partner evidence/economics pending.

## 8. Kolkata-Haldia / Hooghly River / West Bengal waterways — riverine commuter corridor

**Hero:** Bring premium electric mobility to one of India’s clearest inland-waterway modernization corridors.

**Summary:** West Bengal has a source-backed riverine transport thesis: the World Bank approved $105M for inland water transport infrastructure in Kolkata/West Bengal. Uber’s official roster includes Haldia, Haora/Howrah and many West Bengal rows, supporting an India derivative once exact Atlas aliases are reconciled.

**Why now:** Kolkata’s ferry and riverine commuter network is a real transport layer, not a hypothetical. Haldia adds port/logistics/business movement; Howrah/Kolkata adds commuter density.

**Local use cases:**
1. Kolkata/Howrah/Hooghly ferry-terminal commuter and premium overflow routes.
2. Kolkata-Haldia port/logistics/business transfer and executive river movement.

**Partner fit:** Uber India derivative can use city-supported rows after exact extraction; Ola/Rapido support feeder framing.

**Phases:**
- Prove: Kolkata/Howrah terminal-adjacent premium route narrative.
- Scale: Haldia and logistics/business route candidates.
- Mature: broader Hooghly corridor only after geometry and vessel range gating.

**Proof points:** World Bank waterway infrastructure project; Uber official West Bengal city rows.

**Objections:** Public ferry modernization may be slow/institutional. Response: start with premium or partner-distributed corridors that complement, not replace, public service.

**Status:** narrative-ready after exact terminal/source cleanup; economics pending.
