# Cannes
_Region: Europe_
_Last updated: 2026-06-23_

## Snapshot
Cannes is the Riviera's **event-surge engine** — a town whose demand is shaped by a calendar of globally televised conventions that turn the bay into a wall of arrivals. The **Festival de Cannes** (May) is the world's most famous film festival; **MIPIM** and **MIPCOM** fill the **Palais des Festivals** with business travel; and the **Cannes Yachting Festival** (September) is **Europe's leading in-water boat show — 700+ boats and ~680 exhibitors across its two harbours**. Those two harbours — the **Vieux Port** and **Port Pierre Canto** — give Cannes a distinct two-basin pier architecture. Just offshore, the **Îles de Lérins** (Île Sainte-Marguerite ~1.5 nm out) are a captive boat-only day-trip market served today by diesel excursion craft. Cannes' distinct counterparty (a dominant municipal events convener) and its surge-and-island demand make it operationally different from the gateway at Nice or the yacht-harbour at Antibes — a textbook split-trigger node.

## Demand spine
- **Festival de Cannes:** the world's most famous film festival (May) — global media + HNW arrival surge; harbour fills with charter yachts
- **Cannes Yachting Festival:** **Europe's leading in-water boat show — 700+ boats, ~680 exhibitors** (September) across Vieux Port + Port Canto — peak yacht-transfer demand
- **MIPIM / MIPCOM:** Palais des Festivals business conventions — non-summer corporate arrival surges
- **Îles de Lérins:** Île Sainte-Marguerite ~1.5 nm offshore — a captive, boat-only day-trip market served today by diesel excursion craft
- **Two-basin harbour:** Vieux Port + Port Pierre Canto give distinct berthing capacity and concession structure

## Regulatory
French maritime authority (Préfecture maritime de la Méditerranée; Affaires maritimes); the **Ville de Cannes / SEMEC** (events + port operator) is the dominant convener and a single municipal counterparty for both harbours and the event calendar. Event weeks are simultaneously the highest-visibility demo windows and the tightest marine-access windows — plan dual-use. The Lérins islands sit within a protected coastal zone — low-wake all-electric foiling aligns with the conservation framing.
